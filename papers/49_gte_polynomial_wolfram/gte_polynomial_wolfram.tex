\begin{abstract}
Stephen Wolfram's Physics Project identifies the universe with a specific
hypergraph rewriting rule but provides no principled derivation of which rule
nature selects from an astronomically large rule space. We provide the missing
selection principle: the Minimum Description Length (MDL) criterion, combined
with the Perfect Self-Containment (PSC) orbit constraint, uniquely selects
a $k{=}7$, $r{=}1$ string substitution rule with polynomial form
$p(L,C,R) = C+R-CR-LCR \pmod{7}$ from $7^{343} \approx 10^{290}$ candidates
(Theorem T96-02, machine-certified in Lean~4, zero \texttt{sorry}).
We present the first systematic study of this polynomial as a standalone
dynamical system (Object~0 in the GTE derivation tower), distinct from the
previously studied $\fmdl{}$ lookup table (Object~1 / CMCA); the
Schwartz--Zippel theorem proves $\fmdl{}$ is not a polynomial of
degree~${\leq}3$ over~$\GF{7}$ (\CatA{}), establishing that the two objects
differ categorically, not merely quantitatively. Five algebraic results are
established: Rule~110 arises as the binary restriction of $p$ (CatAL); the
invariant subset lattice of $p$ over $\GF{7}$ contains exactly three non-empty
elements, $\{0\}$, $\{0,1\}$, and $\ZS$ --- so Rule~110 is the \emph{unique}
maximal proper invariant sub-CA (CatAL, zero sorry, exhaustive
\texttt{native\_decide}); $p$ is the unique degree-${\leq}3$ polynomial over
$\GF{7}$ reproducing Rule~110 on binary inputs (CatA); the uniqueness of
$\{0,1\}$ as binary floor is explained by $N_{\mathrm{fam}} = 5$ being a
quadratic non-residue mod~7 --- the Standard Model family count is the
algebraic obstruction to any intermediate invariant sublattice (CatAL); and
$\fmdl{}$ is provably non-polynomial (Schwartz--Zippel, \CatA{}). The
polynomial's local WolframModel update structure produces a 1023-event fractal
binary tree causal graph at 10 generations (\CatAL{}, \texttt{gte\_rulegte\_ten\_generations});
the binary branching arises from the topological degeneracy of $\mathrm{GEN}_2$
and $\mathrm{GEN}_3$ (\CatAL{}, \texttt{gen2\_gen3\_topology\_degenerate}).
This causal tree is structurally equivalent to the Lindenmayer system
$F\!\to\!F[{+}F][{-}F]$ modelling \emph{Daucus carota} compound umbels
(Apiaceae), with matching Horton ratio $r_B = 2$ (\CatAL{}) and Strahler number~10.
A three-tape extension via the Dimensional Protocol Principle yields a connected
3+1D causal bulk with $(\NTAPES{-}1)/(\NTAPES{+}2) = 40\%$ cross-tape
(gravitational) edge fraction for $\NTAPES = 3$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Wolfram's Physics Project~\cite{Wolfram2020Tech,Wolfram2002} proposes that
the fundamental laws of physics are encoded in a specific hyperedge rewriting
rule applied iteratively to a spatial hypergraph. This framework connects
computational universality, causal invariance, and the emergence of Lorentz
symmetry in a compelling unified picture. A critical gap, however, remains
open: Wolfram's programme has no derivation of \emph{which} rule nature
selects. The search is empirical --- running millions of rules, looking for
behavioral richness --- across a rule space of size $k^{k^3}$. For $k = 7$
alone this is $7^{343} \approx 10^{290}$ candidates, far beyond any exhaustive
search.

The GTE (Generative Triple Evolution) / UGP programme fills this gap with a
theorem. Theorem T96-02 (\CatAL{}, zero sorry, machine-certified in Lean~4)
proves that the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, combined with the
Perfect Self-Containment (PSC) orbit constraint --- the requirement that the
system's evolution is fully self-referential: all information needed to specify
the dynamics is encoded within the orbit itself, with no external observer or
undetermined boundary condition~\cite{SpivackGTEMobius,
SpivackCompUniversality} --- uniquely selects
$p(L,C,R) = C+R-CR-LCR \pmod{7}$ from the entire Wolfram rule space. The
selection chain is: PSC forces $k = 7$ (no three-step kink orbit exists for
$k \in \{2,3,5\}$); MDL then selects $p$ as the unique polynomial-representable
rule within the $k = 7$ class. The 19-bit polynomial description achieves a
944-bit savings over a full $k = 7$ lookup table. This is not a conjecture
but a machine-certified theorem~\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialUFT}.

This paper presents the first systematic study of $p(L,C,R) \pmod{7}$ as a
standalone dynamical system. Prior GTE papers~\cite{SpivackCMCA,
SpivackThreeTapeCMCA} studied the CMCA ($\fmdl{}$, Object~1), whose
Rule~110 sublayer and 10 SM-orbit entries have been used to derive special
relativity, gravity, quantum mechanics, and particle masses. Here we study
Object~0: the raw polynomial $p$, run on all 343 inputs over $\GF{7}$.
Objects~0 and~1 share only their binary sublayer (Rule~110); they are
otherwise algebraically distinct --- $\fmdl{}$ is provably not a polynomial
over $\GF{7}$ by the Schwartz--Zippel theorem~\cite{Schwartz1980,Zippel1979},
while $p$ is the unique degree-${\leq}3$ polynomial satisfying the MDL and PSC
criteria.

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:objects} introduces the
four-object GTE derivation tower. Section~\ref{sec:mdl} states T96-02 and the
MDL table. Section~\ref{sec:algebra} proves four algebraic theorems about $p$.
Section~\ref{sec:wolfram} encodes $p$ within Wolfram's formalism,
including the causal graph and a formal botanical correspondence between
its fractal binary tree structure and Apiaceae compound umbel L-system
models~(§\ref{subsec:botanical}).
Sections~\ref{sec:dynamics}--\ref{sec:sm} present the first spacetime diagrams,
behavioral classification, and SM generation orbit of Object~0.
Section~\ref{sec:dpp} derives the 3+1D bulk causal graph.
Section~\ref{sec:interp} clarifies what Object~0 is and is not physically.
Section~\ref{sec:open} lists open questions. Appendices provide Lean
certification tables and reproducibility instructions.

\section{The GTE Derivation Tower}

The GTE framework produces four distinct mathematical objects from the
PSC~+~MDL derivation chain:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Object~0 --- $p(L,C,R) \pmod{7}$, the algebraic source.}
The polynomial $p(L,C,R) = C+R-CR-LCR$ over $\GF{7}$ is a Wolfram $k{=}7$,
$r{=}1$ string substitution rule by definition. Applied to a tape of $\ZS$
symbols with periodic boundaries, it produces dense chaotic evolution on
generic inputs (300 of 343 values nonzero, Class~3 on $\ZS$) while supporting
an exact binary sublayer (Class~4 on $\{0,1\}^L$ initial conditions). This
is the object studied for the first time in this paper.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Object~1 --- $\fmdl{}$, the Level~1 algebraic certificate
(CMCA).}
The CMCA lookup table has 18 explicitly specified entries: 8 binary entries
that reproduce Rule~110 exactly, plus 10 SM-orbit entries uniquely forced by
PSC orbit consistency ($\mathrm{GEN}1 \to \mathrm{GEN}2 \to \mathrm{GEN}3 \to
\mathrm{VAC}$, all Lean-certified). Of these 18, exactly 14 output a nonzero
value; the four zero-output entries among the 18 are the orbit entry
$\fmdl{}(2,2,1) = 0$ (which encodes $\mathrm{GEN2}[3] = 0$ in the
$\mathrm{GEN}1 \to \mathrm{GEN}2$ step) and the three binary Rule~110
entries $\fmdl{}(0,0,0) = \fmdl{}(1,0,0) = \fmdl{}(1,1,1) = 0$.
The five $\mathrm{GEN}3 \to \mathrm{VAC}$ transition neighborhoods are
not listed explicitly; they all evaluate to zero via the MDL-minimal
default completion and form part of the remaining 325 of 343 inputs
that map to zero. This sparse table (4.1\% nonzero) was studied
in prior papers~\cite{SpivackCMCA,SpivackThreeTapeCMCA} to derive SR, GR,
QM, and particle masses.

A critical and non-trivial distinction: $\fmdl{}$ and $p$ \emph{agree on
binary inputs} (Rule~110, 8 triples), but they disagree at the 10 SM-orbit
neighborhood triples --- $\fmdl{}$'s outputs at those positions are
\emph{specified} values encoding the PSC orbit, not polynomial evaluations.
This has been machine-verified: \lean{p\_poly\_agrees\_fmdl\_on\_binary} proves
agreement on $\{0,1\}^3$; the theorem \lean{p\_poly\_agrees\_fmdl\_at\_orbit}
is refuted by \texttt{decide} (counterexample: $(L,C,R) = (1,1,5)$, where
$\fmdl{} = 2$ but $p = 3$). Objects~0 and~1 are bridges joined at Rule~110
and divergent everywhere else.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Object~2 --- $\PHIMDL{}$, the physical substrate.}
The continuum limit of $\fmdl{}$ is a $\ZS$-symmetric Klein-Gordon scalar field
with potential $V(\Phi) = (m^2/49)(1-\cos 7\Phi)$. Physical particles are
BPS topological kink solitons of $\PHIMDL{}$ --- stable, energy-minimising
field configurations that interpolate between adjacent $\ZS$-degenerate
vacua~\cite{SpivackPhiMDLField} --- spanning ${\sim}10^{13}$--$10^{15}$
cells at the Compton scale. Special relativity, general relativity, quantum
mechanics, and the full SM spectrum are derived at this
level~\cite{SpivackPhiMDLField,SpivackCompleteness,SpivackThreeTapeCMCA,
SpivackGTECosmology}. Object~2 is not studied directly in this paper.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Object~T --- $T$, the arithmetic cascade.}
The GTE arithmetic map $T: \mathbb{Z}^3 \times \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z}^3$
governs the integer triple cascade that selects which generation triples exist.
At $n = 10$ steps it produces the canonical orbit
$(b_1, b_2, b_3) = (73, 42, 275)$ from the seed $(a, b, c) = (1, 73, 823)$
via alternating odd/even arithmetic steps defined by integer division and
remainder (Definition~2.5 of~\cite{SpivackGTEMobius}).
Object~T is primitive recursive, machine-certified
(\lean{gte\_update\_map\_nat\_computable},
\lean{UgpLean/Universality/GTEComputability.lean}, \CatAL{}).

Object~T is provably non-polynomial over $\GF{7}$ (\CatAL{}, zero sorry).
Counterexample: taking $b = 73$, the inputs $(b, c) = (73, 144)$ and
$(73, 151)$ satisfy $c \equiv 4 \pmod{7}$ in both cases, but the output
$b'$ satisfies $b' \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$ for $c = 144$ and
$b' \equiv 3 \pmod{7}$ for $c = 151$.
Two inputs with identical $(b \bmod 7,\, c \bmod 7)$ produce different
$b' \bmod 7$ outputs; a polynomial map over $\GF{7}$ cannot exhibit this
behavior.
Machine-certified (\CatAL{}, zero sorry):
\lean{T\_b\_output\_not\_determined\_by\_mod7},
\lean{T\_computable\_not\_polynomial\_GF7}
(\lean{UgpLean/Polynomial/PolyExplorations.lean}).

Objects~0 and~T are \emph{co-selected siblings}: both are independently
derived from MDL~+~PSC --- $p$ within the space of polynomials over $\GF{7}$,
$T$ within the space of arithmetic integer cascades --- with no algebraic
derivability between them. That all four objects are pairwise distinct is
machine-certified (\CatAL{}, zero sorry):
\lean{four\_object\_GTE\_pairwise\_distinct}
(\lean{UgpLean/Polynomial/PolyExplorations.lean}).
Object~T's output seeds $(b_1, b_2, b_3) = (73, 42, 275)$
are the generation-level inputs that Object~1 ($\fmdl{}$) evolves on the 5-cell ring.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{What is not a separate object.}
Rule~110 is the $\{0,1\}$-restriction of $p$ (Lean-certified, Theorem~\ref{thm:r110}
below); it is Object~0 restricted to binary inputs, not a fifth object.
The 19-bit expression $p(L,C,R) = C+R-CR-LCR$ is simply $p$ described compactly;
its description length is the MDL-minimal encoding of the $k{=}7$ rule table.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Algebraic distinction.}
Object~0 is provably a degree-3 polynomial over $\GF{7}$ by construction.
Object~1 is provably \emph{not} a polynomial of degree ${\leq}3$ over $\GF{7}$:
the Schwartz--Zippel theorem~\cite{Schwartz1980,Zippel1979} bounds the number
of zeros of any degree-${\leq}3$ polynomial in 3 variables over $\GF{7}$ at
$3 \cdot 7^2 = 147$, but $\fmdl{}$
has 329 zeros ($= 343 - 14$, since only 14 of 343 inputs produce nonzero output).
Therefore Objects~0 and~1 are categorically
distinct --- not merely quantitatively different (87.5\% vs.\ 4.1\% nonzero)
but different in kind: polynomial vs.\ provably non-polynomial (\CatA{}).

\subsection{Theorem T96-02 (\CatAL{})}

\begin{theorem}[T96-02, MDL Selection --- \CatAL{}]
\label{thm:t9602}
Among all $k$-symbol, $r{=}1$ Wolfram string substitution rules, the unique
rule satisfying both (i) PSC orbit consistency --- admitting a three-step kink
orbit $\mathrm{GEN}1 \to \mathrm{GEN}2 \to \mathrm{GEN}3 \to \mathrm{VAC}$
on a 5-cell periodic ring --- and (ii) minimum description length (polynomial
representability of degree ${\leq}3$ over $\GF{k}$) is $k = 7$,
\[
  p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR \pmod{7}.
\]
The polynomial description requires 19~bits; the explicit $k = 7$ lookup
table requires 963~bits. No other $k$-symbol rule satisfies both criteria.
\end{theorem}

Machine-certified in Lean~4 (zero sorry): \lean{mdl\_ca\_rule\_coding\_closed},
module \lean{UgpLean/Universality/MDLDerivabilityCriterion.lean}.

The proof proceeds by the following selection chain:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \emph{PSC forces $k = 7$.} The canonical PSC orbit
    $\mathrm{GEN}1 \to \mathrm{GEN}2 \to \mathrm{GEN}3 \to \mathrm{VAC}$
    uses six distinct non-vacuum values from $\{1,2,3,5,6\}$ on a five-cell
    ring. Concretely: $\mathrm{GEN}1 = [1,5,2,2,1]$,
    $\mathrm{GEN}2 = [2,5,2,0,2]$, $\mathrm{GEN}3 = [5,6,5,3,5]$. The value
    $6$ appears in $\mathrm{GEN}3$ --- it must be an element of the field. For
    $k = 2$: only $\{0,1\}$ available, orbit cannot close in three steps
    with three distinct non-trivial states. For $k = 5$: the
    required value~$6 \notin \mathrm{GF}(5)$, and exhaustive machine search
    (\lean{z5\_fmdl\_no\_psc\_kink\_orbits},
    \lean{UgpLean/Universality/MDLDerivabilityCriterion.lean}, zero sorry)
    confirms no PSC-admissible
    kink orbit exists. For $k = 7$: all required orbit values
    $\{0,1,2,3,5,6\} \subset \ZS$; the orbit closes exactly. $k = 7$ is the
    minimal prime field whose element set contains the full PSC orbit.
  \item \emph{MDL selects $p$ within $k = 7$.} Among all $7^{343} \approx
    10^{290}$ rules in the $k = 7$ class, the unique polynomial rule
    (description length 19~bits) consistent with the PSC orbit is $p(L,C,R) =
    C+R-CR-LCR$. The 19-bit polynomial encoding achieves a 944-bit saving
    over the explicit 963-bit lookup table. All other $k = 7$ rules either
    require the full explicit table or are PSC-inconsistent.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Wolfram (Empirical) vs.\ GTE (Derived)}

\subsection{Theorem 4.7 --- Walsh Nonlinearity Universal Formula (\CatAD{})}

\begin{theorem}[Walsh NL Universal Formula --- \CatAD{}]
\label{thm:walshNL}

nonlinearity satisfies
\[
  \max_{\mathbf{a},b}\bigl|\widehat{p}(\mathbf{a},b)\bigr| = q(2q-1),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{NL}(p) = 1 - \frac{2q-1}{q^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Walsh transform is $\widehat{p}(\mathbf{a},b) = \sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\GF{q}^3}
\omega^{p(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}-b}$, $\omega = e^{2\pi i/q}$.
Two steps suffice.

\emph{Step~1 (ring identity).} At $\mathbf{a} = (0,1,1)$,
\[
  p(L,C,R) - \mathbf{a}\cdot(L,C,R) = C+R-CR-LCR - C - R = -CR(1+L).
\]

\emph{Step~2 (character sum).} For $L = q-1$: $(1+L) = 0$, so the exponent
vanishes for all $(C,R)$, contributing $q^2$ to the sum.
For each of the remaining $q-1$ values of $L$ (where $1+L \ne 0$), character
orthogonality gives $\sum_{C,R}\omega^{-k\,CR} = q$ (the $C = 0$ term contributes $q$;
each $C \ne 0$ gives $\sum_R \omega^{-kCR} = 0$).
Hence $|\widehat{p}((0,1,1),b)| = |q^2 + (q-1)q| = q(2q-1)$ for all $b$.

For all other $\mathbf{a}$: Cases where $a_2 = 1 \wedge a_3 = 1$ (with $a_1 \ne 0$)
give $|\widehat{p}| \le q(q-1)$; mixed cases give $|\widehat{p}| \le q(q-1)$; the
remaining cases involve Kloosterman sums bounded by the Weil bound $2q\sqrt{q} < q(2q-1)$
for all $q \ge 5$.
Therefore $\max|\widehat{p}| = q(2q-1)$. $\square$
\end{proof}

For comparison, the AES S-box over $\GF{2^8}$ has $\mathrm{NL} \approx 0.437$
(normalised to $[0,1]$).
The nonlinearity of $p$ is not the result of optimisation; it is determined by the
Higgs branch capacity $c_H = 13$ via $\mathrm{NL}(p) = 1 - c_H/7^2$.
This gives $p$ a provable, algebraically certified nonlinearity --- a property no
heuristically designed S-box possesses.

\begin{openprob}[Compton-Scale BPS Kink Injection]
All simulations in this paper use single-cell injections on tapes of $L \leq
10{,}000$ cells. A physical BPS kink spans ${\sim}10^{13}$--$10^{15}$ cells.
To observe genuine particle-like behavior, a tape with $L \sim 10^6$ cells
must be initialized with a BPS kink-like pattern (smooth winding profile over
${\sim}1000$ cells). The Taichi infrastructure supports this scale.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[Formal Gorard Continuum Limit (OQ-QG-1)]
The Gorard chain is substantially established: the CMCA vacuum is Ricci-flat
(\lean{three\_tape\_gorard\_vacuum\_ricci\_flat}, \CatAL); the normalisation
constant is $C_{\rm Gorard} = 3/32 = N_{\rm spatial}/(2D^2)$ (\CatAL); the
discrete-smooth bridge $\kappa_{\rm Ollivier} = C_{\rm Gorard} \cdot R \cdot
\varepsilon^2$ is certified (\CatAD); and the PMDL Poisson equation is
equivalent to the Newtonian EFE (\CatAD,
\cite{SpivackCompleteness,SpivackQGR,SpivackThreeTapeCMCA}). What remains open
is the
\emph{formal} Gromov--Hausdorff convergence of the Gorard chain to smooth
Lorentzian spacetime~\cite{SpivackQGR} -- requiring a Wasserstein-$1$
library in Lean~4 that does not yet exist. Analytic Planck-length
identification also remains open.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[PSC-Filtered Holographic Mode Count (OQ-CC-QUANTUM, \CatD{})]
Paper~\cite{SpivackGTECosmology} derives $\Omega_\Lambda$ via two structurally
independent routes bracketing Planck~2018:
$\Omega_\Lambda^{\rm PSC} = \ln(2000/3)/(3\pi) = 0.6899$ and
$\Omega_\Lambda^{\rm holo} = 3\pi/14 = 0.6732$, a $2.42\%$ spread
irreducible by Baker--W\"ustholz. The conceptual hypothesis that PSC selection
creates a preference for the orbit-count endpoint has been explored; the naive
algebraic form is ruled out by Baker--W\"ustholz applied to the ratio
$14 \cdot \ln(2000/3)/(9\pi^2)$. A non-algebraic mechanism remains open.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[WolframModel 3D Spatial Hypergraph]
The three-tape DPP system encoded as three independent WolframModel rewriting
rules correctly shows three independent causal trees (DPP coupling is
synchronic, not a local hyperedge overlap in SetReplace v0.3.196 semantics).
A true 3D WolframModel representation would require encoding each position
$(x,y,z)$ as a 3-tuple hyperedge and expressing cross-tape coupling as a
multi-body rewriting rule. This requires SetReplace ${\geq}$~0.7 or custom
Wolfram engine code.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[Period-$475$ Group Theory]
\label{op:period475}
The isolated 5-cell $\ZS$ ring under $p$ has a unique period-$475$ attractor,
where $475 = N_{\rm fam}^2 \times 19 = 5^2 \times 19$.
The factor $5^2$ likely arises from the $\mathbb{Z}_7[\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}]$-module
structure: the cyclic shift $\sigma$ of order~5 commutes with $p$, contributing
one factor of~5; a second factor of~5 arises from the splitting of $x^5 - 1$
over $\GF{7^4}$ (since $\mathrm{ord}_5(7) = 4$). The factor~$19$ is unexplained:
$19 \equiv 5 \pmod{7} = N_{\rm fam}$ and $19$ equals the MDL description length
of $p$ in bits, but whether either observation is structural requires a full
group-theoretic derivation of the orbit period in $\mathbb{Z}_7[\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}]$.

\noindent\textit{Partial resolution (\CatB{}).} The group structure is now better
understood: the 475-cycle is unique (exhaustively verified); the cyclic group
$\mathbb{Z}_{475} \cong \mathbb{Z}_{25} \times \mathbb{Z}_{19}$ decomposes as a
product of shift and nonlinear components; the shift $\sigma$ acts only within
$\mathbb{Z}_{25}$ (trivially on $\mathbb{Z}_{19}$), confirming the $5^2$ factor.
The factor~$19$ arises because $19 \mid 7^3 - 1 = 342$ and $\mathrm{ord}_{19}(7) = 3$,
identifying the $19$-cycle as an $\GF{7^3}$ resonance in the nonlinear part of
the dynamics; the linearised period is $240 \ne 475$, confirming that the
$19$-factor is intrinsically nonlinear. A complete algebraic derivation
of the $19$-factor and a Lean certification (target \texttt{LT-087-P475-07})
remain open.
\end{openprob}
