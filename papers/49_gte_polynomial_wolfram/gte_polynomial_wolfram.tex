\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Wolfram's Physics Project~\cite{Wolfram2020Tech,Wolfram2002} proposes that
the fundamental laws of physics are encoded in a specific hyperedge rewriting
rule applied iteratively to a spatial hypergraph. This framework connects
computational universality, causal invariance, and the emergence of Lorentz
symmetry in a compelling unified picture. A critical gap, however, remains
open: Wolfram's programme has no derivation of \emph{which} rule nature
selects. The search is empirical --- running millions of rules, looking for
behavioral richness --- across a rule space of size $k^{k^3}$. For $k = 7$
alone this is $7^{343} \approx 10^{290}$ candidates, far beyond any exhaustive
search.

The GTE (Generative Triple Evolution) / UGP programme fills this gap with a
theorem. Theorem T96-02
proves that the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, combined with the
Perfect Self-Containment (PSC) orbit constraint --- the requirement that the
system's evolution is fully self-referential: all information needed to specify
the dynamics is encoded within the orbit itself, with no external observer or
undetermined boundary condition~\cite{SpivackGTEMobius,
SpivackCompUniversality} --- uniquely selects
$p(L,C,R) = C+R-CR-LCR \pmod{7}$ from the entire Wolfram rule space. The
selection chain is: PSC forces $k = 7$ (no three-step kink orbit exists for
$k \in \{2,3,5\}$); MDL then selects $p$ as the unique polynomial-representable
rule within the $k = 7$ class. The 19-bit polynomial description achieves a
944-bit savings over a full $k = 7$ lookup table. Every link of this chain
is machine-certified in Lean~4 (zero sorry): the cross-family coding step as
a Lean coding inequality, and the within-$k{=}7$ uniqueness by the
Direct-Interpolation Lift and sparsity-floor theorems established in this
paper (§\ref{subsec:lift}); §\ref{sec:mdl} states the precise certified
content of each link~\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialUFT}.

This paper presents the first systematic study of $p(L,C,R) \pmod{7}$ as a
standalone dynamical system. Prior GTE papers~\cite{SpivackCMCA,
SpivackThreeTapeCMCA} studied the CMCA ($\fmdl{}$, Object~1), whose
Rule~110 sublayer and 10 SM-orbit entries have been used to derive special
relativity, gravity, quantum mechanics, and particle masses. Here we study
Object~0: the raw polynomial $p$, run on all 343 inputs over $\GF{7}$.
Objects~0 and~1 share only their binary sublayer (Rule~110); they are
otherwise algebraically distinct --- $\fmdl{}$ is provably not a polynomial
over $\GF{7}$ by the Schwartz--Zippel theorem~\cite{Schwartz1980,Zippel1979},
while $p$ is the unique degree-${\leq}3$ polynomial satisfying the MDL and PSC
criteria.

\subsection{Walsh Nonlinearity Universal Formula (\CatAD{})}
\label{subsec:walsh-nl}

\begin{theorem}[Walsh NL Universal Formula --- \CatAD{}]
\label{thm:walshNL}
For $p(L,C,R) = C+R-CR-LCR$ over $\GF{q}$ (any prime $q$), the Walsh--Hadamard
nonlinearity satisfies
\[
  \max_{\mathbf{a},b}\bigl|\widehat{p}(\mathbf{a},b)\bigr| = q(2q-1),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{NL}(p) = 1 - \frac{2q-1}{q^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Walsh transform is $\widehat{p}(\mathbf{a},b) = \sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\GF{q}^3}
\omega^{p(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{x}-b}$, $\omega = e^{2\pi i/q}$.
Two steps suffice.

\emph{Step~1 (ring identity).} At $\mathbf{a} = (0,1,1)$,
\[
  p(L,C,R) - \mathbf{a}\cdot(L,C,R) = C+R-CR-LCR - C - R = -CR(1+L).
\]

\emph{Step~2 (character sum).} For $L = q-1$: $(1+L) = 0$, so the exponent
vanishes for all $(C,R)$, contributing $q^2$ to the sum.
For each of the remaining $q-1$ values of $L$ (where $1+L \ne 0$), character
orthogonality gives $\sum_{C,R}\omega^{-k\,CR} = q$ (the $C = 0$ term contributes $q$;
each $C \ne 0$ gives $\sum_R \omega^{-kCR} = 0$).
Hence $|\widehat{p}((0,1,1),b)| = |q^2 + (q-1)q| = q(2q-1)$ for all $b$.

For all other $\mathbf{a}$: Cases where $a_2 = 1 \wedge a_3 = 1$ (with $a_1 \ne 0$)
give $|\widehat{p}| \le q(q-1)$; mixed cases give $|\widehat{p}| \le q(q-1)$; the
remaining cases involve Kloosterman sums bounded by the Weil bound $2q\sqrt{q} < q(2q-1)$
for all $q \ge 5$.
Therefore $\max|\widehat{p}| = q(2q-1)$. $\square$
\end{proof}

reproducible from the published scripts).
