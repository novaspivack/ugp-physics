\begin{abstract}
We apply 't~Hooft's cellular automaton interpretation of quantum mechanics
to the GTE cellular automaton $f_{\rm MDL}$ on $\mathbb{Z}_7^5$,
the 16,807-state visible-sector ring encoding the Standard Model generation structure.

The complete orbit decomposition of $f_{\rm MDL}$ (\CatA) reveals a single cycle:
the vacuum fixed point $(0,0,0,0,0)$ with $E=0$.
The physical Hilbert space is one-dimensional; $f_{\rm MDL}$ belongs to 't~Hooft's
information-loss regime.
SM generation states are transients with tail lengths
$\ell({\rm gen}_1)=3 > \ell({\rm gen}_2)=2 > \ell({\rm gen}_3)=1$,
matching the empirical stability hierarchy (\CatA).
A direct eigenvalue-to-mass correspondence is falsified on every formulation tested (\CatA).

The \textbf{Two-Role Structural Principle} (\CatAD) separates roles:
the cogwheel construction provides Hilbert space structure, unitarity, and the Born rule
(\CatAL, Lean-certified); the GTE $N_{\rm eff}$ cascade provides the mass spectrum.

The seven $\ZS$ winding-equivalence classes of $\mathbb{Z}_7^5$
(\CatAL, \lean{gauge\_arithmetic\_identification}) are identified as
't~Hooft's information-equivalence gauge classes (\CatAD):
the five SM winding sectors $\{0,2,3,4,6\}$ yield $U(1)_{\rm EM}$, $SU(2)_L$,
and $SU(3)_c$; sector $W=1$ predicts the SU(5) $Y$-leptoquark;
the $X$-leptoquark ($Q=+4/3$) cohabits $W=4$.

The AFCA causal graph is update-schedule-independent (Lean-certified);
discrete Minkowski causal cones are embedded with coordinate surjection certified.
Full Minkowski bijection remains open (\CatD).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Whether quantum mechanics is fundamental or emergent from an
underlying deterministic substrate is one of the deepest open
questions in theoretical physics.
't~Hooft's cellular automaton interpretation of quantum
mechanics~\cite{tHooft2016CA} provides a rigorous framework in which
quantum states are superpositions of ontological basis states
(``beables''), the Hamiltonian is defined by the periods of deterministic
orbits, and the Born rule is a consequence of information loss at the
coarse-graining scale.
The framework accommodates both fully reversible automata
(Chapter~2 of~\cite{tHooft2016CA}) and irreversible automata with
information loss (Chapter~7), and shows that the two regimes lead to
qualitatively different quantum structures.

The UGP Physics programme, the quantitative branch of the Reflexive Reality programme~\cite{SpivackCompUniversality,SpivackResearchProgrammes}
identifies Rule~110 as the minimal description-length (MDL) cellular
automaton compatible with the Standard Model generation structure.
The GTE cellular automaton $f_{\MDL}$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_7$-valued
extension of Rule~110 over a five-cell ring $\mathbb{Z}_7^5$
(16{,}807 states), whose charge and winding assignments reproduce the
SM particle spectrum~\cite{ugp-p01}.
The five-cell visible-sector ring has been used to derive the Weinberg
angle~\cite{SpivackWeinbergAngle}, the CKM matrix
structure~\cite{SpivackCKM}, emergent spacetime
dimension~\cite{SpivackEmergentGravity}, and GUT charge
quantization~\cite{ugp-p01}.

The present paper asks: what quantum mechanical structure does 't~Hooft's
framework assign to the $f_{\MDL}$ automaton?
The answer turns out to be precisely the information-loss regime
(Chapter~7): $f_{\MDL}$ on $\mathbb{Z}_7^5$ is a maximal information
eraser --- 98.71\% of states are Garden-of-Eden states with no predecessor,
and every trajectory reaches the vacuum fixed point in at most seven
steps.
The resulting physical Hilbert space is one-dimensional, and the SM
generation states are transient excitations whose stability ordering
is encoded in their tail lengths.

\paragraph{What this paper does and does not claim.}
We prove (at \CatA{} or \CatAD{}) that:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the $f_{\MDL}$ Hilbert space has dimension 1, spanned by
  $\ket{\text{vac}}$, with $E=0$;
\item the SM generation stability ordering is reproduced qualitatively
  by the tail-length hierarchy;
\item the SM gauge group $G_{\rm SM}=SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times
  U(1)_{\rm EM}$ can be derived from the $\mathbb{Z}_7$ winding-sector
  information-equivalence classes.
\end{itemize}
We do \emph{not} claim to derive the Schr\"{o}dinger equation from first
principles (the continuum limit from a discrete CA to continuous quantum
field theory is an open problem, \CatD{}), nor do we claim that the
cogwheel eigenvalues determine the SM mass spectrum (they are falsified
as a mass formula at \CatA{}).
The gauge identification of \cref{sec:gauge} is 't~Hooft's own conjecture
(\S9.3 of~\cite{tHooft2016CA}) applied to the specific \GTE{} automaton;
its Lean 4 certification is identified as a target for future work.

The paper is organised as follows.
\Cref{sec:hilbert} presents the $f_{\MDL}$ Hamiltonian spectrum and the
Hilbert space structure, including the dark-sector contrast.
\Cref{sec:two_role} states and justifies the Two-Role Structural Principle; this separation is forced by the failure of the direct cogwheel eigenvalue-to-mass correspondence (demonstrated in \S\ref{ssec:eigen_fail}).
\Cref{sec:gauge} derives the SM gauge groups from $\mathbb{Z}_7$
information-equivalence classes.
\Cref{sec:born} discusses the Born rule and the role of measurement.
\Cref{sec:lorentz_causal} connects AFCA causal invariance to Lorentz
structure and special-relativistic time dilation.
\Cref{sec:open} collects open problems.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$f_{\protect\MDL}$}{fMDL} Hilbert Space}
\label{sec:hilbert}

\subsection{Cogwheel construction on \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{Z}_7^5$}{Z75}}
\label{ssec:cogwheel}

In 't~Hooft's cogwheel framework~\cite{tHooft2016CA}, an ontological
CA with state space $\Omega$ defines a quantum system as follows.
Let $T:\Omega\to\Omega$ be the deterministic evolution operator.
The physical Hilbert space $\Hil_{\rm phys}$ is spanned by the
\emph{periodic orbit states}: if $s$ belongs to a cycle of period $N$
under $T$, then it contributes $N$ energy eigenstates with eigenvalues
$E_k = 2\pi k/N$, $k=0,\ldots,N-1$, to $\Hil_{\rm phys}$.
Transient states --- those that eventually reach a cycle but are not
themselves cycle states --- do not contribute cycle eigenstates; they
are the \emph{Garden-of-Eden} (GoE) states if they have no predecessor
under $T$.

\begin{remark}[CA ontology and Wolfram Physics]\label{rem:wolfram_beable}
The present construction grounds quantum mechanics in ontological CA
states (beables) following 't~Hooft's cogwheel mechanism; an
independent programme grounding quantum mechanics in CA-like ``rules''
and identifying physical states with CA configurations is developed
in~\cite{Wolfram2020Tech} (\S8.2).
The two approaches share the CA-level ontological foundation but
differ in their computational substrate and derivation strategy.
\end{remark}

We apply this construction to $f_{\MDL}:\mathbb{Z}_7^5\to\mathbb{Z}_7^5$,

\begin{theorem}[Dimension of the $f_{\MDL}$ Hilbert space, \CatA{}]
\label{thm:hilbert_dim}
The physical Hilbert space $\Hil_{\rm phys}$ of the $f_{\MDL}$
cogwheel on $\mathbb{Z}_7^5$ is one-dimensional:
\[
\Hil_{\rm phys} \;=\; \mathbb{C}\,\ket{\mathrm{vac}},
\qquad

\]

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By exhaustive orbit decomposition (\CatA{}, Table~\ref{tab:orbit}),
the only cycle is the vacuum fixed point $(0,0,0,0,0)$ of period $N=1$.
Under 't~Hooft's prescription, only cycle states contribute to
$\Hil_{\rm phys}$; a period-1 cycle contributes one eigenvalue
$E_0 = 2\pi\cdot 0/1 = 0$.
No other cycles exist, so $\dim\Hil_{\rm phys} = 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quotient Hilbert space construction]\label{rem:quotient}
The passage from the full ontological Hilbert space to the physical Hilbert space
follows 't~Hooft's Chapter~7 quotient construction.
Define the long-time equivalence relation: $s \sim s'$ if $\lim_{t\to\infty}
f_{\MDL}^t(s) = \lim_{t\to\infty} f_{\MDL}^t(s')$, i.e.\ two CA states are
identified if they share the same long-time attractor.
The full ontological Hilbert space is $\mathcal{H}_{\rm full} =
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\ket{s} : s \in \mathbb{Z}_7^5\}$ (dimension $7^5 =
16{,}807$).
Since $f_{\MDL}$ on $\mathbb{Z}_7^5$ has a unique cycle --- the vacuum fixed point
$(0,0,0,0,0)$ --- every trajectory converges to the vacuum.
Consequently $\lim_{t\to\infty} f_{\MDL}^t(s) = (0,0,0,0,0)$ for all $s \in
\mathbb{Z}_7^5$, and the equivalence relation collapses all 16{,}807 states into a
single equivalence class.
The physical Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{\rm phys} = \mathcal{H}_{\rm full}/{\sim}$
is therefore one-dimensional: $\mathcal{H}_{\rm phys} = \mathbb{C}\ket{\mathrm{vac}}$.

The SM generation states gen$_1$, gen$_2$, gen$_3$ are \emph{transient excitations}
in this quotient picture: they are not members of the equivalence class (they are
not cycle states) but are pre-image chains leading to it, with tail lengths
$\ell(\mathrm{gen}_1) = 3$, $\ell(\mathrm{gen}_2) = 2$, $\ell(\mathrm{gen}_3) = 1$
(Table~\ref{tab:sm_orbit}).

A rough calibration of the CA timestep $\tau_{\rm CA}$ follows from the muon (gen$_2$)
tail length.
The muon has tail length 2, so it decays to vacuum in 2 CA steps:
$\tau_{\mu} \approx 2\tau_{\rm CA}$.
Using the empirical muon lifetime $\tau_{\mu} = 2.197\,\mu\text{s}$, this gives
$\tau_{\rm CA} \approx 1.1\,\mu\text{s}$ as an effective CA timestep for
second-generation decay.
This is an order-of-magnitude estimate only (\CatD{}: the CA step may correspond to
many underlying fundamental steps; no quantitative mass derivation is claimed here).
The figure $1.1\,\mu\text{s}$ is a purely formal ratio; it does not imply the CA
timestep is macroscopic.  The physical CA timestep is expected to be of order the
Planck time (${\sim}10^{-44}\,\text{s}$), many orders of magnitude below the muon
lifetime; the generation-tail ratio 2:1 is a dimensionless combinatorial count,
not a physical time interval.
The tail lengths 3:2:1 reproduce the qualitative stability ordering exactly, but the
quantitative decay rates require a separate dynamical calculation beyond orbit
combinatorics.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The direct eigenvalue-mass correspondence fails}
\label{ssec:eigen_fail}

A natural conjecture is that the SM generation masses are proportional
to the cogwheel Hamiltonian eigenvalues.
We test this conjecture systematically.

\paragraph{T=3 cyclic approximation.}
Treating the SM generation orbit as a cyclic cogwheel of period $T=3$
yields equally-spaced eigenvalues $E_k = 2\pi k/3$ for $k=1,2,3$.
The ratio spread between $E_k/(\Neff(\text{gen}_k)/c_H)$ is 69.3\%,
far from the 0\% that proportionality would require.

\paragraph{T=4 cyclic approximation.}
Including the vacuum as a fourth state and assigning $T=4$ gives
a ratio spread of 63.0\% --- also far from proportionality.

\paragraph{Direct mass comparison.}
Any period-$T$ cogwheel predicts mass ratios $1:2:\cdots:T$ (equally
spaced).
The PDG lepton masses give $m_e:m_\mu:m_\tau \approx 1:207:3477$,
a discrepancy of 10{,}238\% for $m_\mu/m_e$ and 115{,}807\% for
$m_\tau/m_e$ relative to the cogwheel prediction.

\paragraph{Alternative hypotheses.}
Orbit depth as an energy proxy fails because the depth ordering is
\emph{inverted} relative to the mass ordering
(deeper = lighter, not heavier).
The predecessor-count hypothesis fails because there is only one
eigenvalue $E=0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_7^5$, leaving no distinct eigenvalues
to carry degeneracy multiplicities.
All $N_{\rm eff}$ values (73, 42, 275) differ from the predecessor
counts (0, 1, 1) for $\text{gen}_{1,2,3}$.

\begin{proposition}[Eigenvalue-mass falsification, \CatA{}]
\label{prop:eigen_fail}
The direct correspondence $E_k \propto m(\text{gen}_k)$ fails on every
formulation: equally-spaced cogwheel ($>10{,}000\%$ discrepancy),
orbit-depth proxy (ordering inverted), degeneracy assignment (only
$E=0$ exists).
The near-miss $|\text{tail-3 state count}| = 75$ vs.\ $\Neff(\text{gen}_1)=73$
(2.7\% difference) is noted but does not constitute a theorem.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Statement of the Two-Role Structural Principle}
\label{ssec:two_role}

The failure of the direct eigenvalue-mass correspondence points to a

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]

These contributions are non-redundant and complementary: the
cogwheel provides the quantum mechanical scaffolding; the GTE cascade

This is an organizing structural principle (\CatAD{}), not a

\end{remark}

\subsection{Physical significance}
\label{ssec:two_role_physics}

The Two-Role Structural Principle explains why the direct eigenvalue-mass
correspondence fails: masses are determined not by the period of the
cycle in $\mathbb{Z}_7^5$ (which is trivially 1) but by the arithmetic
of the GTE cascade that generates the Z$_7$ state alphabet.
The cogwheel is the \emph{kinematic} framework; the GTE arithmetic is
the \emph{dynamical} input that fills in the mass spectrum.

This separation parallels the structure of standard quantum field theory,
where the Hilbert space and the canonical commutation relations (the
kinematic framework) are universal, while the specific mass spectrum
depends on the Lagrangian (the dynamical input).
In the present framework, the Lagrangian's role is played by the GTE
$\Neff$ cascade.

\section{Information-Equivalence Classes and Gauge Structure}
\label{sec:gauge}

\subsection{Information erasure and equivalence classes}
\label{ssec:erasure}

As established in \cref{sec:hilbert}, $f_{\MDL}$ on $\mathbb{Z}_7^5$
erases all information within at most 7 steps: $T^7(s) =
(0,0,0,0,0)$ for every $s \in \mathbb{Z}_7^5$.
The naive forward-trajectory equivalence $s \sim s'$ iff
$T^t(s) = T^t(s')$ for some $t \geq 0$ therefore yields only a
single equivalence class (all 16{,}807 states are equivalent).
This extreme form of information erasure produces no non-trivial
gauge structure at the CA beable level.

To recover gauge structure, we follow 't~Hooft's \S9.3 prescription:
the physically meaningful equivalence classes are defined by
\emph{observable quantum numbers} --- the quantities that
macro-scale observers can measure --- not by the full CA configuration.
For the \GTE{} framework, the relevant observable quantum number
is the $\mathbb{Z}_7$ winding number $W = \sum_{i=1}^5 w_i \bmod 7$.

\subsection{Winding-sector equivalence classes}
\label{ssec:winding}

\begin{definition}[Winding equivalence]
\label{def:winding}
Two configurations $s, s' \in \mathbb{Z}_7^5$ are winding-equivalent,
$s \sim_W s'$, if $\sum_i s_i \equiv \sum_i s'_i \pmod{7}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Winding class structure, \CatAL{}]
