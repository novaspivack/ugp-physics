\subsection{The cosmological constant problem}

The cosmological constant problem is the largest quantitative discrepancy in
physics. A naive sum of quantum vacuum-energy contributions up to the Planck
scale exceeds the observed dark-energy density by roughly $120$ orders of
magnitude~\cite{Weinberg1989}. The standard responses fall into three families:
fine-tuning of a bare constant against vacuum contributions to one part in
$10^{120}$; an anthropic selection over a landscape of vacua~\cite{Weinberg1987};
or a dynamical relaxation mechanism, of which Weinberg's no-go theorem shows that
no scalar field coupled to gravity through a local effective action can cancel the
constant without reintroducing a fine-tuning of equal severity. None of these
predicts the \emph{value} of the observed dark-energy fraction
$\OmL = 0.6889$~\cite{Planck2018}.

Holographic approaches~\cite{CohenKaplanNelson1999,Hsu2004} reduce the discrepancy
by correlating the ultraviolet and infrared cutoffs of an effective field theory,
yielding the parametric scaling $\rho_\Lambda \sim \MPl^2\Hnought^2$, which is of
the right order. They fail, however, on the equation of state: the
Cohen--Kaplan--Nelson (CKN) construction and its refinements predict
$w \equiv p/\rho = 0$ over cosmological distances~\cite{Hsu2004}, in sharp conflict
with the observed accelerated expansion, which requires $w \approx -1$.

This paper derives both the magnitude and the value of $\OmL$ from a discrete
substrate with no free parameters, and shows that the equation of state is fixed at
$w = -1$ by a structural boundary condition rather than by dynamics --- evading
both Weinberg's no-go theorem and the CKN obstruction.

\subsection{Difference from holographic dark energy}

holographic dark-energy programme founders precisely on $w = 0$~\cite{Hsu2004};
the present construction does not, because the dark-energy density is a constant of
the cosmic evolution by self-containment, not a horizon-tracking quantity.
