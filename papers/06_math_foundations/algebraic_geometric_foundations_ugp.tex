\begin{abstract}
We present the complete mathematical foundations of the Universal Generative Principle (UGP) \cite{SpivackUGPFormalization,ugp-lean}, a deterministic, computable framework from which the laws of physics emerge. We demonstrate that the UGP is a self-contained, axiomatically-driven mathematical structure. We prove the Quarter-Lock Law as a theorem of the UGP's invariant space and derive the algebraic constants of the Elegant Kernel from its symmetries. The centerpiece of this work is a two-stage, first-principles derivation of the Standard Model gauge couplings. First, we derive the bare algebraic values of $g_1^2, g_2^2, g_3^2$ from their unique, group-specific invariants. Second, we derive a Universal Instantiation Factor, $\delta_{\text{UGP}}$, a parameter-free correction that universally applies to all bare constants, accounting for the physical realization of the theory on its discrete substrate. This factor is derived from the Principle of Invariant Restoration, a necessary consequence of the Quarter-Lock Law. This work establishes the UGP as a single, coherent mathematical object that derives the algebraic structure and several exact parameter values; other sectors are classified A/D or B in the companion paper~\cite{Spivack2026_SM_UGP}. The full SM parameter status ledger, including A/D and B sectors, is given in~\cite{Spivack2026_SM_UGP}.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The Quarter-Lock Law as a Theorem of Invariant Space}
The GTE dynamics can be analyzed in a 3D space of "invariant defects" $(M, G, L)$.

\begin{theorem}[Kernel Symmetry Theorem]
A two-step GTE evolution block acts as a rank-one perturbation of the identity on the invariant defect triple $(M, G, L)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The GTE's mirror and gap symmetries freeze two of the three degrees of freedom in the defect space, leaving only a single direction for evolution. Any change must therefore be a projection onto this one-dimensional subspace, which is a rank-one update of the form $T = I + u v^T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The Quarter-Lock Law]
Any invariant kernel of the GTE must have its coefficients lie on the plane defined by the exact identity:
\begin{equation}
    k_M = k_{\text{gen2}} + \frac{1}{4}k_{L^2}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
An invariant kernel must be in the left null space of the rank-one perturbation. Explicit calculation shows the perturbation vector $u$ is parallel to $(1, -1, -1/4)$. The orthogonality condition $k \cdot u = 0$ directly yields the Quarter-Lock identity.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Derivation of Möbius Coefficients: $k_a, k_b, k_c$}

The three flavor/interaction coefficients $(k_a, k_b, k_c)$ are the most constrained constants in the Elegant Kernel. \paragraph{(i) Discovery Pathway: PSLQ Integer-Relation Search}

The values $(k_a, k_b, k_c)$ were first identified by a PSLQ (integer-relation) search algorithm applied to the empirical UCL coefficients emerging from UGP dynamical simulations at $n=10$. The PSLQ algorithm (Ferguson-Bailey) seeks integer linear dependencies among real numbers, effectively discovering exact algebraic or rational constants from high-precision decimal approximations.

\textbf{Discovery Protocol:}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*., itemsep=0.15em]
\item Run GTE simulation at $n=10$ for $10^6$ steps, accumulating statistics for $M, G, L$ defects.
\item Fit a quadratic kernel model to the defect distribution, extracting empirical coefficients.
\item Feed the three floating-point Möbius coefficients (3-element vector) to the PSLQ algorithm with 256-bit precision, requesting small-integer relationships.
\item PSLQ returns three independent integer relations of the form:
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_1 k_a + q_1 &= 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad k_a = -q_1/p_1 \\
p_2 k_b + q_2 &= 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad k_b = -q_2/p_2 \\
p_3 k_c + q_3 &= 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad k_c = -q_3/p_3
\end{aligned}
\]
where $(p_i, q_i)$ are small integers (typically $|p_i|, |q_i| < 10$).
\item Verify the candidate rational values by computing the gauge couplings and comparing with experimental data.
\end{enumerate}

The PSLQ search conclusively identified:
\[
(k_a, k_b, k_c) = \left(\frac{1}{8}, -\frac{3}{2}, \frac{4}{3}\right)
\]
with integer relations $(8k_a - 1 = 0)$, $(2k_b + 3 = 0)$, $(3k_c - 4 = 0)$, respectively. These results are documented in the PSLQ output file \texttt{ucl\_pslq\_best.json} in the Discovery Lab archives.

The success of PSLQ hinges on two mathematical facts: (1) the UGP dynamics are \emph{exactly} computable in rational arithmetic (no transcendental approximations), and (2) the MDL axiom forces the minimal-complexity kernel to have small-integer numerators and denominators. Thus, the "true" values are low-complexity rationals, and PSLQ efficiently recovers them from high-precision decimal approximations.

\textbf{The Möbius Product Defect $\mathcal{M}$:}
Recall that $\mathcal{M} = M(b_1,b_2) \cdot M(q_1,q_2)$, where $M(a,b) = \prod_{p \mid \gcd(a,b)} p$ is the product of prime divisors of the GCD. The UCL expansion for $\mathcal{M}$ involves three interaction terms:
\[
\log\mathcal{M} = k_a\log b_1 + k_b\log b_2 + k_c\log c_1 + \ldots
\]
where $c_1 = F_{b_1}$ (the $b_1$-th Fibonacci number) is the prime-locked capacity.

\textbf{Constraint 1 (SU(2) Parallel Averaging):}
The SU(2) coupling invariant must satisfy the parallel-averaging condition: for the three squared face areas $A_{ab}^2, A_{bc}^2, A_{ca}^2$ of the Möbius tetrahedron, the invariant $f$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{3}\!\left(\frac{1}{A_{ab}^2} + \frac{1}{A_{bc}^2} + \frac{1}{A_{ca}^2}\right).
\label{eq:parallel_avg}
\end{equation}
This is a consistency condition of the SU(2) system under the UGP's ridge symmetry: parallel averaging is the unique combination (Theorem~\ref{thm:su2_rigidity}) that respects permutation symmetry, 1-homogeneity, and the single-plane limit $f(x,\infty,\infty) = 3x$. Note that $k_a + k_b = 1/8 - 3/2 = -11/8$; a naive ``dimension-matching'' relation $k_a + k_b \approx 1/2$ is not satisfied and is not part of the structural derivation.

\textbf{Constraint 2 (Mirror Duality):}
The $(b_1, b_2)$ system exhibits mirror duality under the swap $b_1 \leftrightarrow q_1$. This imposes a sign asymmetry:
\begin{equation}
k_a = -k_b / \alpha
\label{eq:mirror_constraint}
\end{equation}
for some rational $\alpha > 0$.

\textbf{Constraint 3 (Capacity Normalization):}
The capacity term $c_1 = F_{b_1}$ grows exponentially, $F_n \sim \varphi^n$. To prevent exponential dominance, the kernel must soft-suppress capacity via a fractional positive coefficient:
\begin{equation}
0 < k_c < 2, \quad k_c \in \mathbb{Q}
\label{eq:capacity_constraint}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Constraint 4 (Minimal Complexity / MDL Axiom):}
Among all rational solutions satisfying \eqref{eq:parallel_avg}--\eqref{eq:capacity_constraint}, the MDL axiom selects the unique triplet with \emph{minimal description length}. \section{Rigidity Proofs for Gauge Group Invariants}
\label{app:rigidity_proofs}

The discrete invariants $D_2$ (for SU(2)) and $D_3$ (for SU(3)) are not arbitrary functions of the Möbius coefficients—they are the \emph{unique} symmetric functions satisfying the UGP's algebraic and geometric constraints. We present rigorous uniqueness proofs for both.

\subsection{SU(2) Rigidity: The Harmonic Mean is Unique}
\label{app:su2_rigidity}

\begin{theorem}[SU(2) Harmonic Mean Uniqueness]
\label{thm:su2_rigidity}
Let $f: \mathbb{R}^3_{>0} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a function of the three squared pairwise products $(A_{ab}^2, A_{bc}^2, A_{ca}^2)$ where $A_{ij} = k_i k_j$. If $f$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Permutation Invariance ($S_3$ symmetry):} $f$ is symmetric under all permutations of its three arguments.
    \item \textbf{1-Homogeneity:} $f(\lambda A_{ab}^2, \lambda A_{bc}^2, \lambda A_{ca}^2) = \lambda\, f(A_{ab}^2, A_{bc}^2, A_{ca}^2)$.
    \item \textbf{Parallel Averaging:} $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{3}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{A_{ab}^2} + \dfrac{1}{A_{bc}^2} + \dfrac{1}{A_{ca}^2}\right)$.
    \item \textbf{Single-Plane Limit:} $f(x, \infty, \infty) = 3x$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $f$ is uniquely determined to be the \textbf{harmonic mean of the squared pairwise products}:
\begin{equation*}
f = \operatorname{HM}(A_{ab}^2, A_{bc}^2, A_{ca}^2)
= \frac{3}{\dfrac{1}{A_{ab}^2} + \dfrac{1}{A_{bc}^2} + \dfrac{1}{A_{ca}^2}}.
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
The key lemmas (LM1–LM3) are verified with exact rational arithmetic by the UGP Discovery Lab module \texttt{su2\_rigidity\_proof.py}; LM4 and LM5 rely on finite sampling and numerical verification respectively (see Remark~\ref{rem:comp_verification} below). We outline the key steps:

\textbf{Step 1 ($S_3$ Symmetry — LM1):}
Permutation invariance is verified exactly for the harmonic mean by direct computation.

\textbf{Step 2 (1-Homogeneity — LM2):}
Under $A \to \lambda A$, $\mathrm{HM}(\lambda A_{ab}^2, \lambda A_{bc}^2, \lambda A_{ca}^2) = \lambda \cdot \mathrm{HM}(A_{ab}^2, A_{bc}^2, A_{ca}^2)$. Verified exactly.

\textbf{Step 3 (Parallel Averaging — LM3):}
Constraint (iii) is the rational characterisation of the harmonic mean: $1/\mathrm{HM}(x,y,z) = (1/x + 1/y + 1/z)/3$. Verified with exact rational arithmetic.

\textbf{Step 4 (Uniqueness of Power Mean — LM4):}
Among all power means $M_p(x,y,z) = ((x^p+y^p+z^p)/3)^{1/p}$, only $p = -1$ satisfies the parallel-averaging condition (LM3). The module checks violation at the sample exponents $p\in\{-2,+1,+2\}$ by floating-point comparison; the general case follows analytically from constraint (iii), which is itself the definition of $p=-1$.

\textbf{Step 5 (Single-Plane Limit — LM5):}
$f(x, \infty, \infty) = 3x$ is numerically verified using a surrogate $M = 10^{12}$ for $\infty$, achieving agreement to within $10^{-6}$. The exact limit follows directly from the harmonic-mean formula as $M \to \infty$.

Together, LM1–LM5 uniquely select the harmonic mean as the SU(2) invariant. No experimental target is used in this derivation; the rigidity is purely algebraic.
\end{proof}

\subsection{SU(3) Rigidity: The Vandermonde Discriminant is Unique}
\label{app:su3_rigidity}

\begin{theorem}[SU(3) Vandermonde Uniqueness]
Let $g: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$ be an antisymmetric function of three variables $(k_a, k_b, k_c)$. If $g$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Full Antisymmetry:} $g(k_{\sigma(1)}, k_{\sigma(2)}, k_{\sigma(3)}) = \text{sgn}(\sigma) \cdot g(k_1, k_2, k_3)$ for all permutations $\sigma \in S_3$.
    \item \textbf{Homogeneity:} $g(\lambda k_a, \lambda k_b, \lambda k_c) = \lambda^3 g(k_a, k_b, k_c)$.
    \item \textbf{Polynomial Structure:} $g$ is a polynomial in $(k_a, k_b, k_c)$.
    \item \textbf{Non-Commutativity Measure:} $g^2$ (the squared function) must be positive-definite for distinct inputs and vanish if any two inputs coincide.
\end{enumerate}
Then $g$ is uniquely determined (up to a sign and overall constant) to be the \textbf{Vandermonde discriminant}:
\begin{equation*}
g = \Delta(k_a, k_b, k_c) = (k_a - k_b)(k_b - k_c)(k_c - k_a),
\end{equation*}
and the SU(3) invariant is $D_3 = \Delta^2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
The full proof is classical (related to the theory of symmetric functions) and has been computationally verified by \texttt{su3\_rigidity\_proof.py}.

\textbf{Step 1 (Antisymmetry Forces Product Form):}
Any fully antisymmetric polynomial must be divisible by the Vandermonde determinant $\Delta$. That is, $g = c \cdot \Delta \cdot h(k_a, k_b, k_c)$ for some symmetric polynomial $h$.

\textbf{Step 2 (Homogeneity Fixes $h$):}
Since $\Delta$ has degree 3 (one factor of each $k_i$ in the product of differences) and $g$ must have degree 3, we need $h$ to have degree 0. Thus $h$ is a constant, and $g = c \cdot \Delta$.

\textbf{Step 3 (Normalization from Gauge Coupling):}
The constant $c$ is absorbed into the Lie factor $L_{SU(3)} = 6$ and the master formula normalization. The physically relevant quantity is $D_3 = \Delta^2$, which is manifestly positive.

\textbf{Step 4 (Non-Commutativity Interpretation):}
For SU(3), the squared Vandermonde $\Delta^2$ measures the "non-commutativity volume" of the 3D charge simplex. When $k_a, k_b, k_c$ are collinear (commutative limit), $\Delta = 0$ and the SU(3) structure collapses. The non-zero value $\Delta^2 = 41{,}075{,}281/1{,}327{,}104$ ensures the \emph{non-abelian} nature of the strong interaction.
\end{proof}
