\begin{abstract}
The Universal Generative Principle requires a physical substrate capable of two
operationally distinct but inseparable modes: \emph{computation} (deterministic
evolution under $\fmdl$) and \emph{transputation} (PSC-forced, non-computable
selection among undecidable alternatives).  We formalize this as the
Generative Triple Evolution (GTE)-M?bius architecture---the triple $(A, e, \Dclass)$, where $A$ is the GTE
arithmetic carrier ($\Zseven$ winding dynamics), $e$ is the self-encoding map
conferring Turing completeness, and $\Dclass$ is the class of PSC-consistent
coherence measures constrained by D1--D5.  The M?bius name reflects a
single-surface architecture: computation and transputation share a common
substrate without a sharp syntactic boundary; the G?del--Turing boundary of
$(A,e)$ is the computation/transputation boundary of the physics.
The GTE-M?bius architecture is not a physical substrate independent of
$\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$; it is the computational-architectural description of the
same physical field.  $\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$ is the physical substrate of the
universe; the M?bius structure characterizes how that field processes information
and adjudicates quantum events.

We prove that the $T(2,3)$ torus knot has both parameters GTE-derived
(\catAL{}, zero \texttt{sorry}), define the Turing--PSC (TPC) computability class
as strictly intermediate between Turing-decidable and hypercomputation (13 Lean
theorems, \catAL{}), and show its three-level hierarchy depth equals
$\ngen = 3$ (\catAL{}).  Conjecture~C1 (GTE as the final
$F_{\mathrm{PSC}}$-coalgebra) is fully proved (\catAL{}, zero axiom); four
consistency conjectures (C2--C5) remain open, with C3 and C4 the most tractable.

The abstract carrier $A$ realizes as the PSC-admissible kink sector of the
$\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$ Klein--Gordon field; binary coarse-graining recovers
$\fmdl$ in the continuum limit.  Three Lean-certified lifting theorems connect
beable, discrete, and physical scales (\catAL{}); the GTE equivalence principle
and geodesic theorem (\catAD{}) connect emergent gravity to $\Dclass$-weighted
trajectories.  Quantum field-theoretic and completeness consequences are
developed in companion papers~\cite{SpivackCMCA,SpivackThreeTapeCMCA,SpivackPhiMDLField,SpivackCompleteness,SpivackQGR}.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The UGP Substrate Problem}
\label{sec:substrate-problem}

Papers P22 through P29 in the Universal Generative Principle (UGP) series derive
Standard Model quantum numbers, mass hierarchies, gauge coupling structure, and the
three-generation pattern from the arithmetic of $\Zseven$ winding
dynamics~\cite{SpivackCompUniversality}.  Those derivations establish \emph{what} the Standard Model
predicts; they do not settle a deeper structural question: what \emph{kind} of
computational object underlies those derivations, and how does a single substrate
generate both the deterministic, wave-like evolution of free particles and the
irreversible, outcome-selecting events of quantum measurement?

A free electron propagating between measurements follows amplitude evolution that is,
in principle, perfectly computable: given an initial $\Zseven$ configuration and the
cellular-automaton rule $\fmdl$, the next state is deterministic and
algorithmically reproducible to arbitrary precision.

But when that same electron impinges on a detector, quantum mechanics must select a
definite outcome from a probability distribution---and no Turing-equivalent process
can execute that selection.  A Turing machine can evaluate $|c_n|^2$ for every branch
$n$, but it cannot single out which branch occurs in a given physical event without
violating the Church-Turing thesis.

This paper formalizes the answer.  The substrate is a \emph{single}
mathematical-physical object whose architecture accommodates both modes of action:
\emph{computation} (deterministic $\fmdl$ evolution) and \emph{transputation}
(non-computable, PSC-forced selection among undecidable alternatives).  We call this
object the \textbf{GTE-M?bius architecture} (hereafter, the M?bius architecture)
and specify it as the triple $(A, e, \Dclass)$.
This structure is not a physical substrate independent of $\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$:
$\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$ is the physical substrate of the universe, and the M?bius
architecture is its computational-architectural description.
The abstract carrier~$A$ has its concrete physical realization as the
PSC-admissible kink sector of the $\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$ continuum field
(\S\ref{sec:phimdl-realization}); the discrete rule $\fmdl$ is its Level-1
algebraic certificate, not the physical substrate itself
(\S\ref{sec:subsequent-papers}).

\subsection{The Two Operational Modes and Why They Must Coexist}
\label{sec:two-modes}

The computational mode is Turing-equivalent.  Free propagation, $\fmdl$ orbit
evolution, decay rates, and Born-rule probabilities computed as functions of quantum
numbers are all decidable by finite evaluations on the $\Zseven$ configuration space.
Lean~4 certifies the key instances at zero \texttt{sorry}: the Garden-of-Eden
theorem (\lean{garden\_of\_eden\_gen1}), the three-generation orbit, and the $N_c = 3$
colour count (\catAL, Paper P28).

The transputation mode is provably non-computable.  The \emph{diagonal barrier}
(machine-certified in Lean~4, zero \texttt{sorry}~\cite{SpivackNEMS11}) establishes that
any total-effective adjudicator on diagonal-capable record fragments---one that selects
among equally-consistent continuations while self-consistently updating a macroscopic
record---would decide the halting problem.

Because the halting problem is undecidable, no computable function can serve as
adjudicator for measurement-outcome selection, decay timing, or vacuum-reachability.
The substrate must therefore perform a second, genuinely non-computable mode of action.

These two modes cannot be cleanly separated.  The divide between decidable
reachability questions and undecidable self-referential ones is determined by
\emph{semantic content}, not syntactic form.

The same formal symbol string may encode a bounded $\Zseven$ arithmetic fact (Zone~L1,
Turing-decidable) or an encoding of a halting question (Zone~L2, diagonal), depending
on what it represents.  There is no static syntactic filter.

The operational trigger for Zone~L2 is nonetheless formally precise: a physical event
requires transputation if and only if its outcome is logically equivalent to deciding
the halting problem for $\fmdl$ acting on its own dynamics.  Free particle propagation,
amplitude evolution, and beable-level orbit arithmetic are always Zone~L1.  Quantum
measurement events---the creation of irreversible macroscopic records---are Zone~L2,
because selecting a definite outcome from a superposition is equivalent to deciding
vacuum-reachability for $\fmdl$ (\lean{fmdl\_vacuum\_reachability\_is\_undecidable},
machine-certified, \catAL{} conditional).  The transition is driven by the type of
question asked, not by a change in the underlying physical field.

The M?bius name reflects this topology: what appear to be two distinct faces
(computation; transputation) share a single surface without a sharp boundary.

\subsection{Component \texorpdfstring{$[D]$}{[D]}: The Coherence Measure Class}

  D : \mathcal{R}(A) \times \mathcal{W}(A) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},
  \label{eq:D-map}

\begin{definition}[Perfect Self-Containment (PSC)]\label{def:psc}
A physical framework $\mathcal{T} = (\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{R}, \mathrm{Truth})$ ---
consisting of a mathematical structure $\mathcal{M}$, a set of candidate physical
realizations $\mathcal{R}$, and a truth valuation $\mathrm{Truth}$ ---
satisfies \emph{Perfect Self-Containment} if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Internal determination.}  Every selector for observational
    equivalence classes is determined internally by $\mathcal{T}$: no external model
    selector or free metaparameter is required.
  \item \textbf{No free bits.}  The truth valuation $\mathrm{Truth}$ has no free bits
    outside the framework's own description; every physical question has a
    determinate (though possibly non-computable) answer within $\mathcal{T}$.
  \item \textbf{Extension rigidity.}  Any extension of $\mathcal{T}$ that adds a new
    free bit either violates PSC (if the bit remains undetermined) or is
    record-equivalent to $\mathcal{T}$ (if the bit is fixed by existing structure).
\end{enumerate}
These three requirements are implemented by five axioms: RC (reflexive closure:
anomaly cancellation, unitarity, UV consistency), NM$^*$ (structural stability),
TV (thermodynamic viability), SA (semantic admissibility: information-bearing
subsystems exist), and PI (presentation invariance: minimal Kolmogorov-complexity
Lagrangian is selected)~\cite{SpivackNEMS05}.

In the GTE context, PSC is satisfied by the substrate $(A, e, \Dclass)$:
component $A$ is fully determined by PSC + the P28 derivation chain (zero free
parameters), component $e$ is fixed by MDL minimality, and the coherence measure

The five PSC axioms---reflexive closure (RC), structural stability (NM$^*$),

\subsection{TPC vs.\ Oracle Computation}
\label{sec:tpc-oracle}

$\TPC$ is not an oracle class in the arithmetical hierarchy.  The differences
are substantial.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em, itemsep=4pt]
  \item[TPC $\neq$ Turing machine with halting oracle.]
    $\TPC$ cannot decide the halting problem even with oracle access: D3 forbids
    total-effective adjudication on diagonal-capable fragments.  A Turing machine
    with the halting oracle $\emptyset'$ can decide all $\Sigma^0_1$ questions;
    $\TPC$ cannot.

  \item[TPC $\neq$ any level of the arithmetical hierarchy.]
    The arithmetical hierarchy classifies \emph{decision problems} with yes/no
    answers.  $\TPC$ classifies \emph{selection problems}---the identification of
    a canonical element from a candidate set.  Decision problems and selection
    problems are different logical types; neither contains the other.  $\TPC$ is
    incommensurable with the arithmetical hierarchy.

  \item[TPC $\neq$ hypercomputation of any standard kind.]
    Hypercomputation targets decision problems of higher arithmetical complexity.
    $\TPC$ targets selection problems subject to PSC invariance and Born-rule
    consistency.  These are distinct objectives.

  \item[Computation and transputation are not ordered types.]
    ``Transputation $\supset$ computation'' is an error.  Computation produces
    answers to decision problems (a function $\{0,1\}^* \to \{0,1\}^*$);
    transputation produces canonical selections from candidate sets (a function
    $\mathcal{P}(A) \times \mathcal{W}(A) \to A$).  These are different function
    types; neither is a special case of the other.
\end{description}
