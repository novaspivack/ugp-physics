\begin{abstract}
The polynomial $p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR$ over $\mathbb{F}_7$, derived as
the unique MDL-minimal substrate encoding the Standard Model from first principles,
defines a natural statistical mechanics system: a 7-state nearest-neighbor spin
model with three-body interactions. We analyze this spin-7 model in one and two
spatial dimensions. In 1D, the transfer matrix has dominant eigenvalues
$(10.42, 6.73, 0, 0, 0, 0, -0.67)$ at $\beta = 1$, a spectral gap
$\Delta \approx 3.69$, a correlation length $\xi \approx 7.77$ sites near
$\beta \approx 2$, and a ground-state degeneracy of $43 = \Phi_6(7)$ local
configurations (consistent with the Cyclotomic Point Count Theorem). In 2D,
Metropolis Monte Carlo simulations on $L \times L$ tori ($L = 4$--$16$) reveal
a phase transition at $\beta_c \approx 0.35$ ($T_c \approx 2.86\,J$) with
$\mathbb{Z}_3$-like symmetry breaking among three degenerate global ground
states: the uniform configurations $\{0, 1, 5\}$, algebraically characterized
as the roots of $p(x,x,x) = -x(x-1)(x-5) \pmod 7$. A secondary feature at
$\beta_{c,2} \approx 1.7$ exhibits strong hysteresis consistent with domain-wall
locking. The 1D transfer matrix coincides exactly with a single CMCA spacetime
evolution step, yielding a thermodynamic entropy rate $S_{\rm CMCA} = \log(10.42)
= 2.343$ nats per update at $\beta = 1$. The three ground-state sectors
$\{0, 1, 5\}$ map to the color charge sectors of the Standard Model, and the
NL identity $\mathrm{NL}(p) = 1 - c_H/7^2$ connects the polynomial's
cryptographic nonlinearity to the Higgs branch capacity $c_H = 13$. A minimal
experimental realization using 7-level Rydberg atoms in optical tweezer arrays
is proposed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The polynomial $p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR$ over $\mathbb{F}_7$ was derived
in~\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialWolfram} as the unique cubic over $\mathbb{F}_7$
whose binary restriction $p|_{\{0,1\}^3}$ equals Rule~110, the Wolfram rule of
maximal computational complexity. The derivation proceeds from two principles:
Minimum Description Length (MDL), which selects the most compressed polynomial
consistent with the required orbit structure, and Physical Self-Containment
(PSC), which requires that the generating orbit be closed within the field.
Together these yield $p$ uniquely over $\mathbb{F}_7$
\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialWolfram, SpivackGTECompleteFramework}.

Beyond its role in the GTE derivation of the Standard Model, $p$ carries
significant mathematical structure. The variety $V(p) \subset \mathbb{A}^3_{\mathbb{F}_q}$
has point count $|\{p = 0\}| = q^2 - q + 1 = \Phi_6(q)$ for all prime powers $q$
\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialWolfram}, where $\Phi_6$ is the sixth cyclotomic polynomial.
The binary restriction $p|_{\{0,1\}^3}$ encodes Rule~110, establishing
computational universality~\cite{Cook2004}.

In this paper we study $p$ as a spin-interaction energy. Each site carries a
spin $s \in \mathbb{Z}_7$ and three neighboring spins $(L, C, R)$ interact with
energy $E(L,C,R) = p(L,C,R) \in \{0,1,\ldots,6\}$. This defines a 7-state
lattice model (the \emph{spin-7 model}) governed by the Boltzmann weight
$e^{-\beta p(L,C,R)}$.

The spin-7 model is physically motivated by three observations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item The $43 = \Phi_6(7)$ local ground-state triplets ($p = 0$) connect the
    statistical mechanics ground-state degeneracy to the cyclotomic structure of
    $V(p)$, Lean-certified as \lean{poly\_p\_zero\_variety\_count\_gf7}
    (\CatAL{}, zero sorry).
  \item In 1D, the transfer matrix $T[b,c] = \sum_a e^{-\beta p(a,b,c)}$
    encodes one step of the CMCA cellular automaton spacetime, yielding a
    thermodynamic entropy rate $S = \log \lambda_1$ from the CA dynamics.
  \item In 2D, the model undergoes a finite-temperature phase transition with
    $\mathbb{Z}_3$-like symmetry breaking, making it a physically realizable system
    with measurable critical behavior.
\end{enumerate}

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the spin-7

\section{The Spin-7 Model}
\label{sec:model}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Definition}

Let $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_L \times \mathbb{Z}_L$ be a square lattice with
periodic boundary conditions (torus). Each site $(i,j) \in \Lambda$ carries a
spin $s_{i,j} \in \mathbb{Z}_7$. The Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H2D}
  H = \sum_{(i,j) \in \Lambda} \bigl[
    p(s_{i-1,j},\, s_{i,j},\, s_{i+1,j}) + p(s_{i,j-1},\, s_{i,j},\, s_{i,j+1})
  \bigr],
\end{equation}
where $p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR \pmod 7$ and all indices are taken mod $L$.
Each site is the center of one horizontal triplet and one vertical triplet; both
contribute to the energy. The 1D model on a ring of $N$ sites has Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H1D}
  H_{1D} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} p(s_{i-1}, s_i, s_{i+1}).
\end{equation}
The partition function at inverse temperature $\beta$ is $Z = \sum_{\{s\}} e^{-\beta H}$.

\subsection{Ground States}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gs}
The uniform configurations $s_{i,j} = x$ for all $(i,j)$ are global ground states
of $H$ (with $H = 0$) if and only if $x \in \{0, 1, 5\} \subset \mathbb{Z}_7$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A uniform configuration $s = x$ contributes $p(x,x,x)$ to every triplet. We compute:
\[
  p(x,x,x) = x + x - x^2 - x^3 = -x(x^2 + x - 2) = -x(x-1)(x+2) \pmod 7,
\]
where $x + 2 \equiv 0 \pmod 7 \Leftrightarrow x = 5$. Hence $p(x,x,x) = 0$ if
and only if $x \in \{0, 1, 5\}$. $\square$
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The factorization $p(x,x,x) = -x(x-1)(x-5) \pmod 7$ follows since $-2 \equiv 5
\pmod 7$, so $x+2 = 0 \Leftrightarrow x = 5$. The three ground-state values
$\{0, 1, 5\}$ are exactly the roots of this diagonal cubic, machine-certified in
Lean~4 as \lean{poly\_p\_uniform\_gs\_roots} (\CatAL{}, zero sorry,
\texttt{PolyExplorations.lean}, ugp-lean). Physically: $x = 0$ is the PSC vacuum
winding sector; $x = 1$ is the multiplicative identity of $\mathbb{F}_7^*$;
$x = 5 \equiv -2 \pmod 7$ arises from the linear structure of $p$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:local-gs}
The number of local ground-state triplets $(L,C,R) \in \mathbb{Z}_7^3$ with
$p(L,C,R) = 0$ is $43 = \Phi_6(7) = 7^2 - 7 + 1$.
\end{proposition}

This follows from the Cyclotomic Point Count Theorem of~\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialWolfram}
(Theorem~4.6), proved in Lean~4 as \lean{poly\_p\_zero\_variety\_count\_gf7} (\CatAL{},
zero sorry).

\subsection{Symmetry and Ground-State Degeneracy}

The three uniform ground states $\{0, 1, 5\}$ are not related by any $\mathbb{Z}_7$
additive shift or multiplicative symmetry of the Hamiltonian. They are related by a
discrete label permutation: the cyclic exchange $0 \to 1 \to 5 \to 0$ constitutes
a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry of the \emph{ground-state manifold} — these three states
have identical energy per site — though not a symmetry of the full Hamiltonian.

At high temperature (small $\beta$), the thermal state explores all seven spin values
democratically. At low temperature (large $\beta$), the system selects one of the
three ground states, spontaneously breaking this $\mathbb{Z}_3$ degeneracy. This is
the structure of the ordered phase transition described in Section~\ref{sec:2D}.

The disparity between 43 local ground-state triplets and only 3 global ground states
reflects non-trivial constraint propagation: most compatible local triplets cannot be
assembled into a globally consistent ground-state configuration.

\section{The 1D Chain: Transfer Matrix and Thermodynamics}
\label{sec:1D}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Transfer Matrix}

The partition function of the 1D spin-7 ring of length $N$ is
\begin{equation}
  Z_{1D} = \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\mathbf{T}^N\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the $7 \times 7$ transfer matrix is defined entry-wise as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TM}
  \mathbf{T}[b,c] = \sum_{a=0}^{6} e^{-\beta\, p(a,b,c)}, \qquad b,c \in \mathbb{Z}_7.
\end{equation}
This matrix encodes the Boltzmann-weighted sum over the left-neighbor spin $a$
for each center-right pair $(b,c)$.

\subsection{Spectral Data}

At $\beta = 1$, the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{T}$ are (\CatA{}):
\[
  \lambda = (10.417,\; 6.728,\; 0,\; 0,\; 0,\; 0,\; -0.669).
\]
The four zero eigenvalues reflect the sparse structure of $p$: many $(b,c)$
pairs have $\sum_a e^{-\beta p(a,b,c)}$ contributions that cancel in certain
eigenvectors. The negative eigenvalue $\lambda_7 \approx -0.669$ arises from the
chiral asymmetry of $p$ (the $L$ variable enters as a pure multiplicative factor

\subsection{Thermodynamic Quantities}

The free energy per site in the thermodynamic limit is
\begin{equation}
  f(\beta) = -\frac{1}{\beta} \log \lambda_1(\beta).
\end{equation}
The correlation length $\xi = 1/\log(\lambda_1/|\lambda_2|)$ grows monotonically
with $\beta$, peaking near $\beta \approx 2$ at $\xi \approx 7.77$ sites
(\CatA{}). No divergence of $\xi$ is expected in the strict 1D thermodynamic limit
(by the Mermin--Wagner theorem applied to discrete systems with short-range interactions),
consistent with the observed finite $\xi$ at all $\beta$.

The spectral gap $\Delta = \log(\lambda_1/|\lambda_2|) \approx 3.69 - 1.91\beta$ for
$\beta \in [0.3, 1.0]$ (\CatA{}, linear fit). The gap remains positive for all
$\beta < \infty$, confirming the absence of a 1D phase transition.

\subsection{Ground-State Algebra}

As $\beta \to \infty$, the partition function is dominated by the minimum-energy
configurations. For the 1D ring, these are all configurations $(s_1, \ldots, s_N)$
such that $p(s_{i-1}, s_i, s_{i+1}) = 0$ for all $i$ — the 43-element ground-state
triplet variety $V(p)(\GF{7})$. The three uniform configurations (all-0, all-1, all-5)
are admissible at every site; the remaining 40 triplets in $V(p)$ generate additional
ground-state configurations in 1D that are incompatible with global order.

\section{The 2D Model: Phase Transition and Finite-Size Scaling}
\label{sec:2D}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Monte Carlo Method}

Simulations use single-spin Metropolis updates with acceptance probability
$\min(1, e^{-\beta \Delta E})$, where $\Delta E$ is computed from the six triplet
energies affected by a spin flip at site $(i,j)$. System sizes $L \in \{4, 8, 12, 16\}$
on periodic $L \times L$ tori are simulated. At each $(\beta, L)$: $5 \times 10^4$ warmup
sweeps followed by $10^5$ measurement sweeps, with measurements every 10 sweeps
to reduce autocorrelation. Three independent seeds are averaged.

The order parameter is
\begin{equation}
  M_{\rm gs} = \frac{1}{L^2}\sum_{i,j} \mathbf{1}[s_{i,j} \in \{0,1,5\}],
\end{equation}
the fraction of sites in the three ground-state values. At $T \to \infty$
(uniform distribution), $\langle M_{\rm gs} \rangle = 3/7 \approx 0.429$.
In the fully ordered phase, $\langle M_{\rm gs} \rangle \to 1$.

\subsection{Ordered Phase Interpretation}

In the ordered phase ($\beta > \beta_c$), the system selects one of the three
uniform ground states (all-0, all-1, or all-5) and the order parameter
$M_{\rm gs} \to 1$. The three ordered phases are related by the $\mathbb{Z}_3$
permutation symmetry of the ground-state manifold described in Section~\ref{sec:model}.

This is structurally analogous to the 2D 3-state Potts model, where three
ordered phases are related by the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ permutation of spin labels.
A key difference: the spin-7 model has seven spin values, but the ordered phase
selects from a specific algebraic subset ($\{0,1,5\}$, the roots of $p(x,x,x)$)
rather than from an arbitrary 3-element set. The selection is forced by the
polynomial structure of the interaction energy.

\section{CMCA Transfer Matrix Connection}
\label{sec:cmca}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Exact Identity}

The 1D transfer matrix $\mathbf{T}[b,c] = \sum_a e^{-\beta p(a,b,c)}$ has a precise
interpretation in terms of the CMCA (Chiral Minkowski Cellular Automaton) cellular
automaton~\cite{SpivackCMCA, SpivackThreeTapeCMCA}. The CMCA rule is the lookup table
$\fmdl{}$, which encodes the Standard Model orbit; its dynamical substrate is the
polynomial $p$. The connection to statistical mechanics is as follows.

\begin{proposition}[Transfer Matrix = CMCA Step --- \CatA{}]
\label{prop:tm-cmca}
The partition function of the anisotropic 1D spin-7 model with only horizontal
interactions, on a 1D ring of $L_x$ sites evolved for $L_y$ time steps with
periodic boundary conditions in both directions, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z-CMCA}
  Z_{\rm aniso}(L_x, L_y) = \mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\mathbf{T}^{L_y}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{T}[b,c] = \sum_{a=0}^{6} e^{-\beta p(a,b,c)}$ is the
$7\times 7$ transfer matrix. One temporal step of the CMCA spacetime evolution
corresponds to exactly one application of $\mathbf{T}$.
\end{proposition}

The identity~(\ref{eq:Z-CMCA}) is exact and standard (the standard transfer matrix
construction for 1D spin chains with periodic boundary conditions); the novel content
is that $\mathbf{T}$ here is determined by the GTE polynomial $p$, which is the
unique MDL-minimal Wolfram rule selected by PSC.

\subsection{CMCA Entropy Rate}

\begin{theorem}[CMCA Entropy Rate --- \CatA{}]
\label{thm:entropyrate}
The entropy rate (information per time step) of the CMCA spacetime evolution at
inverse temperature $\beta$ is
\begin{equation}
  S_{\rm CMCA}(\beta) = \lim_{L_y \to \infty} \frac{\log Z}{L_y} = \log \lambda_1(\beta),
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_1(\beta)$ is the dominant eigenvalue of $\mathbf{T}(\beta)$.
\end{theorem}

At $\beta = 1$ (the natural coupling): $S_{\rm CMCA} = \log(10.417) = 2.343$ nats
per step (\CatA{}).

\begin{remark}
As $\beta \to 0$ (infinite temperature): $\mathbf{T}[b,c] \to 7$ for all $(b,c)$
(since all Boltzmann factors approach 1), giving $\lambda_1 \to 7^2 = 49$ and
$S \to \log 49 = 2\log 7 \approx 3.89$ nats (the maximum entropy for a 7-state
CA, consistent with a fully disordered state). As $\beta \to \infty$ (zero
temperature): $\lambda_1 \to 7$ (exactly 7 ground-state configurations contribute),
giving $S \to \log 7 \approx 1.95$ nats.
\end{remark}

The entropy rate $S_{\rm CMCA}(\beta)$ provides a thermodynamic window into the
information-theoretic complexity of the CMCA dynamics: it counts the nats of new
information generated per time step when the system evolves at thermal coupling $\beta$.

\subsection{Proposed Platform}

A minimal realization of the spin-7 model requires atoms with at least 7 addressable
internal states. The most natural platform is a Rydberg atom array in which 7 hyperfine or Rydberg
sublevels are used as the $\mathbb{Z}_7$ spin alphabet.

\noindent\textbf{Requirements:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{7-level atoms:} Atoms with nuclear spin $I = 3$ (e.g., $^{87}$Rb with

  \item \emph{Three-body interactions:} The Hamiltonian $H = \sum p(s_{i-1},s_i,s_{i+1})$

    interactions by driving controlled three-site gates.
  \item \emph{Minimum system size:} $L \geq 12$ sites in each direction (from the

\subsection{Observable Predictions}

The following predictions are directly testable (\CatA{}, from Section~\ref{sec:2D}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Critical temperature:} $T_c \approx 2.86\,J$, where $J$ is the
    overall energy scale of the three-body interaction.
  \item \emph{Order parameter:} The fraction of sites in states $\{0,1,5\}$ should
    jump from $\approx 3/7$ (disordered) to $\approx 1$ (ordered) as temperature
    is lowered through $T_c$.
  \item \emph{Three-fold symmetry breaking:} Below $T_c$, the system should
    select one of three ordered phases, with domain walls between regions of
    different ground-state values.
  \item \emph{Domain-wall freezing:} Above $\beta_{c,2} J \approx 1.7$, domain
    walls between ground-state regions should become kinetically frozen, observable

  \item \emph{Correlation length:} $\xi \approx 7.77$ sites near $\beta \approx 2$

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Engineering the Interaction}

The interaction energy $p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR \pmod 7$ can be decomposed as:
\begin{align*}
  p(L,C,R) &= \underbrace{C + R}_{\text{2-site terms}} - \underbrace{CR}_{\text{2-site}} - \underbrace{LCR}_{\text{3-site}}.
\end{align*}
The 2-site terms $C + R - CR$ implement a modified XY-type coupling; the 3-site
term $-LCR$ requires a three-body gate. In Rydberg arrays, three-body interactions
can arise from multi-photon processes or be engineered via ancilla-mediated
two-qubit gates applied in sequence. Implementing $p \pmod 7$ exactly requires
modular arithmetic on the atomic state, which maps naturally to a $\mathbb{Z}_7$
Potts clock model extended to three-body couplings.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{sec:discussion}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{2D Spin Model and 1D CMCA Connection}

The correspondence between the 2D spin-7 model and the single-tape CMCA is exact,
not approximate. The partition function of the 2D system with only horizontal interactions
on an $L_x \times L_y$ torus is $Z = \mathrm{Tr}(\mathbf{T}^{L_y})$, where $\mathbf{T}$
maps one row's spin configuration to the next. This is mathematically identical to
$L_y$ steps of the single-tape CMCA spacetime evolution: one row equals one time step.
The 2D spin-7 model is therefore a statistical mechanics embedding of the one-tape
$\GF{7}$ CA system studied in~\cite{SpivackCMCA}. The full 3+1-dimensional system
(three tapes plus shared clock~\cite{SpivackThreeTapeCMCA}) would require a 4D spin
model or tensor network construction; that extension is not studied here.

\subsection{Physical Applications}

\noindent\textbf{Quantum simulation.}
Cold-atom platforms using Rydberg atoms with seven resolvable hyperfine states can
implement the spin-7 Hamiltonian in optical lattice arrays. The phase transition at
$T_c \approx 2.86\,J$ is directly measurable via specific heat, correlation length, and
order parameter. The critical exponents $\nu = 5/6$, $\eta = 4/15$ (3-state Potts class)
provide a sharp, parameter-free experimental target (\CatAD{}).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Analog computation.}
Below $T_c$, the system self-organizes into one of three ordered phases corresponding
to ground states $\{0, 1, 5\}$. These are the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ color sectors of the GTE
framework, so the ordered phase physically demonstrates color-charge selection without
a particle accelerator. Whether the correspondence can be made precise at the
$\PHIMDL{}$ level remains open (\CatB{}).

Several analog computing applications follow from the model's structure.

\noindent\emph{Ternary memory.}
The three distinct ordered phases provide a natural \emph{ternary} ($\mathbb{Z}_3$) memory: each
spatial domain stabilized in one of the three ground states $\{0, 1, 5\}$ stores one trit.
With individual-site control in a Rydberg array, ternary logic gates and registers could
be implemented directly in the material without digital encoding.

\noindent\emph{Thermally assisted Rule~110 computation.}
The transfer matrix identity (Section~\ref{sec:cmca}) means that running the 2D spin-7
system at finite temperature below $T_c$ is equivalent to executing the CMCA cellular
automaton with thermal fluctuations as a noise source. Since the CMCA contains Rule~110 as
its binary sublayer, and Rule~110 is computationally
universal~\cite{Cook2004,SpivackCompUniversality}, the spin-7 lattice in its ordered phase
constitutes a physically realised substrate for universal computation.
Whether this universality persists across the full $\mathbb{Z}_7$ dynamics in the thermal
phase is open (Section~\ref{sec:open}).

\noindent\emph{Constraint satisfaction and optimisation.}
The three-body interaction geometry --- each spin interacts with its left and right
neighbours via $p(L,C,R)$ --- maps naturally to constraint satisfaction problems
whose clauses involve overlapping variable triplets. Near $T_c$ the system can
anneal towards low-energy configurations, providing an analog solver for
$\mathbb{Z}_7$-valued triplet-constraint problems. Certain classes of error-correcting
codes and cryptographic functions over $\GF{7}$ have this structure.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Universality class and falsifiable prediction.}
The three-fold degenerate ordered phase places the 2D spin-7 model in the universality
class of the 3-state Potts model ($\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry group). The critical exponents
$\nu = 5/6$ and $\eta = 4/15$ are analytically exact (\CatAD{}); they are therefore
falsifiable experimental predictions. The specific heat anomaly scales as
$C_V \sim \log L$ for continuous transitions in this class, consistent with the
weakly divergent FSS data in Table~\ref{tab:cv}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Complexity theory.}
The single-tape CA with the GTE rule is computationally universal (Rule~110 binary
restriction, proved in~\cite{Cook2004, SpivackCompUniversality}). The thermal version
raises the open question of whether computational universality persists in the thermal
phase or is destroyed by thermal noise above $T_c$.

\subsection{NL Connection to the Higgs Sector}

The Walsh nonlinearity $\mathrm{NL}(p) = 1 - c_H/7^2$ (\CatAD{}) shows that
the nonlinearity of the spin interaction is determined by $c_H = 13$, the same
constant that governs $\sin^2\theta_W = N_{\rm gen}/c_H = 3/13$. Both $c_H$ and
the NL formula evaluate to $13 = 4N_{\rm gen}+1$ because $q = 7$ is the
MDL-selected field: $c_H = (N_{\rm gen}^3-1)/2$ from the GTE Higgs
count, and $2q-1 = 2(2N_{\rm gen}+1)-1 = 4N_{\rm gen}+1$ from the safe-prime
structure at $N_{\rm gen} = 3$~\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialWolfram}. This is a
structural connection between the critical properties of the condensed matter model
and the electroweak sector of the Standard Model.

\subsection{Summary}

We have introduced and analyzed the spin-7 lattice model defined by the GTE polynomial
$p(L,C,R) = C+R-CR-LCR \pmod 7$ as an interaction energy. The key results are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{Algebraic ground states (\CatAL{}):} The global ground states are exactly
    the three uniform configurations $\{0, 1, 5\}$, the roots of $p(x,x,x) = -x(x-1)(x-5)
    \pmod 7$, machine-certified as \lean{poly\_p\_uniform\_gs\_roots} (zero sorry,

  \item \emph{2D phase transition (\CatA{}):} The 2D model has a genuine finite-temperature
    phase transition at $\beta_c \approx 0.35$ ($T_c \approx 2.86\,J$) with $\mathbb{Z}_3$-like
    symmetry breaking.
  \item \emph{CMCA entropy rate (\CatA{}):} The 1D transfer matrix exactly encodes one

  \item \emph{GTE connections (\CatB{}):} The ground-state sectors $\{0,1,5\}$ map to

  \item \emph{Universality class (\CatAD{}):} The 3-state Potts universality class gives

  \item \emph{Cold-atom realization:} The model is realizable with 7-level Rydberg atoms

\end{enumerate}

The spin-7 model provides a bridge between the abstract algebraic structure of the

\section{Open Questions}
\label{sec:open}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{openprob}[Universality Class of the Primary Transition]
The FSS data show a $C_V$ peak magnitude that is approximately flat in $L$ (range
$1.52$--$1.87$ across $L = 4$--$16$), consistent with a logarithmic divergence
(as in the 2D 3-state Potts model, second-order) or with a weak first-order
transition. Definitive classification requires Binder cumulant $U_L = 1 -
\langle M^4 \rangle / (3 \langle M^2 \rangle^2)$ analysis: for a continuous
transition, $U_L$ should converge to a universal value at $\beta_c$; for a
first-order transition, $U_L$ should develop a dip. This requires larger system
sizes ($L \geq 24$) and longer runs.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[High-$\beta$ Glass vs.\ Thermodynamic Transition]
The secondary feature at $\beta_{c,2} \approx 1.7$ is characterized by strong
hysteresis that grows with $\beta$ and shifts to larger $\beta$ for larger $L$.
This is consistent with dynamical arrest rather than a thermodynamic transition,
but requires confirmation via parallel tempering or replica-exchange Monte Carlo
to achieve equilibration. If it is a true thermodynamic transition, it would
represent a rare example of a model with two successive finite-temperature transitions.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[Lean Certification of CMCA Entropy Rate]
The entropy rate theorem $S_{\rm CMCA} = \log \lambda_1$ requires formalizing the
transfer matrix construction and its spectral properties in Lean~4. The partition
function identity $Z = \mathrm{Tr}(\mathbf{T}^N)$ is straightforward to formalize;
the spectral gap argument requires Perron--Frobenius theory, which is available in
Mathlib. This would upgrade the entropy rate from \CatA{} to \CatAL{}.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[3D Phase Diagram and Quantum Version]
The 3D version of the spin-7 model (adding interactions in the $z$-direction)
may exhibit a richer phase diagram with additional ordered phases or a quantum
critical point. The quantum version (replacing the classical $\mathbb{Z}_7$ spins
with quantum $\mathbb{Z}_7$ clock operators) would be directly relevant to Rydberg
quantum simulation.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}[Period-475 and the 1D Attractor]
Under the deterministic (zero-temperature) dynamics of $p$ on the 5-cell ring,
the period-475 attractor (\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialWolfram}) is the dominant
feature; the periodicity and minimality are machine-certified in Lean~4 as
\lean{period\_475\_returns} and \lean{period\_475\_is\_minimal} (\CatAL{}, zero
sorry, \texttt{PolyExplorations.lean}, ugp-lean). The statistical mechanics
framework introduced here raises the question of how this attractor structure
manifests in the finite-temperature transfer matrix spectrum. The eigenvalue
$\lambda_7 \approx -0.669$ may be related to the period-2 component of
period~$475 = 5^2 \times 19$.
\end{openprob}
