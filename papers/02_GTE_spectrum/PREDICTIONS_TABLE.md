## Scientific Notes

### n-value Degeneracy
Three starred predictions share n-values with known SM particles:
- **GTE-P4** (n=42) — same n as muon; mass 137 MeV vs 105.7 MeV
- **GTE-P5** (n=73) — same n as electron; mass 21 MeV vs 0.511 MeV
- **GTE-P9** (n=275) — same n as charm and tau; mass 561 MeV vs 1270/1777 MeV

This trajectory-path multiplicity is a structural feature of the GTE arithmetic: the same triple (a,b,c) can be reached by multiple distinct GTE evolution paths, each producing a different mass. The canonical SM assignment selects the path closest to the PDG reference; additional paths produce novel predictions at the same coordinates.

### Cross-check vs Paper 01 SM masses

All 9 predictions were verified to not collide with any Paper 01 UCL-derived SM mass.

**One flag:** GTE-P2 (107.4 MeV) is **1.6% above the muon** (105.7 MeV). This is not a problem but requires explicit framing in the paper: GTE-P2 has a different GTE triple (n=4702 vs muon n=42), a completely different n-value, and a mass difference of 1.75 MeV — well outside experimental muon mass uncertainty. The paper must state: *"a novel state at 107.4 MeV, distinct from the muon at 105.7 MeV."*

All other predictions are >14% from the nearest SM mass — no framing concern.

### Stability
All candidates listed here have stability_score = 1.0 and predicted lifetime = 10³⁰ s (effectively stable on any experimental timescale). This makes them potentially detectable via standard collider searches rather than requiring decay-product signatures.

### Falsifiability window
Each prediction can be falsified or confirmed by:
1. Absence/presence of a new stable or long-lived charged particle at the predicted mass
2. No known SM particle occupies any of these mass windows (verified against PDG 2024)
3. The trajectory-path multiplicity (P4, P5, P9) make a specific structural claim about the GTE lattice that can be tested by finding (or failing to find) the predicted partner
