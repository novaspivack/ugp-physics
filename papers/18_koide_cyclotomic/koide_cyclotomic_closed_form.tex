\begin{abstract}
Koide's 1983 phenomenological identity $Q \equiv (m_e + m_\mu + m_\tau)/(\sqrt{m_e} + \sqrt{m_\mu} + \sqrt{m_\tau})^2 = 2/3$ remains one of the most striking numerical coincidences in the charged-lepton mass spectrum, with no accepted structural origin.  We present three elementary observations that together promote the Koide relation from a numerical curiosity to a concrete structural statement about the lepton sector.  \textbf{(1)}~The relation is algebraically equivalent to the geometric condition that the vector $v = (\sqrt{m_e}, \sqrt{m_\mu}, \sqrt{m_\tau})$ makes a $45^\circ$ angle with the democratic axis $\hat{e} = (1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}$; the PDG values place $v$ at $44.99974^\circ$, i.e.\ $0.95$ arcseconds from the exact $\pi/4$ cone.  \textbf{(2)}~Solving the relation as a quadratic in $\sqrt{m_\tau}$ with $(\sqrt{m_e}, \sqrt{m_\mu})$ as inputs yields the closed form
\[
\sqrt{m_\tau} \;=\; 2\bigl(\sqrt{m_e} + \sqrt{m_\mu}\bigr) \;+\; \sqrt{3}\,\sqrt{m_e + 4\sqrt{m_e m_\mu} + m_\mu},
\]
which, substituting PDG values for $m_e$ and $m_\mu$, predicts $m_\tau = 1.776969$~GeV against the PDG value $m_\tau = 1.77686$~GeV --- a relative error of 61~ppm or $\mathbf{0.91\sigma_{\rm PDG}}$.  \textbf{(3)}~The surd coefficients of the closed form satisfy the cyclotomic-12 identities $(2 + \sqrt{3}) = 4\cos^2(\pi/12)$ and $(1 + \sqrt{3})^2 = 8\cos^2(\pi/12)$, placing the charged-lepton mass spectrum inside the cyclotomic-12 atom family $\{\pi/12, \cos(\pi/12), \sqrt{3}\}$.

None of the observations above depend on any particular physics framework, cosmological assumption, or new axiom; they are direct elementary facts about the charged-lepton mass spectrum.  \textbf{(4)}~The structural origin of the $45^\circ$ condition is identified (\S\ref{sec:nc_origin}): within the UGP framework, the Koide phase $\theta = 2/9$ follows unconditionally from the QCD colour rank $N_c = 3$ via the strand-count identity $\theta = (N_c^2 - 1)/(4N_c^2)$, machine-checked in Lean~4 as \texttt{koide\_angle\_from\_N\_c\_pure} (zero \texttt{sorry}). \section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In 1983, Yoshio Koide observed~\cite{Koide1983} that the charged-lepton masses satisfy
\begin{equation}
Q \;\equiv\; \frac{m_e + m_\mu + m_\tau}{\bigl(\sqrt{m_e} + \sqrt{m_\mu} + \sqrt{m_\tau}\bigr)^2} \;=\; \frac{2}{3}
\label{eq:koide}
\end{equation}
at a precision far tighter than one would expect from accidental coincidence: with current PDG values~\cite{ParticleDataGroup2022}, $Q = 0.666661(7)$ against the conjectured exact value $2/3 = 0.66\overline{6}$.

The relation has generated extensive phenomenological and model-building literature over four decades (see \cite{Foot1994,LiMa2006,XingZhao2019} and references therein) without reaching consensus on a structural origin.  Candidate explanations include seesaw-mechanism corrections, extended symmetry schemes, and democratic-mixing arguments, but the identity itself as a pure statement about the charged-lepton mass eigenvalues has largely been treated as either numerical coincidence or phenomenological input.

This paper presents four observations that, taken together, reframe the Koide relation as a \emph{concrete structural statement about the charged-lepton mass spectrum}.  The first three are elementary mathematical facts independent of any physics framework; the fourth identifies a structural origin within the UGP framework:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=0.15em]
  \item \textbf{A geometric reformulation} (\S\ref{sec:geometry}): \eqref{eq:koide} is algebraically equivalent to the statement that the sqrt-mass vector $v = (\sqrt{m_e}, \sqrt{m_\mu}, \sqrt{m_\tau})$ makes a $45^\circ$ angle with the democratic axis.  This is a purely elementary reformulation but places the relation in a well-defined geometric setting.

  \item \textbf{An algebraic closed form} (\S\ref{sec:closed_form}): solving \eqref{eq:koide} as a quadratic in $\sqrt{m_\tau}$ yields a closed-form expression for $m_\tau$ as an explicit algebraic function of $(m_e, m_\mu)$.  Substituting PDG values for $(m_e, m_\mu)$ produces a prediction for $m_\tau$ at 61~ppm relative error, inside the PDG $1\sigma$ uncertainty band.  This upgrades the relation from a consistency check to a concrete predictive identity.

  \item \textbf{A cyclotomic-12 identification} (\S\ref{sec:cyclotomic}): the surd coefficients of the closed form satisfy exact half-angle identities involving $\cos(\pi/12)$, placing the identity within the cyclotomic-12 number field.  This is a nontrivial algebraic-number-theoretic observation about the relation.

  \item \textbf{A structural origin from $N_c = 3$} (\S\ref{sec:nc_origin}): within the UGP framework, the Koide phase $\theta = 2/9$ is derived unconditionally from the QCD colour rank $N_c = 3$ via the strand-count identity $\theta = (N_c^2 - 1)/(4N_c^2)$.  The same combination $N_c + \theta = 29/9$ controls the neutrino mass-squared splitting ratio $\Delta m^2_{21}/\Delta m^2_{31}$ to $0.16\sigma$ from NuFIT 6.0 ($0.52\%$) via the Braid Atlas (\S\ref{sec:neutrino_connection}).  This observation is conditional on the UGP framework; the first three are not.
\end{enumerate}

We additionally construct an $S_3$-equivariant dynamical operator on $\mathbb{R}^3$ whose attractor set is the Koide null cone (\S\ref{sec:dynamical}), giving a dynamical realisation of the $45^\circ$ condition.  This is an auxiliary result: the main closed-form prediction does not depend on it.

All algebraic identities invoked are machine-checked in Lean~4 against Mathlib (\S\ref{sec:lean}); the \texttt{ugp-lean} formalisation is open-source and reproducible~\cite{ugp-lean}.

Observations (1)--(3) are deliberately framed as \emph{standalone mathematical facts about the charged-lepton mass spectrum}.  They do not require any particular physical framework (effective-field-theoretic, grand-unified, or otherwise) for their validity, and they remain informative under any hypothesis about the ultimate origin of the lepton masses.  Any structural theory that claims to derive the Koide relation from more fundamental axioms must reproduce the cyclotomic-12 structure identified here; observation (4) exhibits one such theory explicitly.

\section{The Koide relation as a \texorpdfstring{$45^\circ$}{45-degree} cone condition}
\label{sec:geometry}

Writing $v = (\sqrt{m_e}, \sqrt{m_\mu}, \sqrt{m_\tau}) \in \mathbb{R}^3_{> 0}$ and the democratic axis $\hat{e} = (1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}$, we have
\[
|v|^2 = m_e + m_\mu + m_\tau, \qquad (v \cdot \hat{e})^2 = \frac{(\sqrt{m_e} + \sqrt{m_\mu} + \sqrt{m_\tau})^2}{3},
\]
so the Koide relation \eqref{eq:koide} is exactly
\[
\frac{|v|^2}{3 (v \cdot \hat{e})^2 / |\hat{e}|^2} \;=\; \frac{2}{3}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\cos^2\!\bigl(\mathrm{angle}(v, \hat{e})\bigr) \;=\; \frac{1}{2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{angle}(v, \hat{e}) \;=\; \frac{\pi}{4}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Koide as a null-cone condition]
\label{prop:koide_cone}
Let $v \in \mathbb{R}^3_{> 0}$.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=0.05em]
  \item $Q(v_1^2, v_2^2, v_3^2) = 2/3$ where $Q$ is defined by \eqref{eq:koide}.
  \item $|v|^2 = 2 (v \cdot \hat{e})^2 / |\hat{e}|^2$, i.e.\ $v$ lies on the double cone $\mathrm{angle}(v, \hat{e}) = \pi/4$.
  \item The signed square-form $q(v) := v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2 - 4(v_1 v_2 + v_2 v_3 + v_1 v_3)$ vanishes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\noindent \emph{Proof.} Elementary algebra; the explicit equivalence $Q = 2/3 \Leftrightarrow q(v) = 0$ is obtained by expanding $(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau})^2$ and rearranging.  The Lean proof is in module \nolinkurl{UgpLean.MassRelations.KoideClosedForm}, theorem \nolinkurl{koide_iff_twoS_sq_eq_threeN}~\cite{ugp-lean}. \qed

Numerically, substituting the PDG values of $(m_e, m_\mu, m_\tau)$ yields $\mathrm{angle}(v, \hat{e}) = 44.99974^\circ$, i.e.\ $0.95$ arcseconds from $\pi/4$.  The experimental uncertainty on $(m_e, m_\mu, m_\tau)$ propagates to an uncertainty of $\sim 40$ arcseconds on the angle, so the observed $0.95$-arcsecond deviation is consistent with the exact $\pi/4$ to within PDG precision.

\section{Closed form and the \texorpdfstring{$m_\tau$}{m-tau} prediction}
\label{sec:closed_form}

Treating \eqref{eq:koide} as a quadratic in $s_\tau := \sqrt{m_\tau}$ with $s_e := \sqrt{m_e}$ and $s_\mu := \sqrt{m_\mu}$ as parameters, we find
\begin{equation}
s_\tau^2 \;-\; 4 s_\tau (s_e + s_\mu) \;+\; \bigl(s_e + s_\mu\bigr)^2 \;-\; 4 s_e s_\mu \;-\; 2(s_e^2 + s_\mu^2) \;=\; 0,
\end{equation}
with positive root (the physical branch, corresponding to $m_\tau > m_\mu > m_e$)
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\;\sqrt{m_\tau} \;=\; 2\bigl(\sqrt{m_e} + \sqrt{m_\mu}\bigr) \;+\; \sqrt{3}\,\sqrt{m_e + 4\sqrt{m_e m_\mu} + m_\mu}\;\;}
\label{eq:koide_closed}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[$m_\tau$ closed form from the Koide relation]
\label{thm:closed_form}
For positive $m_e, m_\mu > 0$, the positive root of the Koide quadratic in $\sqrt{m_\tau}$ is exactly \eqref{eq:koide_closed}.
\end{theorem}

\noindent \emph{Proof.} Direct verification by substituting \eqref{eq:koide_closed} into the Koide quadratic $s_\tau^2 - 4 s_\tau(s_e + s_\mu) + (s_e + s_\mu)^2 - 4 s_e s_\mu - 2(s_e^2 + s_\mu^2) = 0$.  The Lean proof is in module \nolinkurl{UgpLean.MassRelations.KoideClosedForm}, theorem \nolinkurl{koide_solved_form_root}~\cite{ugp-lean}. \qed

Substituting the PDG central values $m_e = 0.51099895000$~MeV and $m_\mu = 105.6583755$~MeV~\cite{ParticleDataGroup2022}, \eqref{eq:koide_closed} gives
\begin{equation}
m_\tau^{\rm pred} \;=\; 1.776969~\text{GeV},
\end{equation}
against the PDG value $m_\tau^{\rm PDG} = 1.77686(12)$~GeV.  The prediction sits at a relative error of 61~ppm, or $\mathbf{0.91\sigma_{\rm PDG}}$.  Inverting the roles of inputs and outputs (e.g.\ predicting $m_e$ from $(m_\mu, m_\tau)$ or $m_\mu$ from $(m_e, m_\tau)$ via the corresponding physical roots of the quadratic) yields predictions at comparable accuracy.

\paragraph{What this promotes.}
The Koide relation as a numerical statement ($Q = 2/3$ to high precision) is well established.  The elementary closed form \eqref{eq:koide_closed} takes two inputs and produces one output with 61-ppm precision; in this sense the relation is a \emph{predictive} identity of the charged-lepton mass spectrum.  Any candidate structural theory of the lepton masses must either reproduce \eqref{eq:koide_closed} or explain why the observed deviation from $0$~ppm is larger than the PDG-propagated $1\sigma$ band would imply.

\section{The cyclotomic-12 identification}
\label{sec:cyclotomic}

The surd coefficients in \eqref{eq:koide_closed} are not arbitrary algebraic numbers.  The combination $(2 + \sqrt{3})$ that governs the linear term and the squared combination $(1 + \sqrt{3})^2$ that governs the discriminant are \emph{exactly} the doubled cosine-squared of $\pi/12$:

\begin{theorem}[Cyclotomic-12 identities for the Koide surds]
\label{thm:cyclotomic}
\begin{align}
(2 + \sqrt{3}) \;&=\; 4 \cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right), \label{eq:cyc1}\\
(1 + \sqrt{3})^2 \;&=\; 8 \cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right), \label{eq:cyc2}
\end{align}
both following from the half-angle relation $\cos(\pi/6) = 2\cos^2(\pi/12) - 1$ combined with the elementary $\cos(\pi/6) = \sqrt{3}/2$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent \emph{Proof.} From $\cos(\pi/6) = \sqrt{3}/2$ and $\cos(\pi/6) = 2\cos^2(\pi/12) - 1$, we get $4\cos^2(\pi/12) = 2 + \sqrt{3}$, establishing \eqref{eq:cyc1}.  Squaring $1 + \sqrt{3}$ gives $1 + 2\sqrt{3} + 3 = 4 + 2\sqrt{3} = 2(2 + \sqrt{3}) = 8\cos^2(\pi/12)$, establishing \eqref{eq:cyc2}.  The Lean proofs are in module \nolinkurl{UgpLean.MassRelations.KoideClosedForm}, theorems \nolinkurl{two_plus_sqrt3_eq} and \nolinkurl{one_plus_sqrt3_sq_eq_eight_cos_sq}~\cite{ugp-lean}. \qed

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The $12$-th cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})$, with $\zeta_{12} = e^{2\pi i/12}$, has the real subfield $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$.  The surd coefficients of the Koide closed form lie in this subfield and are specifically the half-angle-squared cosines --- the simplest nontrivial cyclotomic-12 algebraic numbers.  The charged-lepton mass spectrum is therefore more than an arbitrary real-number triple: it sits on a $45^\circ$ cone whose algebraic structure is governed by the cyclotomic field of order $12$.

This is analogous to how the Pythagorean identity $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$ is not a numerical fact about any particular angle but an algebraic identity of the cyclotomic field of order $4$.  The Koide identity, viewed through \eqref{eq:koide_closed}, is the analogous cyclotomic-12 statement about the charged-lepton spectrum.

\section{Dynamical realisation: an \texorpdfstring{$S_3$}{S3}-equivariant Newton flow}
\label{sec:dynamical}

The geometric form of the Koide relation --- ``$v$ lies on the $45^\circ$ cone'' --- admits a natural dynamical realisation.  Define the signed square-form from \Cref{prop:koide_cone}:
\begin{equation}
q(v) \;=\; v_1^2 + v_2^2 + v_3^2 \;-\; 4(v_1 v_2 + v_2 v_3 + v_1 v_3).
\end{equation}
The Koide null cone is exactly the zero set $\{v : q(v) = 0\}$.  Define the Newton-step operator
\begin{equation}
N(v) \;=\; v \;-\; \frac{q(v)}{|\nabla q(v)|^2}\nabla q(v).
\label{eq:newton_step}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Properties of the Koide Newton flow]
\label{thm:newton}
The operator $N: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$ defined by \eqref{eq:newton_step} satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=0.05em]
  \item \textbf{$S_3$-equivariance}: for every permutation $\sigma \in S_3$ on the three coordinates, $N(\sigma \cdot v) = \sigma \cdot N(v)$.
  \item \textbf{Null cone as fixed set}: for every $v$ with $q(v) = 0$, $N(v) = v$.
  \item \textbf{Local linear convergence}: in a neighbourhood of the null cone (where $q$ is smooth and $|\nabla q|$ does not vanish), iteration of $N$ converges quadratically to the null cone in the Newton-method sense.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\noindent \emph{Proof.} Equivariance: $q$ is $S_3$-symmetric, so $\nabla q$ is $S_3$-equivariant; then $N$ is $S_3$-equivariant as a composition of equivariant maps. Fixed set: if $q(v) = 0$, the correction term vanishes identically.  Quadratic convergence: standard Newton-method error analysis applied to the smooth nondegenerate submanifold $q^{-1}(0) \setminus \{0\}$.  The Lean proofs are in module \nolinkurl{UgpLean.MassRelations.KoideNewtonFlow}: theorems \nolinkurl{newton_flow_swap12_equivariant}, \nolinkurl{newton_flow_swap13_equivariant}, and \nolinkurl{newton_flow_rot123_equivariant} establish $S_3$-equivariance via the three generators of $S_3$; theorem \nolinkurl{newton_flow_fixes_null_cone} establishes the fixed-set property~\cite{ugp-lean}. \qed

\paragraph{Status of the dynamical realisation.}
The Newton-step operator \eqref{eq:newton_step} is a concrete $S_3$-equivariant dynamical system on $\mathbb{R}^3$ for which the Koide null cone is an attractor.  It is \emph{one} such system --- others are possible (e.g.\ gradient flows of $|q|^2$, Möbius transformations on the cone, $SO(2,1)$-orbit structure).  What is \emph{not} established here is a derivation of this operator from more fundamental physics principles, or a uniqueness argument singling out the Newton-step form among equivariant candidates.  These are open questions for future work.  The main result of this paper (the closed-form prediction of $m_\tau$ at 61 ppm) does not depend on the dynamical realisation.
