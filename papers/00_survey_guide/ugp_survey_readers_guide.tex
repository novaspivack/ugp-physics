\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Reflexive Reality Programme}

The \textbf{Reflexive Reality} research programme is a sustained formal investigation
into what a perfectly self-contained universe must look like --- one whose laws,
constants, and structure arise entirely from within, with nothing externally imposed.
Its scope spans physics, chemistry, biology, evolutionary systems, computational
systems, cognitive systems, and AI.

The Reflexive Reality programme comprises two branches.  \textbf{NEMS} (No External
Model Selection) formalizes the ``no outside'' constraint and machine-checks its
theorem-grade consequences in Lean~4 with zero \texttt{sorry}~\cite{SpivackNEMSHub}.
The \textbf{UGP Physics programme} is the quantitative branch of Reflexive Reality: it focuses on the
mechanics and dynamics of reflexive systems, deriving the specific generative
structures that produce the Standard Model parameter spectrum and beyond.  The UGP
programme inherits from NEMS, adopting Perfect Self-Containment (PSC) self-consistency and the NEMS closure
axioms, and is documented in the present corpus of papers.

The central NEMS results include:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.15em]
  \item The Standard Model gauge group $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ is \emph{forced} by Layer~I
  consistency constraints; three generations are \emph{selected} by a minimality principle in Layer~II (Two-Layer PSC Theorem)~\cite{SpivackNEMS05}.
  \item The Born rule is the unique closure fixed point of the probability assignment problem, not a postulate~\cite{SpivackNEMS88}.
  \item The arrow of time is a structural consequence of stable physical records, preceding thermodynamic arguments~\cite{SpivackNEMS88}.
  \item Black hole information, holographic encoding, and FTL non-signalling are each constrained by the same diagonal machinery~\cite{SpivackNEMS88}.
\end{itemize}

The full NEMS programme comprises 94 papers (P00--P93) archived on Zenodo; both programmes are catalogued at
\url{https://www.novaspivack.com/research}~\cite{SpivackResearchProgrammes} and described for physics audiences
in NEMS Paper~07~\cite{SpivackNEMS07} and in the flagship survey~\cite{SpivackNEMS88}.

\section{The Epistemic Taxonomy}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

Every quantitative claim across the programme is classified into one of five
statuses.  Understanding this taxonomy is prerequisite to reading any paper.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=gray!4,colframe=gray!50!black,
  title={\textbf{Five-status claim taxonomy (used throughout the programme)}},
  fonttitle=\bfseries,left=6pt,right=6pt,top=5pt,bottom=5pt]
\small
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.3em]
  \item[\textbf{A}$_{\rm Lean}$ (plain \textbf{A}):] Lean~4 machine-checked arithmetic
    or structural identity.  Zero \texttt{sorry}.  Standard Mathlib axiom signature.
    The programme's strongest claim type.
  \item[\textbf{A}$_{\rm MDL}$:] MDL-uniqueness certificate within a declared
    UGP-structural expression class.  Not Lean-derived from invariants.  Example:
    the Higgs quartic $\lambda = \varphi/(4\pi)$ is the unique MDL\,$=4$ expression
    in the declared atom basis.
  \item[\textbf{A/D}:] A physics bridge, external scale, or partial derivation
    remains.  The arithmetic component may be Lean-certified but the full physical
    identification is not.  Example: the $0.295\%$ charged-fermion mass spectrum
    uses the URC algebraic correction layer (A/D) on top of the A$_{\rm Lean}$
    structural pipeline.
  \item[\textbf{B}:] Calibrated to data, or computationally/empirically supported.
    Example: baryon masses (15 binding parameters, QCD confinement placeholder).
  \item[\textbf{D}:] Speculative or frontier.  Example: the GTE-P7 mirror-branch
    dark-matter candidate.
\end{description}
\end{tcolorbox}

9 genuinely novel predicted stable particles (GTE-P1, P2, P3, P6--P11) with specific
predicted masses (3~MeV to 1.9~GeV), quantum numbers ($Q$, colour, spin),
stability scores, and viability tiers (plus GTE-P4/P5 path variants excluded from
the novel register).  All 9 novel mass windows are currently open
under combined laboratory dark-photon constraints.
\emph{Repository:} \texttt{papers/02\_GTE\_spectrum/candidates.csv} (SHA256 in \textsc{reproduce.md}).
\emph{Corpus register:} Master Index Table~\ref{tab:mi-novel-particles}.
\emph{Lookup API:} \texttt{tools/gte\_stability\_api.py -{}-mass [MeV]}.
\emph{Primary use:} experimentalists performing model-independent bump-hunt searches
at collider or fixed-target experiments.
\emph{Utility note:} Most beyond-SM frameworks supply qualitative mass ranges or
order-of-magnitude predictions for new particles.
This database provides specific predicted masses, decay lifetimes, and ranked confidence
scores for every GTE candidate, enabling a direct query-driven workflow: given a candidate
signal at mass $m$, a researcher can determine in seconds whether a GTE-predicted particle
lies within the search window and at what confidence tier.
The database is pre-registered (committed before any collider comparison), making it
falsifiable against current and future collider datasets without post-hoc tuning.

\noindent\textbf{2.\ Nuclear Binding Energy and Stability API (P03).}
Two complementary analytical models are provided:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item \textbf{Parsimonious (6-term):} GTE coordinate features only.
  $0.032\,\mathrm{MeV/A}$ five-fold CV MAE on 1,319 curated nuclei;
  interpretable closed form; no external dependencies.
\item \textbf{Extended (9-term):} same 6 features plus proton magic-number
  proximity, neutron magic-number proximity, and pairing delta
  ($\delta \cdot A^{-3/4}$).
  Trained on 2,548 experimental AME2020 nuclei (full measured table).
  $0.051\,\mathrm{MeV/A}$ five-fold CV MAE across all experimental AME2020
  nuclei; $\mathrm{OOD}\ R^2 = 0.977$ on 1,229 AME2020 nuclei absent from the
  original training set.
  Note: the extended model's CV MAE is higher than the parsimonious figure
  because the Phase~2 training set includes hard exotic nuclei near the drip
  lines; on the original 1,319-nucleus set the extended model matches or
  improves on the parsimonious at every point.
\end{itemize}

The 10-term stability classifier ($94.5\%$ five-fold CV against corrected NUBASE2020
empirical labels) is the recommended tool for stability screening. Note: the smooth
analytical stability law cannot resolve elements with no stable isotopes (Tc, Pm)
due to a fundamental smooth-law limitation; empirical NUBASE2020 data should be
used for known elements~($Z\leq 118$), with the GTE classifier reserved for
extrapolation to unknown elements ($Z>118$).

\emph{Access:}
\texttt{tools/nuclear\_be\_api.py -{}-Z [Z] -{}-N [N] -{}-task be|stability|both}\\
\texttt{\phantom{tools/nuclear\_be\_api.py }{}-{}-model parsimonious|extended|both} (JSON output).

\emph{Caveat:} Both analytical models cover $Z \leq 118$;
predictions for unmeasured superheavy nuclei ($Z > 118$) are Category~D (exploratory).
Use the parsimonious model for structural interpretation and the extended model
for accuracy-sensitive screening across the measured nuclear chart.

\emph{Utility note:} Dedicated DFT and HFB approaches outperform both laws on the full
AME dataset and should be preferred for precision work on known nuclei.
The value of these laws is their analytical, dependency-free form for fast screening
of unmeasured isotopes, and the GTE-derived feature motivation as a reproducible
benchmark for nuclear ML researchers evaluating alternative theoretical feature sets.

\noindent\textbf{3.\ Koide Lepton Mass Predictor (P18).}
A Lean~4-certified closed-form formula that predicts $m_\tau$ from $m_e$ and
$m_\mu$ alone.  With PDG inputs, the prediction $m_\tau = 1776.969\,\mathrm{MeV}$
is within 61~ppm ($0.91\sigma$) of the measured value; a
${\leq}\,30\,\mathrm{keV}$ measurement would provide a $3.6\sigma$ test.  The structural origin,
$\theta = 2/9 = (N_c^2{-}1)/(4N_c^2)$ from $N_c=3$, is machine-checked
(\texttt{koide\_angle\_from\_N\_c\_pure}, zero~\texttt{sorry}).
\emph{Access:} formula in P18; trivial Python implementation ($<$10 lines).
\emph{Primary use:} precision lepton mass measurements at Belle~II.
\emph{Utility note:} The empirical Koide relation has been known since 1982, but
has lacked a structural derivation.
P18 supplies one from first principles ($N_c = 3$ uniqueness), machine-checked in
Lean~4, converting an observed numerical coincidence into a falsifiable structural
prediction.
For a researcher testing lepton universality or planning a Belle~II $m_\tau$
measurement campaign, the formula provides a specific theoretical target
($m_\tau = 1776.969\,\mathrm{MeV}$) with a known derivation and machine-verified proof,
rather than a post-hoc fit.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4.\ Genetic Code Sieve (P25).}
Evaluates any wobble-admissible codon-to-amino-acid mapping against eight
independent biological viability criteria (including the generative-to-dissipative
IPT criterion).  No existing public tool implements the full eight-criterion
combination.  The standard genetic code is the unique survivor of the sieve
($z = +3.22\sigma$; 0/99,998 random wobble-admissible codes outperform it).
\emph{Access:} \texttt{papers/25\_genetic\_code/code/codon\_sieve.py}
through \texttt{codon\_sieve\_phase6.py} (runtime $<10$ minutes; NumPy, SciPy).
\emph{Primary use:} synthetic biology, origin-of-life research, and
information-theoretic analyses of the genetic code.
\emph{Utility note:} Prior computational analyses of genetic code optimality
typically evaluate one or two criteria in isolation---most commonly error
minimisation or polar requirement correlation.
The sieve is the first tool to apply eight criteria simultaneously, including
a generative-to-dissipative information balance criterion not present in any
prior published framework.
Because the eight criteria are evaluated jointly, the uniqueness result ($0/99{,}998$)
is strictly stronger than single-criterion results: a code that satisfies one criterion
well while degrading another is correctly rejected.
Researchers in synthetic biology designing artificial genetic codes, or in
origin-of-life studies quantifying how constrained the standard code is,
can apply the sieve to any candidate mapping and receive a criterion-by-criterion
breakdown, identifying precisely which biological constraints are binding.
