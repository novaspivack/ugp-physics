\begin{abstract}
We prove in Lean~4, with zero \sorry\ for all theorem-grade claims, that the
Universal Generative Principle (UGP) winding, chirality, hypercharge, and
color-fiber data determine the Standard Model's \emph{finite renormalizable
interaction skeleton}: gauge-fermion vertices, Yukawa mass-generating vertices,
anomaly cancellation, representation-level color-singlet constraints,
proton-stability forbiddance at dimension four, and a topological dark-sector
isolation gap.

The winding number $W = N_cQ$, the GTE chirality fiber
$T/T^\dagger$, integerized hypercharge $Y_3 = 2W - T_6 = 3Y$ (e.g.,
$Y_3(e_L) = -3$, $Y_3(Q_L) = +1$, $Y_3(e_R) = -6$, $Y_3(u_R) = +4$), and
the minimal gauge-boson winding spectrum $\{0,\pm 3\}$ suffice to reproduce
all renormalizable SM vertex schemas and exclude all forbidden ones---without
any additional gauge-theoretic input.

The main theorem---$\mathrm{UGPVertex}(f_1,f_2,B) \leftrightarrow
\mathrm{SMVertex}(f_1,f_2,B)$ for all colored fermions $f_1,f_2$ and gauge
bosons $B$---is proved by exhaustive finite case analysis.  A vertex audit
over 64 electroweak schemas returns MISMATCH~COUNT\,=\,0 (SHA-256:
\texttt{c927758a9b7801db\ldots}).  Three independent proofs force $N_c = 3$;
the SM fermion quartet is the unique anomaly-free solution at $N_c = 3$; proton
decay at dimension four is topologically forbidden; and fermions with $W \in
\{1,-2,4\}$ are isolated from all SM particles via SM bosons.

Electroweak predictions from Lean-certified bare couplings give
$m_W = 80.364\,\mathrm{GeV}$ ($-0.42\sigma$ vs PDG 2024 world average
$80.3692 \pm 0.0133$) and $\alpha_s(M_Z) = 0.1179$ ($0.0\sigma$).  These
results were independently foreshadowed computationally: the PR-1/Logos
cellular automaton recovered the winding-transfer structure at 87.38\%
consistency with zero false negatives over $1.02 \times 10^6$ events, and the
PR-0/MFRR field substrate exhibited force emergence from dissonance
minimization, both before the Lean proofs were written.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Anticipating objections}

\paragraph{Is the derivation circular?}
A natural concern: are $\mathrm{UGPVertex}$ and $\mathrm{SMVertex}$ defined to
be equal, making the theorem trivial?  They are not.
$\mathrm{UGPVertex}(f_1, f_2, B)$ is defined from \emph{UGP-native} predicates:
winding conservation $W(f_1) + W_B = W(f_2)$, the same-sector predicate
$\mathrm{sameSector}(f_1, f_2)$ (equal strand counts --- a topological datum),
and chirality constraints from the GTE $T/T^\dagger$ history.
$\mathrm{SMVertex}(f_1, f_2, B)$ is defined from the conventional SM
representation-theoretic vertex table: same fermion type for $\gamma/Z$,
left-handed doublet partners for $W^\pm$, colour-transfer same-type quarks for
gluons.  The SM doublet pairing \emph{is} the output of the UGP analysis, not
its input.  The two definitions are proved equivalent; they are not identical by
construction.

\paragraph{Is the winding table just SM charges times $N_c$?}
In the companion Braid Atlas~\cite{ugp-p17}, the charge formula $Q = W/N_c$
is the starting point.  In this paper, the winding table $\{-3,0,+2,-1\}$ is
proved to be the \emph{unique} solution to the UGP constraints --- anomaly
cancellation, weak-doublet pairing, $N_c = 3$ --- at $N_c = 3$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:unique}).  The SM charges are \emph{derived} as $Q = W/3$
from this table, not assumed.

\paragraph{Does 87.38\% CA corroboration indicate the formal result is weak?}
No.  The $C = 0$ sub-result --- zero false negatives, exact across $1.02
\times 10^6$ events and 8 seeds --- is structurally exact, not statistical: the
R-clause of the Logos rule suppresses all $|\Delta W| = 2$ (cross-family
forbidden) interactions mechanically, not probabilistically.  The $12.62\%$
residual is in the false-positive quadrant (B), arising from $\Delta g \in
\{1,3\}$ pairs where the g-field alone cannot distinguish SM-doublet
($|\Delta W|=3$) from forbidden ($|\Delta W|=1,5$) events without the full
four-field structure.  This is an irreducible single-field limitation, not a
failure of the formal theorem.  The formal theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:c4}) is
machine-verified to $100\%$ over the symbolic model.

\paragraph{Was the PR-1 experiment designed to find the SM structure?}
No.  The Logos condition $g_0 \neq g_1$ was discovered by exhaustive search
over the full space of reversible CA rules, optimizing for composite-object
stability and fermion-spectrum completeness --- not for agreement with
$|\Delta W| \in \{0, 3\}$.  The correspondence to the C4 theorem was

\subsection{Least-action principle for UGP vertices}

\begin{definition}[Step cost]
The \emph{step cost} of a vertex $(f_1, B, f_2)$ is the winding mismatch:
\[
  \mathrm{stepCost}(f_1, B, f_2) = |W(f_1) + W_B - W(f_2)|.

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Path action]
The \emph{UGP path action} of an interaction path

is the total winding mismatch along the path:
\[
  S_\UGP(\gamma) = \sum_{t} \mathrm{stepCost}\!\bigl(f_1^{(t)}, B^{(t)}, f_2^{(t)}\bigr).

\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Least-action principle, zero \sorry]

Every SM-allowed gauge-fermion vertex has $\mathrm{stepCost} = 0$.  Every

This gives the first ``least-action'' statement in the UGP framework:
