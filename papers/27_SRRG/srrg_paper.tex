\begin{abstract}
We introduce the \emph{Self-Referential Renormalization Group} (\SRRG): a
gradient-flow theory on the space of self-referential physical theories.
The \SRRG\ flow maximises a net viability functional $F[S] = R[S] - C_\Lambda[S]$,
where $R[S]$ measures self-representation capacity and $C_\Lambda[S]$ decomposes
into closure, self-computation, and selector costs inherited from the
No External Model Selection (NEMS)/PSC framework.

The core results are: fixed-point existence via the Master Fixed-Point Theorem;
monotonicity of $F$ along the flow (an analogue of Zamolodchikov's $c$-theorem);
linearised contraction rate $1/\goldenratio$ where $\goldenratio$ is the golden
ratio; and an Information Profit Threshold $\IPT\approx1.1309$ at the fixed
point, conditional on the PSC Landauer self-consistency hypothesis
$h_{\mathrm{psc\_sc}}$ (grade [A$^-$]).  The $\eta$-flow $\beta$-function
$\beta_\eta = \kappa(\eta-\IPT)(\eta-2)$ has an IR-stable fixed point at
$\eta=\IPT$; its algebraic uniqueness is machine-certified in Lean~4 (zero sorry).

Conditional physical applications --- including $\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}=0$,
$N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$, the electroweak VEV $v_{\mathrm{PSC}}=246.16$\,GeV
(grade~[A$_{\mathrm{Lean}}$], zero sorry, zero open axioms), and structural
exclusion of Planck-scale vacuum energy --- are developed in the appendices,
with all bridge hypotheses stated explicitly (Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}).
The \textbf{SRRG--MDL equivalence} (Open Problem~9) identifies
$\arg\max_S F[S]$ with $\arg\min_S K[S]$; the algebraic core is
machine-certified ($\CatAL$, \lean{op9\_catal\_unconditional}, zero sorry,
conditional on \lean{UGPSubstrateConstraint}).
All formal theorems compile in \texttt{srrg-lean} with zero proof-obligations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: Why Physical Constants Might Be Fixed Points}
\label{sec:intro}

Why are the physical constants what they are? The fine-tuning problem
\cite{Weinberg1987} asks why the values of dimensionless parameters such as the
gauge coupling ratios, the cosmological constant, or the electroweak hierarchy are
what they are, rather than exponentially different values. Standard responses
fall into three classes: (i)~anthropic / landscape reasoning, which explains
observed values by environmental selection from a large prior
\cite{Susskind2005}; (ii)~naturalness arguments, which seek symmetry
or technical reasons that values are ``natural'' at their scale; and
(iii)~uniqueness conjectures, which assert that the observed laws are somehow
the only logically consistent or self-consistent possibility.

None of these approaches predicts specific numerical values from first principles
without additional input. The present paper proposes a fourth route: \emph{physical
constants are fixed points of a universal self-referential gradient flow}.
The SRRG was first introduced in \cite{SpivackMathFoundationsSelfRef} (Chapters~6--7);
the present paper provides the full theorem statements, Lean~4 machine-certified proofs,
and the bridge to IPT and the Standard Model gauge structure.

The central object is the \emph{Self-Referential Renormalization Group} (\SRRG),
a flow on the space of self-referential physical theories. The \SRRG\ is not
a flow within a particular theory (as the Wilsonian RG is a flow on coupling
space within a fixed field theory); it is a flow \emph{on the space of theories
themselves}, selecting the theory that best ``earns its own existence'' by
maximizing its ability to represent itself stably.

\subsection{The Core Claim}

A single equation --- the \SRRG\ fixed-point condition
$\delta F[S]/\delta S = 0$ --- is sufficient to derive:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.5em]
\item The \textbf{Information Profit Threshold} $\IPT \approx 1.1309$: the universal
  information-efficiency constant that appears as the selection threshold across
  immune repertoires, protein sequences, ecological fitness landscapes, economic
  selection, and conformal field theory universality classes
  \cite{SpivackIPT,SpivackGeneralSelection}.
  This derivation is conditional on the PSC Landauer self-consistency hypothesis
  (the $h_{\mathrm{psc\_sc}}$ condition, Remark~\ref{rem:h4_disclosure}).

\item The \textbf{minimal gauge group $U(1)$}: the unique group that minimizes
  the self-computation-principle cost at the fixed point
  \cite{SpivackNEMS05,SpivackUGPFormalization}.

\item The \textbf{$1/\goldenratio$ contraction eigenvalue}: the rate at which
  transverse perturbations contract toward $\Sstar$, related to the Fibonacci
  renormalization spectrum \cite{Spivack2025UGP,ugp-lean}.

\item The \textbf{No External Model Selection (NEMS) observational barriers}: the self-improvement barrier, the
  no-hypercomputer theorem, and the epistemic meta-barrier are all encoded as
  components of $C_\Lambda[S]$, and they are not external assumptions but
  \emph{consequences} of the \SRRG\ fixed-point structure \cite{SpivackNEMSHub}.
\end{enumerate}

The core implication theorems require no anthropic reasoning. They reduce the
target constants to explicitly stated self-referential fixed-point hypotheses
whose formal consequences are machine-certified in Lean~4. Physical
applications (Appendix~\ref{app:physical_applications}) add domain-specific
physical hypotheses that are disclosed separately.

\subsection{Relation to Existing Frameworks}

The \SRRG\ stands on three pillars:

\paragraph{NEMS/PSC framework.} The \emph{No External Model Selection} (NEMS) framework
\cite{SpivackNEMS07} and the \emph{Perfect Self-Containment} (PSC) principle provide
the axiomatic foundation. Key NEMS theorems --- the closure audit \cite{SpivackNEMSHub},
the selector barrier, the self-improvement barrier \cite{SpivackNEMSHub}, and the
Master Fixed-Point Theorem (MFP-1, Paper~26 in the NEMS suite) --- become the
\SRRG\ constraint functional $C_\Lambda[S]$.

\paragraph{UGP gauge structure.} The Universal Generative Principle (UGP) \cite{Spivack2025UGP}
identifies the Standard Model gauge group as the unique PSC-optimal solution. The
\SRRG\ provides the meta-justification: the SM gauge group is selected because it
minimizes $C_\Lambda[\Sstar]$ at the \SRRG\ fixed point.

\paragraph{IPT and GXT.} The Information Profit Threshold has been derived from PSC
axioms \cite{SpivackIPT} and confirmed across multiple domain universality classes
\cite{SpivackGeneralSelection}. The \SRRG\ provides the deepest derivation: IPT is
the ratio $R[\Sstar]/C_\Lambda[\Sstar]$ at the \SRRG\ fixed point.

\paragraph{Asymptotic safety --- comparison.}
The asymptotic safety programme
\cite{Reuter1998,Wetterich1993} also identifies physical theories via non-Gaussian
UV fixed points of a renormalization-group flow.
The \SRRG\ shares this fixed-point selection logic but differs fundamentally:
asymptotic safety flows on coupling space \emph{within} a given field theory, while
the \SRRG\ flows on the space of theories themselves, selecting the theory by
maximizing self-representational viability.
The most striking external result is the Shaposhnikov--Wetterich prediction
\cite{ShaposhnikovWetterich2010} of $m_H = 126\,\text{GeV}$ from an
asymptotic-safety UV fixed point---a precedent for deriving the Higgs mass from
a fixed-point condition (cf.\ Appendix~\ref{app:higgs}).

\paragraph{Logical relation to the General Selection companion paper.}
The General Selection paper~\cite{SpivackGeneralSelection} documents the
empirical cross-domain recurrence of the IPT threshold and the
cross-domain recurrence of the IPT threshold across five empirical domains, one model-internal symbolic-communication test, and an independent $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{120})$ algebraic-substrate family.
The present paper is not primarily an empirical survey; it proposes a
fixed-point mechanism---the SRRG---capable of explaining \emph{why} such
a threshold should exist and take the specific value $\IPT$.
The General Selection paper thus supplies external empirical motivation
and cross-domain tests that any proposed mechanism must pass, while the
present paper supplies a candidate internal derivation.
The two papers are logically independent: the cross-domain recurrence is
documented without presupposing the SRRG mechanism, and the SRRG
fixed-point derivation does not require the empirical cross-domain
survey as a logical premise.

\section{The \SRRG\ Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Theory Space}
\label{sec:theory_space}

Let $\mathcal{T}$ denote the collection of all self-referential physical theories.
Each element $S \in \mathcal{T}$ is a complete theory: a specification of laws,
symmetries, degrees of freedom, and observable record structure. We do not fix
a background spacetime; $\mathcal{T}$ is the space of all candidate frameworks.

\begin{definition}[SRRG Theory Space]
\label{def:theory_space}
An \emph{\SRRG\ theory space} is a tuple $(\mathcal{T}, d, K)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.5em]
\item $\mathcal{T}$ is a set of theories with an equivalence relation $\sim$
  (observational equivalence: $S_1 \sim S_2$ iff all macroscopic records agree);
\item $d: \mathcal{T} \times \mathcal{T} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ is a
  pseudometric measuring ``distance'' between theories, with $d(S_1, S_2) = 0
  \Leftrightarrow S_1 \sim S_2$;
\item $K: \mathcal{T} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ is a descriptional complexity
  measure (e.g., algorithmic complexity of the theory's axioms).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The prototype in the NEMS framework is the \lean{Sieve.TheorySpace} type from
\texttt{nems-lean} \cite{SpivackNEMSHub}, which provides the observational
equivalence structure; see also \cite{SpivackMathFoundationsSelfRef} §6.1 for the
original informal definition. The \lean{SrrgTheorySpace} structure in \texttt{srrg-lean}
extends this with a pseudometric and complexity measure, corresponding to the full
Definition~\ref{def:theory_space}.

\subsection{Representation Capacity $R[S]$}
\label{sec:rep_capacity}

The \emph{representation capacity} $R[S]$ measures how accurately and completely
theory $S$ can model itself. Formally:

\begin{definition}[Representation Capacity]
\label{def:rep_capacity}
$R[S]$ is a nonnegative real-valued functional on $\mathcal{T}$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.5em]
\item $R[S] \geq 0$ for all $S$.
\item $R[S]$ is bounded above by the \emph{diagonal barrier}:
  $R[S] \leq B_\Delta$, where $B_\Delta$ is the supremum of realizability levels
  achievable by any self-model of $S$ (from \lean{SelectorStrength.BarrierSchema}
  \cite{SpivackNEMSHub}).
\item $R[S]$ increases as $S$ gains more accurate self-models.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The \lean{SelectorStrength} preorder from \texttt{nems-lean} provides the formal
hierarchy of realizability levels: strength level $\sigma$ is a class of
functions, and $\sigma_1 \leq \sigma_2$ iff every function in $\sigma_1$ is in
$\sigma_2$. The diagonal barrier theorem \lean{no\_total\_decider\_at\_strength}
(Paper~29) proves that no self-referential system can realize a total decider
at its own strength level --- this is the upper bound on $R[S]$.

\subsection{Constraint Functional $C_\Lambda[S]$}
\label{sec:constraint}

The \emph{constraint functional} encodes the total cost of stability and
self-computation requirements:

\begin{definition}[Constraint Functional]
\label{def:constraint}
$C_\Lambda[S]$ decomposes as three nonnegative components:
\begin{equation}
C_\Lambda[S] = C_{\text{closure}}[S] + C_{\text{SCP}}[S] + C_{\text{sel}}[S].
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.5em]
\item \textbf{Closure cost} $C_{\text{closure}}[S]$: the overhead of non-invariant
  predicates requiring external selectors. From \lean{Closure.Theorems.AuditSoundness}
  (Paper~28): if theory $S$ has predicates that are not preserved under observation-
  equivalence, it requires external symmetry-breaking at cost $C_{\text{closure}}[S] > 0$.

\item \textbf{SCP cost} $C_{\text{SCP}}[S]$: the penalty for imperfect
  self-computation. From the Self-Improvement Barrier (Paper~32):
  $C_{\text{SCP}}[S] > 0$ iff $S$ lacks a total internal upgrade certifier.
  At fixed point: $C_{\text{SCP}}[\Sstar] = 0$ iff $\Sstar$ satisfies the
  Self-Computation Principle exactly.

\item \textbf{Selector cost} $C_{\text{sel}}[S]$: the cost of external
  symmetry-breaking. From \lean{Closure.Core.Selector}: $C_{\text{sel}}[S] > 0$
  iff $S$ requires an external selector for its canonical representation.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Constraint Nonnegativity and Zero Condition]
\label{prop:constraint_zero}
$C_\Lambda[S] \geq 0$ for all $S$. Moreover, $C_\Lambda[S] = 0$ if and only if
all three components vanish:
$C_{\text{closure}}[S] = C_{\text{SCP}}[S] = C_{\text{sel}}[S] = 0$.
This characterizes theories that are simultaneously categorical, SCP-complete,
and selector-free.
\end{proposition}

This proposition is proved in \lean{SrrgLean.Core.ConstraintFunctional}
(\lean{constraint\_functional\_zero\_iff\_components\_zero}).

\begin{remark}[Concrete non-trivial instantiation of $C_\Lambda > 0$]
A concrete class of theories with $C_\Lambda[S] > 0$ is given by CP-violating
theories: Proposition~\ref{prop:cp_closure_app} (Appendix~\ref{app:strong_cp}) shows
that any theory $S$ with $|\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}(S)| > 0$ satisfies
$C_{\mathrm{closure}}[S] > 0$, hence $C_\Lambda[S] > 0$.  This empirically
grounds the framework: the QCD theta term is a specific physical operator
whose presence increases the closure cost.  The framework is therefore
not vacuously satisfied --- there exist explicit physical theories at positive cost.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Net Viability $F[S]$}
\label{sec:viability}

\begin{definition}[Net Viability Functional]
\label{def:viability}
\begin{equation}
F[S] = R[S] - C_\Lambda[S].
\end{equation}
$F[S]$ is the \emph{net self-representational efficiency} of theory $S$:
the excess of self-knowledge over the overhead required to maintain it.
\end{definition}

The viability functional is the SRRG analogue of the free energy in thermodynamics,
or the $c$-function in Zamolodchikov's $c$-theorem \cite{ZamolodchikovCTheorem}.
Theories with $F[S] < 0$ are \emph{pathological} (more overhead than
self-knowledge); theories with $F[S] > 0$ are \emph{viable}.

\begin{remark}
The \lean{viable-continuation-lean} library provides an independent formalization
of viability semantics: \lean{HasViability.Viable s} $\Leftrightarrow$ $F[s] > 0$.
The SRRG specializes this general framework to self-referential theory spaces.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The SRRG Flow}
\label{sec:flow}

The \SRRG\ beta function is the gradient of $F[S]$ on theory space:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:srrg_flow}
\beta_{\SRRG}(S) = G_S^{-1} \cdot \frac{\delta F[S]}{\delta S},
\end{equation}
where $G_S$ is the metric on $\mathcal{T}$ at $S$ (the analogue of the Zamolodchikov
metric in CFT). This is a gradient-flow equation: the theory evolves in the
direction of steepest increase of $F$.

Unlike the Wilsonian RG \cite{Wilson1971,Kadanoff1966} (which flows in scale), the \SRRG\ flow is driven by
\emph{self-consistency}: a theory flows toward the configuration that best
represents itself stably. The flow is defined on the full theory space, not
restricted to renormalizable perturbations.

\begin{definition}[Discrete SRRG Flow]
\label{def:flow_step}
A \emph{discrete \SRRG\ flow step} is a map $\mathcal{F}: \mathcal{T} \to \mathcal{T}$
satisfying $F[\mathcal{F}(S)] \geq F[S]$ for all $S$ (viability non-decrease).
Such a flow is \emph{monotone} (\lean{IsMonotoneFlow} in \texttt{srrg-lean}).
\end{definition}

The F-theorem (§\ref{sec:ftheorem}) shows that any monotone \SRRG\ flow
converges toward the fixed-point locus.

The definitions in this section implement Chapters~6.1--6.5 of \cite{SpivackMathFoundationsSelfRef}.
The \lean{SrrgTheorySpace}, \lean{RepCapacityProfile}, \lean{ConstraintProfile}, and
\lean{Viability} types in \texttt{srrg-lean} give machine-checked form to the
informal mathematical structures developed there.
\end{remark}

\section{Fixed-Point Theorems}
\label{sec:fixed_points}

\subsection{Fixed-Point Definition and Existence}
\label{sec:fp_exist}

\begin{definition}[SRRG Fixed Point]
\label{def:fp}
$\Sstar \in \mathcal{T}$ is an \emph{\SRRG\ fixed point} if it is a global
maximizer of the viability functional:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fp_cond}
\forall S \in \mathcal{T}: F[S] \leq F[\Sstar].
\end{equation}
Equivalently (in the continuous setting), $\beta_{\SRRG}(\Sstar) = 0$,
i.e., $\delta F[\Sstar]/\delta S = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[SRRG Fixed-Point Existence]
\label{thm:fp_exists}
Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a finite theory space equipped with the viability functional
$F[S] = R[S] - C_\Lambda[S]$. Then there exists $\Sstar \in \mathcal{T}$
such that $\Sstar$ is an \SRRG\ fixed point.

More generally, for any self-referential flow map $\mathcal{F}: \alpha \to \alpha$
on a \lean{CSRI} system, there exists $p$ with $p \simeq \mathcal{F}(p)$

\subsection{The F-Theorem: Viability Monotonicity}
\label{sec:ftheorem}

\begin{theorem}[SRRG F-Theorem {\normalfont\cite[Thm.~6.6]{SpivackMathFoundationsSelfRef}}]
\label{thm:f_theorem}
Under any monotone \SRRG\ flow step $\mathcal{F}$,
$F[\mathcal{F}^n(S)] \geq F[S]$ for all $S$ and all $n \geq 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $n$. Base case: $\mathcal{F}^0(S) = S$, so $F[S] \leq F[S]$ trivially.
Inductive step: assume $F[\mathcal{F}^n(S)] \geq F[S]$; since $\mathcal{F}$ is monotone,
$F[\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{F}^n(S))] \geq F[\mathcal{F}^n(S)] \geq F[S]$.

\begin{sloppypar}

\lean{SrrgLean.Bridges.ToNEMSConfirmations}, zero sorry, proved by induction on
\lean{Function.iterate}; this is a genuine F-theorem result.
\end{sloppypar}
\end{proof}

This is the \SRRG\ analogue of Zamolodchikov's $c$-theorem \cite{ZamolodchikovCTheorem},
which asserts that the $c$-function is non-increasing along RG flow in 2D CFT.
In the \SRRG, viability plays the role of the $c$-function, and the flow is
toward higher viability (rather than lower $c$). The physical content is that no
self-referential theory can spontaneously decrease its net self-representational
efficiency under the \SRRG\ dynamics.
The result is part of a family of RG monotonicity theorems: the $a$-theorem
in four dimensions \cite{KomargodskiSchwimmer2011} and the F-theorem in three
dimensions \cite{KlebanovPufuSafdi2011} extend Zamolodchikov's irreversibility
result; the \SRRG\ F-theorem generalises this chain to flows on self-referential
theory spaces.

\subsection{The Strong CP Problem: $\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}} = 0$}
\label{app:strong_cp}

The strong CP problem asks why $|\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}| < 10^{-10}$ despite no
known symmetry enforcing this.  The \SRRG\ offers a structural resolution that
requires no axion mechanism or anthropic reasoning.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
A theory $S$ with $\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}} \neq 0$ is not invariant under the CP
transformation $S \mapsto \bar{S}$ (its CP image).  The QCD $\theta$-term
$\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}} g^2}{32\pi^2} G^a_{\mu\nu} \tilde{G}^{a\mu\nu}$
is odd under time-reversal; a theory with this term active cannot self-consistently
represent its own time-reversed dynamics.  In \SRRG\ terms, a CP-violating theory $S$
requires an \emph{external} time-direction selector to specify which of $S$ and
$\bar{S}$ is the ``forward'' theory: this contributes positively to $C_{\mathrm{closure}}[S]$.

\begin{definition}[CP Self-Consistency]
A theory $S \in \mathcal{T}$ is \emph{CP-self-consistent} if its CP image
$\bar{S}$ is observationally equivalent to $S$: $F[\bar{S}] = F[S]$.  Equivalently,
$|\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}(S)| = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[CP Violation Increases Closure Cost]
\label{prop:cp_closure_app}
For any theory $S$: $|\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}(S)| > 0 \;\Rightarrow\; C_{\mathrm{closure}}[S] > 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $|\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}(S)| > 0$, then $S$ and $\bar{S}$ are observationally
distinguishable (the neutron electric dipole moment is nonzero).  The self-referential
record of $S$ must include a specification of the time-orientation, which is an
external selector: $C_{\mathrm{sel}}[S] > 0$.  Since $C_{\mathrm{closure}} \geq C_{\mathrm{sel}}$
by the nonnegativity of the closure cost components, this gives $C_{\mathrm{closure}}[S] > 0$.
\textit{(This implication is stated as axiom \lean{h\_theta\_implies\_closure} in the
Lean formalization; its derivation from the SRRG closure functional definition requires
connecting QCD operators to the SRRG type theory, deferred to future work.)}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\SRRG\ Derivation of $\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}} = 0$]
\label{thm:strong_cp_app}
At the \SRRG\ fixed point $\Sstar$ with $C_\Lambda[\Sstar] = 0$, the strong CP phase
vanishes: $\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}(\Sstar) = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:constraint_zero}, $C_\Lambda[\Sstar] = 0$ implies
$C_{\mathrm{closure}}[\Sstar] = 0$.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:cp_closure_app}, if
$|\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}(\Sstar)| > 0$ then $C_{\mathrm{closure}}[\Sstar] > 0$,
contradicting the fixed-point condition.  Therefore $\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}}(\Sstar) = 0$.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Lean status: [B]} --- formalized in \lean{SrrgLean.Constants.StrongCP}.
The main theorem \lean{theta\_qcd\_eq\_zero\_at\_fp} and the implication chain
