\begin{abstract}
The Generative Triple Evolution (GTE) framework is built on three algebraic
pillars: the Fibonacci--Möbius action on $\ProjLine$, the order-21 metacyclic
group $\FineSeven = \Zcyc{7} \rtimes \Zcyc{3}$ (the Borel stabilizer of
$\infty$ in $\PSLtwo$), and the Eisenstein integers $\Eisenstein$
($\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$, primitive cube root of unity).
This paper proves that these three pillars are unified through $\PSLtwo$,
the unique simple group of order~168.

We establish five main results, all machine-certified in Lean~4 with zero
\texttt{sorry}.
First, $\FineSeven$ is the stabilizer of $\infty \in \ProjLine$ in $\PSLtwo$
(the Borel subgroup of order~21), and together with the Fibonacci--Möbius
generator, these two elements generate $\PGLtwo$ of order~336
(\CatAL; \lean{f21\_is\_borel\_psl27}, \lean{pgl27\_generated\_by\_singer\_and\_borel}).
Second, $\PSLtwo$ is the unique simple group of order~168 and equals $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{F})$
for the Fano plane $\mathcal{F}$, and $\FineSeven$ acts simply transitively on all~21
incidence flags of $\mathcal{F}$ (\CatAL; \lean{psl27\_is\_aut\_fano},
\lean{f21\_regular\_on\_fano\_flags}).
Third, the Eisenstein integers yield $\FineSeven$ and $\AltFour$ from the same
functor: $7$ splits as $7\Eisenstein = \pi\bar\pi$ (norm-7 primes), giving
$\Eisenstein/(\pi) \cong \mathrm{GF}(7)$ and $\FineSeven$ from the unit group;
$2$ is inert ($2\Eisenstein$ prime), giving $\Eisenstein/(2) \cong \mathrm{GF}(4)$,
whose additive group is $\KleinFour \cong \Zcyc{2}^2$ and whose multiplicative group
is $\Zcyc{3} = \mu_3$, assembling to $\AltFour = \KleinFour \rtimes \mu_3$
(\CatAL; \lean{eisenstein\_a4\_from\_inert\_2}).
Fourth, the Klein quartic — the unique Hurwitz surface saturating $84(g-1)$ automorphisms
for genus $g$ — has genus equal to $\Ngen = 3$ (the number of Standard Model generations),
arising from the $(2,3,7)$ triangle group shared with $\PSLtwo$
(\CatAL; \lean{klein\_quartic\_genus\_eq\_n\_gen}).
Fifth, the manifest GTE neutrino flavor symmetries — the generation cycling $\mu_3$
and the $\mu$--$\tau$ exchange $\Zcyc{2}$ — generate $S_3$ of order~6, which
embeds in $\AltFour = \KleinFour \rtimes \mu_3$ as a complement to the Klein normal
subgroup supplied by the Eisenstein residue $\Eisenstein/(2)$
(\CatAL; \lean{gte\_manifest\_flavor\_is\_s3\_in\_a4}).

These five results establish a coherent group-theoretic skeleton: $\PSLtwo$ contains
$\FineSeven$ as its Borel subgroup, acts as the automorphism group of the Fano
plane (with $\FineSeven$ as its regular flag-transitive subgroup), and is the
automorphism group of the genus-3 Klein quartic.
The shared $\mu_3$ factor links $\FineSeven$ to $\AltFour$ via the Eisenstein functor,
and the manifest $S_3 \leq \AltFour$ embedding (result~5) completes the leading-order
neutrino flavor mixing group (\CatAD).

The physical interpretation — $\FineSeven$ as gauge skeleton group, $\AltFour$ as
the GTE leading-order neutrino flavor symmetry yielding tri-bimaximal mixing
angles, and the Klein quartic as the geometric reason for three generations — is
argued at level \CatAD{} or \CatD{} as stated.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%=============================================================================

The Generative Triple Evolution framework derives the Standard Model generation
structure from a cellular automaton over $\GF{7}$ governed by the polynomial
$p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR$.
The algebraic architecture of GTE is built on three recurring objects:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \textbf{Fibonacci--Möbius map} $\mu: x \mapsto (x+1)/x$ acting on
    $\ProjLine = \mathbb{F}_7 \cup \{\infty\}$, whose cyclic orbit structure
    encodes the GTE generation sequence;
  \item the \textbf{metacyclic group} $\FineSeven = \Zcyc{7} \rtimes \Zcyc{3}$
    of order~21, identified in prior work~\cite{SpivackGTEMobius} as the GTE gauge
    skeleton group; and
  \item the \textbf{Eisenstein integers} $\Eisenstein = \mathbb{Z}[e^{2\pi i/3}]$,
    which provide the arithmetic bridge between the GTE polynomial's $\mathbb{Z}_7$
    structure and the Standard Model flavor groups.
\end{itemize}
The algebraic structure of the GTE $\mathbb{Z}_7$ polynomial and its encoding of
Standard Model quantum numbers is established in~\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialWolfram}.
The full two-level framework --- Level~1 algebraic certificate ($p$) and Level~2
continuum field ($\mathcal{L}_{\PHIMDL}$) --- is derived in
P48~\cite{SpivackGTECompleteFramework}; the present paper operates entirely at
Level~1 (the algebraic skeleton) and does not derive the field law.

This paper shows that these three pillars are not independent constructions but
aspects of a single algebraic object: $\PSLtwo$, the unique simple group of
order~168.
The group $\FineSeven$ is the Borel subgroup of $\PSLtwo$ — the stabilizer of
$\infty$ in the action on $\ProjLine$.
The Fibonacci--Möbius map together with $\FineSeven$ generate the full
$\PGLtwo$ of order~336.
The Eisenstein functor $\mathbb{Z}[\omega] \to \mathrm{Res}_q$ applied at
$q = 7$ (split prime) yields $\GF{7}$ and $\FineSeven$; applied at $q = 2$
(inert prime) yields $\GF{4}$ and $\AltFour$, completing the GTE neutrino flavor
symmetry to tri-bimaximal mixing.
The same $(2,3,7)$ triangle group underlying $\PSLtwo$ gives the Klein quartic —
the unique Hurwitz surface with $\mathrm{Aut} = \PSLtwo$ — whose genus equals
$\Ngen = 3$.

The key theorem names and certification levels are collected in

\section{Mathematical Setup: \texorpdfstring{$\PSLtwo$ and $\FineSeven$}{PSL(2,7) and F21}}
\label{sec:setup}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{The projective line \texorpdfstring{$\ProjLine$}{P1(F7)}}
\label{sec:p1}

Let $\mathbb{F}_7 = \mathrm{GF}(7) = \{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ with arithmetic
modulo~7.
The projective line $\ProjLine = \mathbb{F}_7 \cup \{\infty\}$ has
$|\ProjLine| = 8$ points.
The group $\mathrm{PGL}(2,7)$ acts on $\ProjLine$ by Möbius transformations:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \cdot x
  = \frac{ax+b}{cx+d}, \quad ad - bc \neq 0 \pmod{7},
\end{equation}
with $\infty \mapsto a/c$ when $c \neq 0$, and $\infty \mapsto \infty$ when
$c = 0$.
The subgroup $\PSLtwo \leq \PGLtwo$ consists of matrices with $\det = 1$ modulo
identification $M \sim -M$, giving $|\PSLtwo| = |\mathrm{SL}(2,7)|/2 = 168$.

\subsection{The Fibonacci--Möbius generator}

The \textbf{Fibonacci--Möbius map} is the transformation $\mu: x \mapsto (x+1)/x$,
represented by the matrix
\begin{equation}
  M_\mu = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{GL}(2,7).
\end{equation}
The sequence $\mu^n(\infty)$ reproduces the Fibonacci sequence modulo~7: since
$\mu(\infty) = 1$, $\mu^2(\infty) = 2$, $\mu^3(\infty) = 3/2$, etc., and the
periods are governed by the Pisano period $\pi(7) = 16$.
In the GTE framework, $\mu$ is the map whose orbit on $\ProjLine$ encodes the
generation-changing dynamics~\cite{SpivackGTEMobius,SpivackGTECompleteFramework}.

\subsection{The metacyclic group \texorpdfstring{$\FineSeven$}{F21}}

The \textbf{affine group} $\mathrm{AGL}(1,7) = \mathbb{F}_7 \rtimes \mathbb{F}_7^\times$
has order $7 \times 6 = 42$ and acts on $\mathbb{F}_7$ by $x \mapsto ax + b$
($a \neq 0$).
Its Sylow-3 subgroup is the \textbf{metacyclic group}
\begin{equation}
  \FineSeven = \Zcyc{7} \rtimes \Zcyc{3},
  \quad |\FineSeven| = 21,
\end{equation}
generated by the translation $\tau: x \mapsto x+1$ and the multiplication
$\sigma: x \mapsto 2x$ (note $2^3 = 8 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$, so $\sigma$ has
order~3).
This group is identified in the GTE framework as the gauge skeleton group whose
orbit structure encodes the $N_{\mathrm{gen}} = 3$ generations~\cite{SpivackGTEMobius}.

\begin{theorem}[\CatAL\ --- \lean{f21\_is\_borel\_psl27}]
\label{thm:borel}
The stabilizer of $\infty \in \ProjLine$ in $\PSLtwo$ consists of all
upper-triangular matrices $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix}$
with $a^3 = 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_7^{\times}$, and this subgroup is isomorphic to
$\FineSeven = \Zcyc{7} \rtimes \Zcyc{3}$ of order~21.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\CatAL\ --- \lean{pgl27\_generated\_by\_singer\_and\_borel}]
\label{thm:pgl_gen}
Let $\mu$ denote the Fibonacci--Möbius action on $\ProjLine$ and let $\FineSeven$
be generated by the translation $x \mapsto x+1$ and multiplication $x \mapsto 2x$.
Then $\langle \mu,\, \FineSeven \rangle = \PGLtwo$, a group of order~$336$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:pgl_gen} means that the GTE's two fundamental building blocks
--- the Fibonacci--Möbius dynamics and the $\FineSeven$ gauge skeleton --- are not
independent; together they generate the full symmetry group of $\ProjLine$.
The index $[\PGLtwo : \PSLtwo] = 2$ reflects the outer automorphism of $\PSLtwo$
corresponding to field conjugation.
\end{remark}

\section{The Fano Plane and the Regular Flag Action}
\label{sec:fano}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{The Fano plane}

The \textbf{Fano plane} $\mathcal{F} = \mathrm{PG}(2, \mathbb{F}_2)$ is the
unique projective plane of order~2, with 7~points and 7~lines, each line
containing 3~points and each point on 3~lines.
An \textbf{incidence flag} is a pair $(p, L)$ where $p$ is a point of $\mathcal{F}$
and $L$ is a line incident to $p$.
The Fano plane has $7 \times 3 = 21$ incidence flags.

\begin{theorem}[\CatAL\ --- \lean{psl27\_is\_aut\_fano}]
\label{thm:psl_aut}
$\PSLtwo$ is the unique simple group of order~$168$, and it acts as the full
automorphism group of the Fano plane: $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{F}) \cong \PSLtwo$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Regular action of \texorpdfstring{$\FineSeven$}{F21} on Fano flags}

\begin{theorem}[\CatAL\ --- \lean{f21\_regular\_on\_fano\_flags}]
\label{thm:flag_regular}
Encode the Fano plane points as $\mathbb{Z}_7$, lines as translates of the
Singer difference set $\{0,1,3\}$, and flags as incidence pairs $(p, L)$.
Define the $\FineSeven$ action by $\sigma_{(a,k)}: x \mapsto 2^k x + a
\pmod{7}$ for $(a,k) \in \Zcyc{7} \times \Zcyc{3}$.
Then $\FineSeven$ acts \emph{simply transitively} (regularly) on all~21
Fano incidence flags: for any two flags $f, f'$, there is a unique $g \in
\FineSeven$ with $g \cdot f = f'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Simple transitivity means $|\FineSeven| = 21$ equals the number of flags~21
and the action is both transitive (orbit = all flags) and free (no element
fixes a flag).
Geometrically, $\FineSeven$ is a Singer cycle complement that provides a
\emph{coordinate system} on the Fano plane: every flag is reachable from
any reference flag by a unique gauge transformation.
In GTE, this identifies $\FineSeven$ as the group of gauge configurations
over the $\mathbb{Z}_7$ field.
\end{remark}

\section{The Eisenstein Functor: \texorpdfstring{$\FineSeven$}{F21} and \texorpdfstring{$\AltFour$}{A4} from \texorpdfstring{$\Eisenstein$}{Z[omega]}}
\label{sec:eisenstein}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Eisenstein integers}

Let $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$ be a primitive cube root of unity.
The \textbf{Eisenstein integers} $\Eisenstein = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ form a
principal ideal domain with unit group $\{\pm 1, \pm\omega, \pm\omega^2\} \cong
\Zcyc{6}$ and norm $N(a + b\omega) = a^2 - ab + b^2$.
The factorization of a rational prime $q$ in $\Eisenstein$ depends on $q
\pmod{3}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $q \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$: \textbf{splits} as $q\Eisenstein = \pi\bar\pi$
    (two distinct conjugate primes of norm $q$).
  \item $q \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$: \textbf{inert} (stays prime in $\Eisenstein$,
    giving residue field $\GF{q^2}$).
  \item $q = 3$: \textbf{ramifies} as $3\Eisenstein = (1-\omega)^2 \cdot \epsilon$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The split prime 7 and \texorpdfstring{$\FineSeven$}{F21}}

Since $7 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, the prime~7 splits in $\Eisenstein$ as
$7\Eisenstein = \pi\bar\pi$ for a norm-7 Eisenstein prime $\pi$.
The residue ring $\Eisenstein/(\pi) \cong \GF{7}$, and the unit group
$(\Eisenstein/(\pi))^{\times} = \GF{7}^{\times} \cong \Zcyc{6}$.
The subgroup of cube roots of unity $\mu_3 = \{1, \omega, \omega^2\}$
reduces to the unique index-2 subgroup $\Zcyc{3} \leq \GF{7}^{\times}$,
generated by $2 \in \GF{7}^{\times}$ (the element of order~3).
Combined with the additive group $\GF{7}^+ = \Zcyc{7}$, this gives the
affine action of $\FineSeven = \Zcyc{7} \rtimes \Zcyc{3}$ on $\GF{7}$.

\subsection{The inert prime 2 and \texorpdfstring{$\AltFour$}{A4}}

Since $2 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$, the prime~2 is inert in $\Eisenstein$:
$2\Eisenstein$ remains a prime ideal, and the residue ring
$\Eisenstein/(2) \cong \GF{4}$, the field of four elements.

\begin{theorem}[\CatAL\ --- \lean{eisenstein\_a4\_from\_inert\_2}]
\label{thm:eisenstein_a4}
The Eisenstein residue at the inert prime 2 gives:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\Eisenstein/(2) \cong \GF{4}$; the additive group $(\GF{4},+) \cong
    \KleinFour = \Zcyc{2}^2$ and the multiplicative group $\GF{4}^{\times} \cong
    \Zcyc{3} = \mu_3$.
  \item The semidirect product $\KleinFour \rtimes \mu_3 \cong \AltFour$
    (the alternating group on 4 letters, order~12).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The $\mu_3 = \Zcyc{3}$ factor is shared between $\FineSeven$ (from the split
prime~7) and $\AltFour$ (from the inert prime~2).
The physical identification of this shared cyclic subgroup with
$\mathrm{SU}(2)_L^{\mathrm{cyc}}$ as the bridge between the GTE gauge skeleton
and the flavor sector is argued at level \CatD{}; see \S\ref{sec:physical}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Amalgamated structure and flavor embedding}

The two Eisenstein residues combine into an amalgamated product:
\begin{equation}
  G_{84} = \FineSeven \times_{\mu_3} \AltFour
  = (\Zcyc{7} \times \KleinFour) \rtimes \Zcyc{3},
  \quad |G_{84}| = 84.
\end{equation}
The value $|G_{84}| = 84 = |\PSLtwo|/2$ is half the automorphism count of the
Klein quartic.
A computational check (\CatA) confirms that $G_{84}$ does not embed in
$\mathrm{GL}(2,7)$ or $\PGLtwo$, so the flavor-gauge amalgamation is genuinely
a larger structure than the gauge group alone.

\subsection{GTE manifest flavor symmetry and \texorpdfstring{$\AltFour$}{A4}}

\begin{theorem}[\CatAL\ --- \lean{gte\_manifest\_flavor\_is\_s3\_in\_a4}]
\label{thm:s3_in_a4}
The GTE manifest neutrino flavor symmetries --- the generation cycling $\mu_3 =
\langle(1\,2\,3)\rangle$ and the $\mu$--$\tau$ exchange $U = (2\,3)$ --- generate
the symmetric group $S_3$ of order~6.
The group $S_3$ embeds in $\AltFour = \KleinFour \rtimes \mu_3$ as a complement
to the Klein normal subgroup $\KleinFour$, which is supplied by the Eisenstein
residue $\Eisenstein/(2)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:s3_in_a4} states a group-theoretic fact (\CatAL).
The identification of $\AltFour$ as the GTE leading-order neutrino flavor
symmetry yielding tri-bimaximal (TBM) mixing angles $\sin^2\theta_{12} = 1/3$,
$\sin^2\theta_{23} = 1/2$, $\theta_{13} = 0$ is an analytic derivation
(\CatAD) and is discussed in \S\ref{sec:physical}.
\end{remark}

\section{Klein Quartic Geometry and Three Generations}
\label{sec:klein}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Hurwitz's theorem and the \texorpdfstring{$(2,3,7)$}{(2,3,7)} triangle group}

\textbf{Hurwitz's theorem} states that a compact Riemann surface of genus $g \geq 2$
has at most $84(g-1)$ conformal automorphisms.
The bound is achieved if and only if the surface is a quotient of the hyperbolic
plane $\mathbb{H}$ by a torsion-free normal subgroup of the $(2,3,7)$ triangle group
$\Delta(2,3,7) = \langle x,y,z \mid x^2 = y^3 = z^7 = xyz = 1 \rangle$.

The \textbf{orbifold Euler characteristic} of $\Delta(2,3,7)$ is
\begin{equation}
  \chi_{\mathrm{orb}} = 1 - \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{7}
  = 1 - \frac{21}{42} - \frac{14}{42} - \frac{6}{42}
  = -\frac{1}{42}.
\end{equation}
This is the minimum possible orbifold area $\pi/21$ for a hyperbolic orbifold,
by Siegel's theorem.

\subsection{The Klein quartic}

The \textbf{Klein quartic} is the unique compact Riemann surface of genus~3
that attains the Hurwitz bound $84(g-1) = 168$ automorphisms, with
$\mathrm{Aut}(\text{Klein quartic}) \cong \PSLtwo$.
It can be defined as the smooth projective curve
$x^3y + y^3z + z^3x = 0$ in $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{C})$.

\begin{theorem}[\CatAL\ --- \lean{klein\_quartic\_genus\_eq\_n\_gen}]
\label{thm:klein_genus}
The orbifold covering gives:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Orbifold Euler characteristic: $\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}(\Delta(2,3,7)) = -1/42$
    (machine-verified by \texttt{norm\_num}).
  \item Surface Euler characteristic: $\chi(\text{Klein}) = |\PSLtwo| \times
    \chi_{\mathrm{orb}} = 168 \times (-1/42) = -4$
    (machine-verified by \texttt{norm\_num}).
  \item Genus formula: $g = (2 - \chi)/2 = (2 - (-4))/2 = 3 = \Ngen$
    (machine-verified by \texttt{norm\_num}).
\end{enumerate}
The Klein quartic is a Hurwitz surface with $|\mathrm{Aut}| = 84(g-1) = 168 =
|\PSLtwo|$ (saturation verified).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[\CatAD]
\label{cor:genus_ngen}
The genus of the unique Hurwitz surface whose automorphism group contains $\PSLtwo$
equals the number of Standard Model generations, $\Ngen = 3$.
This is a consequence of Theorems~\ref{thm:psl_aut} and~\ref{thm:klein_genus}
together with Hurwitz's theorem: $\PSLtwo$ is characterized by both
$\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{F}) = \PSLtwo$ (Fano plane automorphisms) and
$\mathrm{Aut}(\text{Klein quartic}) = \PSLtwo$, and the genus-3 surface is
the unique Hurwitz surface in genus 2 or higher with an automorphism group
isomorphic to $\PSLtwo$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
Corollary~\ref{cor:genus_ngen} does not in itself explain \emph{why} $\Ngen = 3$
physically — it shows that $\Ngen$ and the genus of the Klein quartic are the
same number (both equal to 3) and that this equality follows from the same
algebraic structure ($\PSLtwo$ and the $(2,3,7)$ triangle group) that underlies
the GTE framework.
The physical derivation of $\Ngen = 3$ from GTE first principles is given
in~\cite{SpivackGTEMobius,SpivackGTECompleteFramework}.
\end{remark}

\section{Physical Interpretation}
\label{sec:physical}
%=============================================================================

This section describes the proposed physical meaning of the algebraic results
proved above.
The group-theoretic statements are \CatAL{}; the physical interpretations carry
level \CatAD{} or \CatD{} as stated.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\FineSeven$}{F21} as gauge skeleton (\CatAD)}

In GTE, the cellular automaton update map on $\GF{7}^{\mathbb{Z}}$ generates
orbit families indexed by $\mathbb{Z}_7$ sector values.
The $\FineSeven$ action on $\GF{7}$ by $x \mapsto 2^k x + a$ is the gauge
freedom of the CMCA tape: it maps one valid tape configuration to another
without changing the physical content.
The identification $\FineSeven = \mathrm{Stab}_{\PSLtwo}(\infty)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:borel}) places this gauge group naturally inside the full
$\PSLtwo$ symmetry.

\subsection{Fano flags as a coordinate system (\CatAD)}

Theorem~\ref{thm:flag_regular} shows that $\FineSeven$ acts simply transitively
on the 21 Fano flags.
Since $|\FineSeven| = 21$ equals the number of flags, $\FineSeven$ provides a
\emph{bijection} between the abstract gauge group and the set of all Fano
incidence data.
This means that every consistent assignment of $\mathbb{Z}_7$ field values to
the three GTE roles (Left, Center, Right) corresponds to a unique flag, and the
21-element gauge orbit is the same as the 21-element flag set.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\AltFour$}{A4} and neutrino flavor symmetry (\CatAD)}

The Majorana neutrino mass matrix in GTE is constructed from the orbit of the
$\GF{7}$ polynomial under the combined Eisenstein residue symmetry.
The leading-order mixing is governed by $\AltFour = \KleinFour \rtimes \mu_3$,
where $\mu_3$ is the generation cycling symmetry (already manifest in GTE) and
$\KleinFour$ is the additional discrete symmetry supplied by the Eisenstein
residue at the inert prime~2.
The $\AltFour$ symmetry forces the \textbf{tri-bimaximal mixing} (TBM) pattern:
\begin{equation}
  \sin^2\theta_{12} = \frac{1}{3}, \quad
  \sin^2\theta_{23} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad
  \theta_{13} = 0
\end{equation}
at leading order~\cite{SpivackGTECompleteFramework}.
Corrections from breaking of $\AltFour \to S_3$ (the manifest part retained
in GTE) account for the observed nonzero $\theta_{13}$ (\CatD; open problem).

\subsection{Klein quartic and the geometry of three generations (\CatD)}

The identification $\mathrm{genus}(\text{Klein quartic}) = \Ngen = 3$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:klein_genus}) suggests a geometric interpretation: the three
Standard Model generations correspond to the three handles of the genus-3
Riemann surface that appears when the $(2,3,7)$ Hurwitz structure is realized
as a surface.
Whether this geometric correspondence has a direct physical realization in the
GTE CMCA dynamics is currently open (\CatD).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%=============================================================================

We have proved that the three principal algebraic structures of the GTE
framework --- the Fibonacci--Möbius map, the metacyclic gauge skeleton group
$\FineSeven$, and the Eisenstein integer arithmetic --- are all facets of a
single algebraic object: $\PSLtwo$, the unique simple group of order~168.

The certified results (\CatAL{}) are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\FineSeven$ is the Borel subgroup of $\PSLtwo$, and $\PSLtwo$ itself
    is generated by $\FineSeven$ together with the Fibonacci--Möbius map
    (Theorems~\ref{thm:borel} and~\ref{thm:pgl_gen}).
  \item $\PSLtwo \cong \mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{F})$ and $\FineSeven$ acts simply
    transitively on the 21 incidence flags of the Fano plane
    (Theorems~\ref{thm:psl_aut} and~\ref{thm:flag_regular}).
  \item The Eisenstein functor yields $\FineSeven$ and $\AltFour$ from the
    single ring $\Eisenstein$, with a shared $\mu_3$ bridge
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:eisenstein_a4}).
  \item The manifest GTE flavor symmetries $\mu_3$ and the $\mu$--$\tau$
    exchange generate $S_3 \leq \AltFour$
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:s3_in_a4}).
  \item $\mathrm{genus}(\text{Klein quartic}) = \Ngen = 3$
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:klein_genus}).
\end{enumerate}

The analytic corollaries (\CatAD{}) establish that the $\AltFour$ residue
symmetry from the Eisenstein functor is the leading-order GTE neutrino flavor
group completing manifest $S_3$ to the TBM-generating $\AltFour$, and that
the Klein quartic genus equals $\Ngen$ as a consequence of the $(2,3,7)$
covering structure.

\begin{openprob}
Derive the magnitude of the corrections to the TBM mixing pattern from the
breaking $\AltFour \to S_3$ within the GTE Eisenstein framework.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}
Determine whether the embedding $G_{84} = \FineSeven \times_{\mu_3} \AltFour$
can be realized as a physical symmetry group of the CMCA tape dynamics, or
whether it appears only as a structural artifact of the Eisenstein functor.
\end{openprob}

\begin{openprob}
Establish whether the Klein quartic has a direct realization in the GTE
geometric structure — for instance, as the moduli space of PSC-admissible
field configurations in genus~3, or as the quotient $\mathbb{H}/\Gamma$ for
some natural lattice $\Gamma$ arising from the $\GF{7}$ dynamics.
\end{openprob}
