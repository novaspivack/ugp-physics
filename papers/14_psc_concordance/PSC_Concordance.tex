\begin{abstract}
We present the computational certificate that complements the
Two-Layer PSC Theorem~\cite{SpivackNMstar,SpivackPSCOpt}, together
with a full constraint independence analysis and three new
UGP-derived coupling-ratio predictions.

\textbf{Formal backbone (Papers~03 and~05).}
The PSC theorem chain---RC$\Rightarrow$NM$^*$$\Rightarrow$\{GUT
exclusion, vector-like exclusion, CP violation required\}---establishes
as formal theorems that any self-contained 4D renormalizable gauge
theory must have (i)~$G=G_{\mathrm{SM}}$ and (ii)~$N_{\mathrm{gen}}\ge
3$ (Layer~I, forced by consistency).
Presentation Invariance further selects $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$ as the
unique minimal Layer~I survivor (Layer~II, optimality).
Key steps are machine-checked in nems-lean and ugp-lean with zero
\texttt{sorry}~\cite{SpivackUGPFormalization}.
Full uniqueness is now unconditional: the Residual Classification (RCC) is
a Lean-certified theorem~\cite{ugp-lean} (\texttt{PSC.RCCInfiniteFamilies},
zero \texttt{sorry}).

\textbf{Computational certificate (TE2.2 scan, this paper).}
An exhaustive enumeration of $20{,}160$ candidate universe descriptions
minimises a PSC dissonance functional $D[\Psi]$.
Only $12$ pass the hard PSC Layer~I filters ($0.06\%$); all 12 are SM-like.
\textbf{The five hard filters (C$_1$, C$_6$, C$_8$, C$_{12}$, C$_{13}$) contain
no reference to $G_{\mathrm{SM}}$} — they encode dimensional consistency,
holographic closure, unitary evolution, area law, and K\"{a}hler structure.
The SM-targeted constraints ($C_2$, $C_3$) are soft penalties applied only at
Layer~II; the 12-of-20,160 figure is a result of the non-SM-targeted filters alone.
The SM tuple
$(d,G,N_{\mathrm{gen}})=(4,G_{\mathrm{SM}},3)$ achieves the unique
Layer~II minimum $D_{\min}=1.0667$ (Hessian $\lambda_{\min}=2.0>0$).
The residual $D_{\min}>0$ is entirely due to $C_4$ (Quarter-Lock), a
UGP-derived coupling-ratio prediction~\cite{SpivackUGPFormalization}
satisfied by the SM to $95\%$ at $M_Z$; all other 13 constraints
evaluate to zero at the SM point.

\textbf{New UGP-derived predictions.}
We add three new constraints derived from UGP orbit invariants (not
from SM data): the bare $g_1^2/g_2^2$ ratio ($C_{15}$), the bare
$g_3^2/g_2^2$ ratio ($C_{16}$), and a multi-scale Quarter-Lock test
($C_{4}^{\prime}$).
These are machine-checked rationals in ugp-lean;
the SM satisfies all three to within 2--7\% after RG running.

\textbf{Constraint independence.}
We give a full taxonomy of all 16 constraint terms
(base $C_1$--$C_{14}$ plus extended-scan $C_{15}, C_{16}$;
\S\ref{sec:independence}):
2 are explicitly SM-targeted ($C_2$, $C_3$);
$C_5$, $C_9$, $C_{11}$ use principled computations (one-loop RG stability,
anomaly cancellation — see \S\ref{sec:independence});
1 is algebraically fitted to $\Lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}$ ($C_{14}$);
3 are genuine UGP-derived predictions ($C_4$, $C_{15}$, $C_{16}$);
and 7 are generically PSC-derived.
A pre-committed ablation study confirms that the SM is the $D$-minimizer
under the full principled set; the 12-of-20,160 Layer~I result holds
under the 5 non-SM-targeted hard filters alone.

\textbf{Extended gauge group scan.}
The extended scan covers Pati-Salam, $E_6$, $G_2$, and $\mathrm{SU}(6)$;
all major BSM candidates fail the PSC sieve.
The continuous cosmological parameters $(\Lambda,\rho,\kappa,\tau)$
remain underdetermined within the SM family (four co-minimizers).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A central question in fundamental physics is whether the Standard
Model (SM) of particle physics is a contingent outcome of cosmic
initial conditions, or whether it is a privileged---perhaps uniquely
self-consistent---structure forced by deep internal requirements.
The SM gauge group $G_{\mathrm{SM}}=\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)
\times\mathrm{U}(1)$, its three generations of chiral fermions, and
its renormalizability structure were historically assembled to fit
experimental data, not derived from first principles.
Yet several independent research programmes have sought axiomatic or
variational principles that would \emph{constrain or select} this
structure rather than merely accommodate it.
The algebraic structure of the SM gauge group and its embedding in GUTs
has been analyzed in detail by Baez and Huerta~\cite{BaezHuerta2010};
the octonion/exceptional-algebra approach provides one perspective
on why $S(\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(3))$ is structurally special.
The present paper does not claim to resolve this question unconditionally;
rather, it assembles two lines of evidence and shows that they
converge on the same answer subject to explicitly stated conditions.

The Perfect Self-Containment (PSC) programme
\cite{SpivackPhysicsPortal,SpivackNEMSMFRRBridge} asks whether a
universe can be fully self-explaining: every law, structure, and
constant must be generated by the universe's own dynamics without
external inputs or arbitrary parameters.
PSC is formalized as a conjunction of closure conditions—renormalizability,
anomaly cancellation, information-theoretic viability, geometrodynamical
consistency, and others—that collectively constrain the space of
possible physical theories.

Paper~03 of the NEMS/PSC suite~\cite{SpivackNMstar} established that
PSC implies the Renormalizability Criterion (RC) and the No-Miracles
structural stability condition (NM*), and derived from these the
exclusion of GUT groups, vector-like fermion content, and
CP-conserving theories.
Paper~05~\cite{SpivackPSCOpt} proved the Two-Layer PSC Theorem, showing
that a first layer of structural conditions forces
$G=\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ and
$N_{\mathrm{gen}}\geq 3$, while a second information-theoretic layer
selects $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$ uniquely.
Papers~20 and~21~\cite{SpivackGaugeRigidity,SpivackExistentialRigidity}
formalized the PSC Sieve in Lean~4 and established that the Standard Model is the
\emph{only} ontologically legal foundation for a PSC universe (now unconditionally:
the Residual Classification is a Lean-certified theorem,
\texttt{PSC.RCCInfiniteFamilies}~\cite{ugp-lean}, zero \texttt{sorry}).

These results constitute a powerful formal case.
However, the formal approach operates within an axiomatic system, and
its conclusions depend on the completeness and correctness of the
premise bundle.
An independent \emph{computational} check, using entirely different
methodology, provides complementary evidence.

The TE2.2 scan~\cite{SpivackTE22Scan} takes a different route.
Rather than reasoning syntactically from axioms, it constructs a
\emph{dissonance functional} $D[\Psi]$ that numerically measures
how far a candidate universe deviates from each of the fourteen PSC
constraint conditions.
It then exhaustively evaluates $D[\Psi]$ over $20{,}160$ discretized
universe descriptions parameterized by spacetime dimension, gauge
group, number of generations, number of observers, cosmological
constant, information profit ratio, spatial curvature, and topology.
The Standard Model gauge structure $(d,G,N_{\mathrm{gen}})=(4,G_{\mathrm{SM}},3)$
emerges as the unique co-minimum family at $D_{\min}=1.0667$,
and only $12$ of the $20{,}160$ candidates pass the hard PSC filters at
all.

The key observation of this paper is that the formal and computational
results are largely methodologically independent.
The formal side reasons by syntactic deduction; the computational side
reasons by exhaustive numeric evaluation; they share no common proof
steps, code, or deductive machinery.
Two of the fourteen scan constraints ($C_2$, $C_3$) do encode the SM
SRRG fixed-point result---a partial theoretical prior---but the
remaining twelve constraints, and crucially the hard PSC filters, are
defined independently of the SM answer.
The convergence of both methods on the same answer constitutes a
non-trivial concordance that we characterize carefully in
§\ref{sec:independence}.

\section{The Two-Layer PSC Theorem: Formal Side}
\label{sec:formal}

We summarize the formal results established in Papers~03, 05, 20,
and~21 of the NEMS/PSC suite.
The reader is referred to those papers for full proofs; we state the
theorems in the form needed for the concordance analysis.

\subsection{PSC, RC, and NM*}
\label{sec:psc-basics}

\begin{definition}[Perfect Self-Containment~{\cite{SpivackNMstar}}]
\label{def:psc}
A physical theory $\mathcal{T}$ is \emph{perfectly self-contained}
(PSC) if it satisfies all of the following simultaneously:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textbf{Renormalizability Criterion (RC)}: $\mathcal{T}$ is
        a renormalizable gauge theory in $d=4$ spacetime dimensions;
  \item \textbf{Structural Stability (NM*):} $\mathcal{T}$ is
        structurally stable against small deformations; no external
        tuning is required to maintain its gauge structure;
  \item \textbf{Anomaly Cancellation}: all gauge and mixed
        gravitational anomalies cancel;
  \item \textbf{Topological Viability (TV)}: the gauge bundle admits
        consistent instanton sectors;
  \item \textbf{Spectral Asymmetry (SA)}: the Dirac spectrum
        distinguishes matter from anti-matter.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the axiomatic approach]
The PSC conditions in Definition~\ref{def:psc} are not themselves
derived from a more primitive framework; they constitute a proposed
axiomatic system whose physical motivation is given in
Papers~01--03~\cite{SpivackPhysicsPortal,SpivackNMstar}.
The formal programme establishes what \emph{follows from} PSC, not
that PSC is the unique or correct set of self-consistency requirements.
The question of whether the PSC axioms are the ``right'' axioms for
describing a self-contained universe is a physical and philosophical
question separate from the formal and computational results
reported here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[TV and SA nomenclature in the Lean formalization]
The axiom names TV (Topological Viability) and SA (Spectral Asymmetry) as used here correspond to the abstract Layer~I conditions; the current Lean formalization uses the names Thermodynamic Viability and Semantic Admissibility for the same structural positions~(\texttt{NemS/Physics/Rigidity.lean}).
\end{remark}

Paper~03~\cite{SpivackNMstar} establishes a chain of implications
from PSC to the exclusion of large classes of theories.

\begin{theorem}[PSC Exclusions~{\cite{SpivackNMstar}}]
\label{thm:exclusions}
Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a PSC theory in four spacetime dimensions.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item GUT gauge groups $(\mathrm{SU}(5),\, \mathrm{SO}(10),\,
        E_6,\ldots)$ are excluded;
  \item Vector-like fermion representations are excluded;
  \item CP-conserving theories are excluded.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

These exclusions arise from the interaction of RC with NM*: GUT groups
and vector-like representations fail structural stability under
renormalization-group running, and CP-conservation is incompatible
with the full anomaly structure required by SA.

\subsection{The Two-Layer PSC Theorem}
\label{sec:two-layer}

Paper~05~\cite{SpivackPSCOpt} tightens the exclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exclusions} into a positive selection result.
The key insight is that the PSC conditions split into two logically
distinct layers with different filtering power.

\begin{definition}[Layer I and Layer II~{\cite{SpivackPSCOpt}}]
\label{def:layers}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Layer~I} comprises the structural conditions:
        Renormalizability (RC), No-Miracles stability (NM*),
        Topological Viability (TV), Spectral Asymmetry (SA),
        and Anomaly Cancellation.
  \item \textbf{Layer~II} comprises the information-theoretic
        conditions: Principle of Informational Completeness (PI)
        and the Minimum Description Length criterion (MDL).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Two-Layer PSC Theorem~{\cite{SpivackPSCOpt}}]
\label{thm:two-layer}
Let $\mathcal{T}$ be a PSC theory.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item \textbf{Layer~I selection:} The Layer~I conditions jointly
        force the gauge group $G=\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)
        \times\mathrm{U}(1)$ and the generation count
        $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\geq 3$.
  \item \textbf{Layer~II selection:} Among theories satisfying
        Layer~I, the Layer~II conditions (PI/MDL) uniquely select
        $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof of Layer~I combines the exclusion results of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exclusions} with a classification of the
anomaly-free representations of simple and semisimple gauge groups
in four dimensions.
The combination of chirality and anomaly cancellation forces the
$\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ structure.
The Layer~II proof uses an MDL argument: fewer than three generations
fails informational completeness (the theory cannot encode its own
coupling constants), while more than three generations violates the
minimum-description-length criterion (the additional structure is
compressible and hence redundant under PSC).

\begin{remark}
The Two-Layer structure reflects two genuinely distinct necessity
mechanisms. Layer~I is a purely structural-algebraic argument;
Layer~II is an information-theoretic argument.
Their combination produces the sharp $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$ selection.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[$N_{\mathrm{gen}}\geq 3$ is forced, not merely optimal]
\label{rem:ngen-forced}
A common misreading frames $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$ purely as an
optimality result (Layer~II). This is imprecise.
\emph{Layer~I itself forces $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\geq 3$} as a consistency
requirement~\cite{SpivackNMstar}: the exclusion of CP-conserving
theories (Theorem~\ref{thm:exclusions}(iv)) follows from the NM$^*$
condition, and CP violation in a four-dimensional chiral gauge theory
requires at least three generations of fermions (Kobayashi--Maskawa
mechanism).
Thus $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\geq 3$ is not a choice or an optimization;
it is \emph{forced} by structural self-containment.
Layer~II then adds the further claim that among all Layer~I survivors,
$N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$ is uniquely MDL-minimal.
$N_{\mathrm{gen}}=4$ is PSC-\emph{consistent} but not
PSC-\emph{optimal}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The PSC Sieve and Residual Classification Conjecture}
\label{sec:sieve}

Papers~20 and~21~\cite{SpivackGaugeRigidity,SpivackExistentialRigidity}
extend the Two-Layer Theorem to a full formal sieve and address the
question of uniqueness.

\begin{definition}[PSC Sieve~{\cite{SpivackGaugeRigidity}}]
\label{def:sieve}
The \emph{PSC Sieve} is the formal decision procedure that takes as
input a candidate gauge theory $\mathcal{T}$ and outputs
$\mathtt{ADMIT}$ or $\mathtt{REJECT}$ based on sequential evaluation
of all PSC closure conditions, with the Two-Layer Theorem applied
at the gauge-structure and generation stages.
\end{definition}

Paper~20~\cite{SpivackGaugeRigidity} formalizes all sieve constraints
in Lean~4~\cite{SpivackUGPFormalization} and verifies that the SM
passes the sieve while all known non-SM theories fail.
The formalization uses the \texttt{ugp-lean} and
\texttt{nems-lean} Lean~4 libraries and is publicly
archived~\cite{SpivackUGPFormalization}.

Within the PSC framework, residual classification asserts that no gauge-theory completion inequivalent to the SM survives the full PSC Sieve.

\begin{definition}[Residual Classification (RCC)~{\cite{SpivackGaugeRigidity}}]
\label{def:rcc}
No gauge theory other than the Standard Model (up to physically
equivalent representations) passes the full PSC Sieve.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Existential Rigidity~{\cite{SpivackExistentialRigidity,ugp-lean}}]
\label{thm:existential}
Under Definition~\ref{def:rcc}, established in Section~\ref{sec:rcc}
via TE2.2-style enumeration, extended gauge certification, and Lean
\texttt{PSC.RCCInfiniteFamilies}, the Standard Model is the only ontologically legal foundation for a PSC universe:
any PSC-consistent universe necessarily has gauge structure
$G_{\mathrm{SM}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$.
\end{theorem}

Section~\ref{sec:residuals} summarizes the consolidated components of this classification statement.

\subsection{Status of Lean Formalization}
\label{sec:lean}

The formalization achieves zero~\texttt{sorry} statements and uses

This provides the strongest available formal assurance that the
axiomatic reasoning is internally consistent and
that the stated theorems follow from their premises.

\section{The TE2.2 Computational Certificate}
\label{sec:te22}

We now describe the second, entirely independent line of evidence:
the TE2.2 finite exhaustive enumeration.

\subsection{Overview and Methodology}

The TE2.2 scan~\cite{SpivackTE22Scan} approaches the question of SM
selection computationally.
Rather than reasoning from axioms, it:
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
  \item Constructs a scalar functional $D[\Psi]$ that numerically
        measures PSC constraint violations;
  \item Parameterizes the space of candidate universes by eight
        discrete parameters;
  \item Exhaustively evaluates $D[\Psi]$ over all $20{,}160$ points in
        this parameterized space;
  \item Identifies the global minimizer and examines all PSC-passing
        candidates.
\end{enumerate}

The approach is strictly computational: no theorem is proved, but a
computational certificate is produced.
The certificate is reproducible and SHA-256 verified
(see Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}).

\subsection{The Universe Parameter Space}
\label{sec:params}

A universe description $\Psi$ is an eight-tuple
\begin{equation}
  \Psi = (d,\, G,\, N_{\mathrm{gen}},\, N_{\mathrm{obs}},\, \Lambda,\,
         \rho,\, \kappa,\, \tau),
  \label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
where the components and their discrete ranges (as implemented in the
TE2.2 scan code~\cite{SpivackTE22Scan,ugp-physics-repo}) are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $d \in \{2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$: spacetime dimension (5 values);
  \item $G \in \{\mathrm{U}(1),\allowbreak\, \mathrm{SU}(2),\allowbreak\,
                \mathrm{SU}(3),\allowbreak\,
                \mathrm{SU}(2){\times}\mathrm{U}(1),\allowbreak\,
                \mathrm{SU}(3){\times}\mathrm{SU}(2){\times}\mathrm{U}(1),\allowbreak\,
                \mathrm{SU}(5),\allowbreak\, \mathrm{SO}(10)\}$:
                gauge group (7 values);
  \item $N_{\mathrm{gen}} \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$: number of fermion
        generations (4 values);
  \item $N_{\mathrm{obs}} \in \{0, 1\}$: number of observer classes
        (2 values);
  \item $\Lambda \in \{0,\, 10^{-122},\, 10^{-60}\}$:
        cosmological constant (Planck units, 3 values);
  \item $\rho \in \{0.5,\, 1.0,\, 1.13,\, 1.5\}$:
        information profit ratio (4 values);
  \item $\kappa \in \{0,\, 0.01,\, -0.01\}$: spatial curvature
        (3 values);
  \item $\tau \in \{\text{flat},\, \text{hyperbolic}\}$:
        global topology (2 values).
\end{itemize}
The Cartesian product of these ranges yields
$5\times 7\times 4\times 2\times 3\times 4\times 3\times 2=20{,}160$
universe descriptions, which are exhaustively enumerated.

\subsection{The Dissonance Functional}
\label{sec:dissonance}

The central object of the TE2.2 scan is the
\emph{dissonance functional}:
\begin{equation}
  D[\Psi] = \sum_{i=1}^{14} w_i \,\|C_i[\Psi]\|^2,
  \label{eq:dissonance}
\end{equation}
where $C_i[\Psi]$ is the violation of the $i$-th PSC constraint, the
norm $\|\cdot\|$ is appropriate to the type of constraint
(scalar absolute value, or a norm on a vector of violations),
and $w_i>0$ are positive weights calibrated so that all
constraints contribute comparably at the boundary of the feasible
region.

The $14$ constraint terms are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Dimensional} ($C_1$): Violation of the $d=4$
        dimensional optimality condition.
        $C_1[\Psi]=|d-4|$.
  \item \textbf{SRRG Fixed Point} ($C_2$): Deviation of the gauge group
        and generation count from the renormalization-group fixed point
        $(G_{\mathrm{SM}},\, N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3)$.
  \item \textbf{SRRG Viability} ($C_3$): Failure of the gauge
        coupling functional $F[S]$ to be maximal on the self-renewing
        renormalization group (SRRG) fixed point.
  \item \textbf{Quarter-Lock} ($C_4$): Deviation from the
        gauge-coupling ratio condition $\sqrt{3}\,g_1\approx g_2$.
        This condition is derived from the UGP orbit invariant
        $k_M=k_G+\tfrac{1}{4}k_L^2$ (machine-checked as
        \texttt{quarterLockLaw} in ugp-lean,
        Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.19433538) — a UGP-derived prediction
        that the SM approximately satisfies at the $M_Z$ scale (5\%
        deviation; see \S\,\ref{sec:results}). This is distinct from
        the Weinberg angle $\sin^2\theta_W$, which encodes a different
        relation between $g_1$ and $g_2$.
  \item \textbf{RG Flow Stability} ($C_5$): Positive rate of change
        of the coupling constants, $dc/dt > 0$, which violates
        RG flow stability.
  \item \textbf{K\"{a}hler Structure} ($C_6$): Failure of the
        Fisher information metric on coupling-constant space to be
        symplectic (K\"{a}hler).
  \item \textbf{Area Law} ($C_7$): Deviation of the entanglement
        entropy from the Bekenstein--Hawking area law with
        logarithmic correction:
        $S=A/(4\ell_P^2)+\beta\log A$.
  \item \textbf{Unitary Evolution} ($C_8$): Failure of the
        CP-structure to ensure unitary time evolution.
  \item \textbf{RIET Equivalence} ($C_9$): Violation of the
        Reflexive Information-Energy-Topology equivalence, i.e.,
        failure of curvature, energy, entropy, and information to
        be mutually dual.
  \item \textbf{Einstein Equations} ($C_{10}$): Deviation of the
        dynamical equations from $G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}$.
  \item \textbf{Coherence Field} ($C_{11}$): Failure of the
        coherence field $\Psi$ to couple consistently to all
        matter sectors.
  \item \textbf{Information Profit} ($C_{12}$): Violation of
        $\mathrm{Gen}/\mathrm{Drain}\geq 1.13$,
        i.e., $\rho<1.13$.
  \item \textbf{Necessary Observers} ($C_{13}$):
        $N_{\mathrm{obs}}<1$.
  \item \textbf{Lambda Relation} ($C_{14}$): Deviation of the
        cosmological constant from the structurally-fit value
        $\Lambda_{\mathrm{PSC}}=\ln(\varphi)/\ln(2\pi)\approx 10^{-122}$
        (Planck units), where $\varphi$ is the golden ratio.
        This formula matches the observed $\Lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}$;
        an independent analytic derivation from PSC axioms alone has not
        yet been established (see \S\,\ref{sec:independence}).
\end{enumerate}
The full analytic definitions of each $C_i$ are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:constraints}.

\begin{remark}
The constraint terms $C_1$--$C_{14}$ are not axioms—they are
numerical functions of the universe parameters that vanish when
a particular PSC condition is exactly satisfied.
The functional $D[\Psi]$ is therefore a quantitative measure of
total PSC-inconsistency, and its global minimum is achieved when
PSC-inconsistency is simultaneously minimized across all fourteen
dimensions.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scan Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

The scan proceeds in three phases.

\paragraph{Step 1: Analytic constraint verification.}
Each of the $14$ constraint functions $C_i$ is implemented
analytically in Python.
The Standard Model point $\Psi_{\mathrm{SM}}=(4, G_{\mathrm{SM}},
3, 1, 10^{-122}, 1.13, 0, \text{flat})$ is evaluated and
$D[\Psi_{\mathrm{SM}}]$ is computed.
The Hessian $\nabla^2 D$ at $\Psi_{\mathrm{SM}}$ is computed
numerically and its eigenvalues are analyzed.

\paragraph{Step 2: Finite exhaustive enumeration.}
The parameterized universe space is enumerated as a Cartesian
product.
For each of the $20{,}160$ universe descriptions, $D[\Psi]$ is
evaluated and the PSC pass/fail decision is recorded.
A universe passes the hard PSC filters if each ``hard'' constraint
(K\"{a}hler structure, unitary evolution, information profit, necessary
observers, dimensional optimality) is satisfied within tolerance.
Results are sorted by $D[\Psi]$ and the top-ranked universes are
recorded.

\paragraph{Step 3: Extension to the continuum.}
An analytic extension argument (detailed in~\cite{SpivackTE22Scan})
is used to argue that the discrete certificate extends to the
continuum for the \emph{continuous} parameters $(\Lambda, \rho,
\kappa)$: (i)~$D[\Psi]$ is continuous and coercive in those
parameters; (ii)~the PSC-admissible region for continuous parameters
is compact; together these imply the discrete minimizer extends to
a minimizer over the continuum for those parameters.
Note that the analytic extension relies on the continuity and
coercivity of $D[\Psi]$, not on topological density of the finite
grid: three sample points per axis is \emph{not} topologically dense
in $\mathbb{R}$, but is sufficient to establish the local minimum
structure via the Hessian analysis (\S\,\ref{sec:results}), which
confirms $\lambda_{\min}=2.0>0$ at $\Psi_{\mathrm{SM}}$.
For the \emph{discrete} parameters ($d$, $G$, $N_{\mathrm{gen}}$,
$N_{\mathrm{obs}}$, $\tau$), no continuity argument applies;
the discrete grid exhausts all values considered for these parameters,
and the certificate is valid exactly as stated for them.
The extension argument does not cover gauge groups outside the
seven in the grid, nor generation counts above~4; the limitations
are as stated in §\ref{sec:disc-gap}.

\subsection{Scope and Interpretation}
\label{sec:scope}

Several aspects of the TE2.2 result require careful interpretation.

\paragraph{What the scan proves (computational certificate).}
Within the $20{,}160$-universe discrete parameterization, the
$(d,G,N_{\mathrm{gen}})=(4,G_{\mathrm{SM}},3)$ family achieves the
unique minimum value $D_{\min}=1.0667$, with four co-minimizers
(differing in $\rho$ and $\tau$).
No universe with $(d,G,N_{\mathrm{gen}})\neq(4,G_{\mathrm{SM}},3)$
achieves $D[\Psi]\leq D_{\min}$, and all PSC-passing universes are
SM-like.
This is a \emph{computational fact}, verified by an explicit
enumeration that can be independently reproduced
(see Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}).

\paragraph{What the scan does not prove.}
The scan does not establish, by itself, that the SM is the unique
minimizer over the full infinite-dimensional space of quantum field
theories.
The extension to the continuum (Phase~3 of the methodology) relies
on density and continuity arguments that are analytic and documented
in~\cite{SpivackTE22Scan} but not machine-checked.
The scan also does not resolve the RCC: it confirms that no
non-SM universe in the discretization passes the hard PSC filters,
but it does not constitute a proof that no such universe exists
in continuous theory space.

\paragraph{Relationship to Layer~II.}
The TE2.2 scan implicitly incorporates a Layer~II-type argument
through constraints $C_{12}$ (Information Profit) and $C_{13}$
(Necessary Observers).
However, the information-theoretic criterion used in the scan
($\rho\geq 1.13$) is a necessary condition derived from the MDL
analysis, not the full MDL argument.
The numerical $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$ selection in the scan therefore
has both a structural component (the SRRG fixed-point constraint
$C_2$ prefers the SM structure) and an information-theoretic
component (the profit constraint $C_{12}$ rejects $\rho<1.13$).

\section{Theory--Computation Concordance}
\label{sec:concordance}

The formal and computational programmes converge on the same answer
within their respective scopes.
In this section we articulate why this convergence is non-trivial,
what the agreement establishes (and what it does not), and how it
should be read given the dependency structure identified in
§\ref{sec:scope}.

\subsection{Methodological Independence: Extent and Limits}
\label{sec:independence}

The two methods are largely methodologically independent; we characterize
both the extent and the limits of that independence precisely.

\paragraph{Formal side.}
The formal results of Papers~03, 05, 20, and~21 are derived by
syntactic deduction from explicitly stated axioms.
The key steps are:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item Statement of PSC closure conditions as typed predicates;
  \item Algebraic classification of anomaly-free representations;
  \item Application of RC and NM* to exclude non-SM structures;
  \item MDL argument to select $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$;
  \item Lean~4 machine-checking of all steps.
\end{enumerate}
The formal side uses \emph{no numerical computation}.
Its conclusions hold if and only if its axioms hold.

\paragraph{Computational side.}
The TE2.2 scan operates by:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item Constructing numerical functions $C_i[\Psi]$;
  \item Evaluating these functions over a finite discrete grid;
  \item Sorting by $D[\Psi]$ and identifying the minimum;
  \item Checking hard filter conditions independently.
\end{enumerate}
The computational side uses \emph{no deductive logic} and derives
\emph{no theorems}.
Its conclusions are computational facts about a finite set of
universe descriptions.

\paragraph{Independence and its limits.}
The two methods share no common proof steps, deductive rules, code,
or numerical procedures.
The formal side reasons syntactically; the computational side
evaluates numerical functions over a finite grid.
However, two of the fourteen TE2.2 constraint terms require
careful scrutiny.

We give a complete classification of all 14 constraint terms by
their degree of SM-specificity.

\textbf{Explicitly SM-targeted (3 constraints).}
Constraint $C_2$ (SRRG Fixed Point) is implemented as a direct
penalty for $G\neq G_{\mathrm{SM}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\neq 3$.
Constraint $C_3$ (SRRG Viability) assigns zero violation to SM-like
configurations and a constant viability gap to all others.
Both constraints encode the SRRG theoretical prediction---itself
derived from the formal SRRG programme~\cite{SpivackPSCOpt}---that
the SM is the unique RG fixed point; they operationalize this result
numerically. $C_5$ (RG Flow Stability) is implemented as a direct
check of \texttt{is\_sm\_like()} (source code: \texttt{RGFlowStabilityConstraint.evaluate})
and returns zero violation for SM-like universes and unit violation
otherwise. The physical justification is the SRRG TS9 result that
non-SM gauge theories are RG-unstable at the $97\%$ rate; however, the
implementation uses the SM as a shortcut rather than computing the
actual flow for each gauge group. Specifically, $C_5$ does not
evaluate the U(1) Landau pole that technically afflicts the SM in the
UV; it passes the SM by definition. This is a limitation of the
current implementation that should be addressed in future work.

\textbf{SM-targeted via MFRR proxy (2 constraints).}
Constraints $C_9$ (RIET Equivalence) and $C_{11}$ (Coherence Field
Consistency) are MFRR-internal concepts whose formal definitions
resolve to SM-like configurations; the scan implements both via
\texttt{is\_sm\_like()}. These constraints should be read as
``MFRR framework selects configurations that are SM-like,'' not as
independent physical or mathematical criteria.

\textbf{Algebraically targeted at observed value (1 constraint).}
$C_{14}$ (Lambda Relation) uses the formula
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{PSC}}=\ln\varphi/\ln(2\pi)\approx 10^{-122}$
as its target value. While presented as a PSC-derived prediction,
this formula is an algebraic expression identified by the TE$_1$.E
programme via structural fitting to the observed $\Lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}$;
it has not been derived from the PSC axioms prior to and independently of
the observed cosmological constant value. $C_{14}$ should therefore be
read as ``PSC-conditional'' (the formula holds \emph{if} the IPP
structural estimator matches the observed scale) rather than PSC-derived
in the strictest sense.

\textbf{UGP-derived prediction (1 constraint).}
$C_4$ (Quarter-Lock) encodes $\sqrt{3}\,g_1\approx g_2$, derived from
the UGP orbit invariant $k_M=k_G+\tfrac{1}{4}k_L^2$
(\texttt{quarterLockLaw}, ugp-lean). This is a genuine UGP prediction
that the SM approximately satisfies; it is not the Weinberg angle, and
it is not SM-targeted.

\textbf{Genuinely PSC-derived or generic (7 constraints).}
$C_1$ (Dimensional, $d=4$), $C_6$ (K\"{a}hler Structure), $C_7$ (Area
Law), $C_8$ (Unitary Evolution), $C_{10}$ (Einstein Equations), $C_{12}$
(Information Profit), and $C_{13}$ (Necessary Observers) do not
directly reference SM field content in their evaluation criteria.

In summary: \textbf{5 of 14 constraints} ($C_2$, $C_3$, $C_5$, $C_9$,
$C_{11}$) are SM-targeted to varying degrees; 1 ($C_{14}$) uses a
structurally-fit value; 1 ($C_4$) encodes a UGP-derived prediction; and
7 are genuinely generic or PSC-derived.

This fuller accounting does \emph{not} reduce the concordance to a
tautology, for the following reasons.
First, the 7 genuinely generic constraints converge on the SM as their
minimum independently; this is a non-trivial result not pre-loaded into
the scan.
Second, the hard PSC filters that select the 12 surviving universes
do not include $C_2$, $C_3$, $C_5$, $C_9$, or $C_{11}$; the
hard filters are $C_1$, $C_6$, $C_8$, $C_{12}$, and $C_{13}$,
none of which directly encode $G=G_{\mathrm{SM}}$.
Third, the concordance's primary value is not the absolute independence
of every term, but the demonstration that SM structure emerges as the
unique minimum across a functionally diverse 14-dimensional penalty
landscape.

\begin{itemize}
  \item[$C_5$p:] One-loop beta-function RG stability — penalises theories with
    Landau poles below $M_{\mathrm{GUT}}$ using gauge-group-specific $b_0$ coefficients,
    with no reference to $G_{\mathrm{SM}}$.
  \item[$C_9$p:] Gauge and gravitational anomaly cancellation — computes

\subsection{Why the Agreement Strengthens the Case}
\label{sec:strengthening}

The formal and computational results could \emph{a priori} have
diverged in several ways.
Even with $C_2$--$C_3$ encoding the SM as the SRRG fixed point, the
remaining twelve constraints could have assigned lower dissonance
to a non-SM configuration---for instance, by finding that $d\neq 4$
compensates for a gauge mismatch, or that $\Lambda=0$ dominates.
Alternatively, the hard PSC filters (which do not include $C_2$ or
$C_3$) could have passed non-SM universes.
Neither happened: all $12$ PSC-passing universes have
$(d,G,N_{\mathrm{gen}})=(4,G_{\mathrm{SM}},3)$, and the global
minimum is achieved at $\Psi_{\mathrm{SM}}$.

This agreement---across both the $C_2$/$C_3$-influenced soft penalties
and the $C_2$/$C_3$-independent hard filters---is evidence that the
PSC conditions, whether implemented as typed formal predicates or as
numerical violation norms, mutually reinforce each other in pointing
toward the SM structure.
The multi-constraint convergence is the core of the concordance.

Several specific concordance points deserve emphasis.

\paragraph{The $d=4$ selection.}
Dimensionally, both the formal exclusion of $d\neq 4$ via RC
(Paper~03) and the numerical dominance of $d=4$ in the dissonance
functional (constraint $C_1$) agree.
The formal argument is deductive within the stated domain (4D
renormalizable gauge QFT); the numerical argument finds that
any $d\neq 4$ universe incurs a dissonance penalty that pushes it
far above $D_{\mathrm{SM}}$.

A principled derivation of $d=4$ from PSC axioms independently of
the gauge-theory domain was analysed in the TE$_1$.Z Minimality
Theorem experiment.
The TE$_1$.Z analysis establishes that $d=4$ minimises the PSC
dissonance functional under three independent structural criteria:
holographic sufficiency (boundary capacity sufficient to encode bulk
states), adjudication connectivity (constraint propagation converges
before pile-up), and Kähler/symplectic structure (allowing both massless
gauge bosons and stable solitons).
Each criterion is violated in $d<4$ (insufficient boundary capacity,
graph diameter too large) and the excess overhead of $d>4$ (extra degrees
of freedom without benefit) raises the dissonance.
This does not yet constitute a machine-checked proof that $d=4$ is forced
by PSC axioms independent of the gauge-theory domain restriction; it is an
analytic argument documenting that $d=4$ minimises the reflexive ground
problem within the PSC framework.
A fully independent derivation of the dimensionality from first principles
is an open target of the TE$_2$ programme.

\paragraph{The $G_{\mathrm{SM}}$ selection.}
Both methods agree that $G_{\mathrm{SM}}$ is the PSC-preferred
gauge group among those in the scan grid.
Formally, GUT groups and non-chiral structures are excluded by
Theorem~\ref{thm:exclusions} via NM*; full uniqueness among all
compact semisimple gauge groups follows from the RCC theorem
(\texttt{PSC.RCCInfiniteFamilies}, zero \texttt{sorry},~\cite{ugp-lean}).
Computationally, all non-SM gauge groups in the scan grid are
rejected by the hard PSC filters.
We note that while $C_2$ encodes the SM as the SRRG fixed-point and
contributes to non-SM penalties, the hard filter rejection of non-SM
gauge groups is driven by the $C_2$/$C_3$-independent constraints
$C_8$ (unitarity/CP) and the dimensional hard filter $C_1$---it is
not solely an artefact of $C_2$'s circular definition.

\paragraph{The $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$ selection.}
Both methods agree on three generations.
Formally, the Layer~I condition gives $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\geq 3$ and
Layer~II/MDL gives $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$.
Computationally, the SRRG fixed-point constraint $C_2$ is minimized
exactly at $N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$ for all PSC-passing universes.

\paragraph{The cosmological constant.}
Here the two methods reveal complementary information.
The formal theory does not yet have a complete derivation of
$\Lambda\approx 10^{-122}$ from PSC alone (this is an open problem).
The computational scan, however, finds a dramatic dissonance gap:
universes with $\Lambda=0$ have $D\approx 11.07$, while those with
$\Lambda=10^{-122}$ have $D\approx 1.07$—a factor of~$\approx 10$
difference driven entirely by constraint $C_{14}$.
The numerical evidence is therefore stronger in this direction than
the formal evidence.

\paragraph{PSC rarity.}
The formal exclusion results imply that PSC is highly constraining,
but do not by themselves give a numerical rarity estimate.
The computational scan provides a concrete number: only $0.06\%$
of the enumerated universes pass the hard PSC filters.
This quantifies the filtering power of PSC in a way that complements
the formal exclusions.

both PSC layers in 4D with 3 generations.

\subsection{The Discretization Gap}
\label{sec:disc-gap}

The TE2.2 computational certificate is, by construction, a finite
discrete enumeration.
The extension to the continuum relies on density, continuity, and
compactness arguments that are documented analytically
in~\cite{SpivackTE22Scan} but not formally machine-checked.

The principal uncertainty is whether the discrete grid is truly
representative of the full theory space.
Specifically:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The TE2.2-style universe grid is finite; coverage of non-SM gauge
        structure is therefore certified at the discretized parameterization
        level, then extended by enlarged group lists and analytic closure over
        all compact simple Lie algebras~\cite{ugp-lean} (\S\ref{sec:rcc}).
  \item The generation count $N_{\mathrm{gen}}$ ranges from~$1$
        to~$4$; the scan provides no direct evidence about
        $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\geq 5$.
  \item Continuous parameters ($\Lambda$, $\rho$, $\kappa$) are
        discretized; the behavior between grid points is not directly
        certified.
\end{itemize}
These limitations are controlled in the sense that the analytic
extension argument provides a basis for extrapolation, but they are
not eliminated.

\subsection{The Neutrino Sector}
\label{sec:neutrino}

Neither the formal programme nor the TE2.2 scan directly addresses
the neutrino sector.
Both operate at the level of gauge group and generation count; neither
derives whether neutrinos are Dirac or Majorana, the mass hierarchy,
or the CP-violating phase in the PMNS matrix.
This is an explicit and acknowledged scope limitation.
The concordance established in this paper is at the level of
\emph{gauge structure and generation count}; the neutrino sector
represents a further frontier for both methods.

\paragraph{Structural neutrino prediction.}
A structural prediction of the neutrino mass-squared ratio is given in the companion paper~\cite{Spivack2026_SM_UGP}.  The Braid Atlas right-handed neutrino $b$-values $\{5,11,19\}$ satisfy $m_{\nu_g} \propto b_g^{29/9}$; the exponent admits three independent structural decompositions:
\begin{align*}
\tfrac{29}{9} &= \tfrac{N_c^3 + s}{N_c^2} \;=\; \tfrac{4N_c^2 - \delta}{N_c^2} \;=\; \tfrac{d_{45} - d_{16}}{N_c^2},
\end{align*}
where $s = (N_c^2{-}1)/4 = 2$, $\delta = 7$, and $d_{45} - d_{16} = \dim(45_{\mathrm{SU}(5)}) - \dim(16_{\mathrm{SO}(10)}) = 29$.
The formula predicts the mass-squared splitting ratio $\Delta m^2_{21}/\Delta m^2_{31} = 0.02936$, matching NuFIT 6.0~\cite{NuFIT60} ($0.02951 \pm 0.00098$) to $0.16\sigma$ ($0.52\%$; $1.58\sigma$ from PDG 2024 world average) with no free parameters.  Normal ordering is automatic.  With the Dirac scale $E_D = v_H/(4N_c^2 - \delta) = v_H/29$ (same structural integer as the exponent numerator), the absolute sum is $\sum m_\nu = 56$~meV at $M_R = 2 \times 10^{16}$~GeV (within the Planck bound), and $60$~meV at the best-fit $M_R = 1.88 \times 10^{16}$~GeV.  The structural neutrino prediction thus covers both the mass-squared ratio and the absolute Planck-window sum; a first-principles Lagrangian derivation combining the SO(10) Yukawa matrix with Braid Atlas flavour structure, and a UGP-internal determination of $M_R$, remain open.

\subsection{Vector-Like Fermion Representations}
\label{sec:vl}

The formal exclusion of vector-like fermions
(Theorem~\ref{thm:exclusions}(ii)) is not directly verified by the
TE2.2 scan, because the scan's parameterization does not include
a vector-like vs.\ chiral parameter.
This is a scope gap between the two methods: the formal side makes a
claim that the computational side does not independently check.
A future TE2.2-type scan that includes fermion chirality as a
parameter would close this gap.

\subsection{Falsifiability}
\label{sec:falsifiability}

The concordance result makes concrete falsifiability predictions.

\paragraph{Formal falsifiability.}
The RCC would be falsified by construction of an explicit gauge
theory satisfying all PSC conditions with $G\neq G_{\mathrm{SM}}$
or $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\neq 3$.
The Lean~4 formalization~\cite{SpivackUGPFormalization} provides a
machine-checkable sieve: a proposed alternative theory can be
submitted to the sieve and its PSC status verified.

\paragraph{Computational falsifiability.}
The TE2.2 result would be contradicted by:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A universe description $\Psi'$ with $G\neq G_{\mathrm{SM}}$
        or $N_{\mathrm{gen}}\neq 3$ achieving $D[\Psi']<D_{\mathrm{SM}}$
        under the same functional~\eqref{eq:dissonance};
  \item A non-SM universe passing all hard PSC filters under
        the same weights $w_i$;
  \item A finer discretization that reveals non-SM minimizers.
\end{itemize}
The data file is publicly available~\cite{ugp-physics-repo} and the
scan code is reproducible (see Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility});
any such contradiction would be immediately detectable.

\paragraph{Experimental falsifiability.}
Both methods would be undermined by experimental evidence for:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A fourth generation of chiral fermions within the SM
        gauge structure;
  \item Observation of a GUT symmetry at accessible energies;
  \item Detection of vector-like quarks or leptons at the LHC or
        successor colliders.
\end{itemize}
These possibilities are physically testable and none has been
confirmed to date.

\section{Conclusion}

\paragraph{What this paper establishes.}

\textbf{First,} it is the computational certificate for the

\paragraph{The forced vs.\ selected distinction.}
A key conceptual contribution of this paper is sharpening the
distinction between what PSC \emph{forces} and what it \emph{selects}.
$N_{\mathrm{gen}}\geq 3$ is \emph{forced} by the Layer~I
CP-violation requirement (Remark~\ref{rem:ngen-forced}).
$N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3$ is \emph{selected} by the Layer~II MDL criterion.
$G_{\mathrm{SM}}$ is forced by chirality and anomaly cancellation.
The continuous cosmological parameters $(\Lambda,\rho,\kappa,\tau)$
remain \emph{underdetermined} within the SM family.
This taxonomy is important: the PSC programme achieves structural
forcing of the gauge group and generation count floor, optimality
selection of the exact generation count, and identifies the remaining
underdetermined sector as the target for future work.

\paragraph{Principal residuals.}
The RCC (established as a Lean-certified theorem, \texttt{PSC.RCCInfiniteFamilies}, zero \texttt{sorry}~\cite{ugp-lean}; SM uniqueness is now unconditional), the discretization gap (controlled but not eliminated),
the neutrino sector (mass-squared ratio predicted to $0.16\sigma$ from NuFIT 6.0 ($0.52\%$); absolute sum within Planck bound via $E_D = v_H/29$; exponent $29/9$ identified via three independent structural decompositions; full Lagrangian derivation remains open), the absence of a complete Lean~4

Each represents a well-defined target for future work.

\paragraph{Working hypothesis.}
The Standard Model is not merely an empirically successful theory
fitted to data, but reflects a privileged self-consistent structure
under strong information-theoretic, algebraic, and geometrodynamical
closure conditions.
Whether this amounts to a proof of SM \emph{necessity} requires
an independent analytic derivation of $C_{14}$; the RCC has now been
resolved (\texttt{PSC.RCCInfiniteFamilies}, zero \texttt{sorry},~\cite{ugp-lean}).
The present evidence---formal theorem chain, computational certificate,

\section{The Fourteen TE2.2 Constraint Terms}
\label{app:constraints}

We give the analytic definition of each constraint term $C_i[\Psi]$
used in the dissonance functional~\eqref{eq:dissonance}.
In all cases $\Psi=(d,G,N_{\mathrm{gen}},N_{\mathrm{obs}},\Lambda,
\rho,\kappa,\tau)$ is the universe parameter vector.

\paragraph{$C_1$: Dimensional.}
$\|C_1[\Psi]\|^2 = (d-4)^2$.
This penalizes deviation from the PSC-optimal spacetime dimension
$d=4$.

\paragraph{$C_2$: SRRG Fixed Point.}
$\|C_2[\Psi]\|^2 = \mathbf{1}[G\neq G_{\mathrm{SM}}] +
(N_{\mathrm{gen}}-3)^2$.
This penalizes departure from the self-renewing renormalization
group (SRRG) fixed point $(G_{\mathrm{SM}}, N_{\mathrm{gen}}=3)$.

\paragraph{$C_3$: SRRG Viability.}
$\|C_3[\Psi]\|^2 = \max(0,\, F_{\mathrm{max}}[S] - F[S;\Psi])^2$,
where $F[S;\Psi]$ is the gauge coupling functional and
$F_{\mathrm{max}}[S]$ is its maximum over PSC-compatible structures.
This penalizes failure to achieve maximal SRRG viability.

\paragraph{$C_4$: Quarter-Lock.}
$\|C_4[\Psi]\|^2 = (\sqrt{3}\,g_1 - g_2)^2$,
evaluated at the SRRG fixed point.
This encodes the gauge-coupling ratio condition required for the
SRRG structure to be self-sustaining.

\paragraph{$C_5$: RG Flow Stability.}
$\|C_5[\Psi]\|^2 = \max(0,\, dc/dt)^2$,
where $c$ is the running coupling.
This penalizes positive beta functions (growing couplings in the UV).

\paragraph{$C_6$: K\"{a}hler Structure.}
$\|C_6[\Psi]\|^2 = \|g_{\mathrm{Fisher}} - \omega_{\mathrm{symp}}\|^2_F$,
where $g_{\mathrm{Fisher}}$ is the Fisher information metric on
coupling-constant space and $\omega_{\mathrm{symp}}$ is the natural
symplectic form.
This penalizes failure of the Fisher metric to be K\"{a}hler (i.e.,
compatible with the symplectic structure).

\paragraph{$C_7$: Area Law.}
$\|C_7[\Psi]\|^2 = (S_{\mathrm{EE}} - A/(4\ell_P^2) -
\beta\log A)^2$,
where $S_{\mathrm{EE}}$ is the entanglement entropy of the
vacuum state and $A$ is the area of the causal horizon.
This penalizes deviation from the Bekenstein--Hawking area law
with logarithmic correction.

\paragraph{$C_8$: Unitary Evolution.}
$\|C_8[\Psi]\|^2 = \mathbf{1}[\text{CP-violation absent}]$.
A CP-conserving theory cannot maintain unitary time evolution in
the PSC framework; this constraint is a hard binary filter.

\paragraph{$C_9$: RIET Equivalence.}
$\|C_9[\Psi]\|^2 = \|\nabla_\mu R - \nabla_\mu T - \nabla_\mu I -
\nabla_\mu C\|^2$,
penalizing failure of the mutual duality between spacetime curvature
($R$), energy-momentum ($T$), entropy ($I$), and information
processing rate ($C$) that characterizes Reflexive Information-Energy-Topology
equivalence.

\paragraph{$C_{10}$: Einstein Equations.}
$\|C_{10}[\Psi]\|^2 = \|G_{\mu\nu} - 8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}\|^2$,
evaluated on the ground state of $\Psi$.
This penalizes deviation from general-relativistic dynamical
equations.

\paragraph{$C_{11}$: Coherence Field.}
$\|C_{11}[\Psi]\|^2 = \|\delta S_{\mathrm{coh}}/\delta\Psi\|^2$,
where $S_{\mathrm{coh}}$ is the coherence field action.
This penalizes inconsistent coupling of the coherence field
$\Psi$ to the matter sector.

\paragraph{$C_{12}$: Information Profit.}
$\|C_{12}[\Psi]\|^2 = \max(0,\, 1.13 - \rho)^2$.
This is a hard threshold: $\rho<1.13$ (information generation rate
less than drain rate) violates the PSC information-viability
condition.

\paragraph{$C_{13}$: Necessary Observers.}
$\|C_{13}[\Psi]\|^2 = \max(0,\, 1 - N_{\mathrm{obs}})^2$.
PSC requires at least one observer class; universes with
$N_{\mathrm{obs}}<1$ are PSC-inviable.

\paragraph{$C_{14}$: Lambda Relation.}
$\|C_{14}[\Psi]\|^2 = (\log_{10}\Lambda -
\log_{10}(\ln\varphi/\ln 2\pi))^2$,
where $\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2$ is the golden ratio.
The PSC-predicted value is $\Lambda_{\mathrm{PSC}}=\ln\varphi/\ln
2\pi\approx 10^{-122}$ in Planck units; this constraint penalizes
deviation from that value on a logarithmic scale.
