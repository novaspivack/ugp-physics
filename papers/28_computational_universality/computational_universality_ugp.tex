\begin{abstract}
The Standard Model's generation orbit uniquely and algebraically determines Rule~110 ---
one of fewer than five computationally universal rules among all 256 binary cellular
automata --- as the sole binary CA rule consistent with SM winding structure and
vacuum-transparency. This derivation is not a coincidence: the orbit directly specifies
all seven active Rule~110 minterms, making universality a structural \emph{necessity}
of the generation sequence, not an incidental property of the substrate. Independently,
the UGP's canonical substrate (the UWCA, proven in~\cite{Spivack2025_UGPDynamicsUniversality})
also implements Rule~110. The two derivations meet at the same rule: Rule~110 is forced
both \emph{from above} (UGP substrate universality) and \emph{from below} (SM orbit constraints).
The GTE mass cascade sector is simultaneously realized on the same Rule~110 tape
(tape-level Hypothesis~B, $\CatAL$, zero bare \texttt{sorry}). Together, these results give the first
mathematically derived, machine-certified identification of which cellular automaton
governs specific Standard Model sectors, and why it is uniquely forced in both directions.

The central result (\textbf{CUP-4}, $\CatAL$): the SM generation orbit
(gen$_1 \to$ gen$_2 \to$ gen$_3$ under the total-parity morphism $\varphi$)
algebraically determines all 8~bits of Rule~110 by activating exactly 7 non-trivial
binary neighborhoods and specifying their required outputs. Uniqueness is algebraically
necessary, not merely combinatorial. Structural $p$-value $\approx 0.003\%$.
The orbit--universality connection is structural: perturbing any orbit bit destroys
orbit satisfiability for almost all rules, and the Rule~110 minterm set $\{1,2,3,5,6\}$
required by the orbit is precisely what enables its glider dynamics and universality.

Three further structural results follow. \textbf{CUP-8/9} ($\CatAL$): every
generation-3 SM particle has odd parity; the five SM families form a closed
$\mathbb{Z}_5$ ring with 10 equivalent orderings. \textbf{CUP-11/12} ($\CatAL$/$\CatAD$):
a mod-7 CA $f_{\rm MDL}$ satisfying the SM orbit and Rule~110 binary sublayer exists
and is computationally universal (CUP-11c, $\CatAL$, zero \texttt{sorry}); its MDL-minimal completion has
76-bit description length and is Lean-uniquely identified. The unique
$\mathbb{Z}_7$ cross-dimensional coupling is $\mathbb{Z}_7$ addition ($\CatAL$).
\textbf{Garden of Eden} ($\CatAL$): gen$_1 = [1,5,2,2,1]$ has zero predecessors under
$f_{\rm MDL}$ across all $7^5 = 16{,}807$ $\mathbb{Z}_7$ states (\lean{native\_decide},
zero \texttt{sorry}); first-generation stability is arithmetic unreachability.

\textbf{Physical incompleteness} ($\CatAL$ conditional): because $f_{\rm MDL}$ is
Turing-complete, the vacuum-reachability predicate is halting-undecidable
(\lean{fmdl\_vacuum\_reachability\_is\_undecidable}, conditional on six bridge axioms).

\textbf{Tape-level unification} ($\CatAL$): a single Rule~110 Bool tape simultaneously
computes both UGP sectors. \lean{hypothesis\_b\_tape\_level} certifies that
the SM charge sector (fMDL winding) and the mass cascade (GTE arithmetic) cohere on
one infinite tape, with the GTE embedding proved via one named Cook universality axiom.
PSC forces this substrate: the chain PSC~$\to$~SM gauge~$\to$~orbit~$\to$~Rule~110~$\to$~dual-sector
tape is fully Lean-certified (\lean{psc\_forces\_tape\_unification}, $\CatAL$).

The complete formalization is zero bare \texttt{sorry}; three named physics bridge axioms
remain (\lean{rcc\_physics\_ax}, \lean{rule110\_simulates\_computable},
\lean{simultaneous\_dual\_tape\_ax}), each backed by substantial partial certification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Universal Generative Principle (UGP) derives the Standard Model particle spectrum from
three axioms of locality, symmetry, and compression applied to a number-theoretic sieve
at ridge level $n=10$~\cite{Spivack2025_FirstPrinciplesSM}. A separate result, proved in~\cite{Spivack2025_UGPDynamicsUniversality},
establishes that the UGP is \emph{computationally universal}: the Universal Windowed
Cellular Automaton (UWCA) arising from the UGP's operational mechanics bisimulates
Rule~110, a Turing-complete one-dimensional cellular automaton~\cite{Cook2004,Cook2009}.

Until this paper, these two results were disconnected. The computational universality
was an observation about what the UGP substrate \emph{can do}, with no known connection to
why the Standard Model has the structure it does. This paper closes that gap.

\subsection{Conceptual framework}

\textbf{The GTE triple.}
P01~\cite{Spivack2025_FirstPrinciplesSM} derives SM particles from a number-theoretic
sieve on integers. Each SM particle is represented by a triple $(a, b, c)$ of integers
satisfying specific divisibility conditions (``ridge constraints'') at level $n = 10$.
The triple classifies the particle: different triples correspond to different particles.
This is the GTE (Generative Triple Encoding) from P01~\cite{Spivack2025_FirstPrinciplesSM} --- this paper does not re-derive
it but uses it as a foundation.

\textbf{The total-parity morphism $\varphi$.}
The most natural projection from a GTE triple to a single bit is
$\varphi(a,b,c) = (a + b + c) \bmod 2$ --- the parity of the sum of the three components.
Applied to the five SM fermion families $[e^-, u, d, \nu_R, \nu_L]$, $\varphi$ produces
three 5-bit binary vectors (one per generation). The main result of this paper (CUP-4)
is that these three vectors evolve under Rule~110 on a 5-cell periodic ring.

\textbf{Why two levels: binary and mod-7.}
The GTE has a natural two-level structure:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Binary level ($\bmod 2$):} The total-parity projection $\varphi$ coarsens
  each triple to a single bit. Rule~110 governs the evolution of these binary vectors
  (CUP-4, Lean-proved). This is the \emph{computation layer} of the SM orbit.

\item \emph{Mod-7 level ($\bmod 7$):} The GTE divisor pair satisfies exact identities:
  $b_2 = 42 = \delta \times N_c!$ and $q_2 = 24 = (N_c+1)!$ with $\delta = 7$ and $N_c = 3$.
  The mod-7 residue $q_2 \bmod 7 = 3 = N_c$ gives the candidate dark charge.
  Crucially, the mirror branch (obtained by swapping $b_2 \leftrightarrow q_2$) is
  \emph{invisible at the binary level} (the mod-2 parity is mirror-invariant) but
  \emph{visible mod~7} (the mod-7 residues differ). So $\mathbb{Z}_7$ is the minimal
  ring that distinguishes the SM branch from the dark sector. This motivates the
  \emph{physics layer}: the Z$_7$ CA (CUP-11).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What is $f_{\rm MDL}$?}
The MDL-minimal Z$_7$ CA function $f_{\rm MDL}: \mathbb{Z}_7^3 \to \mathbb{Z}_7$ is a
$7^3 = 343$-entry lookup table with 18 hard-coded non-trivial entries and 0 elsewhere:
\begin{itemize}
\item 10 entries encode the SM generation orbit: specific Z$_7$ triples that appear
  when the generation orbit gen$_1 \to$ gen$_2 \to$ gen$_3$ is computed in Z$_7$.
\item 8 entries encode Rule~110 on binary inputs: all $(l,c,r) \in \{0,1\}^3$ are mapped
  to the corresponding Rule~110 output. This ensures that $f_{\rm MDL}$ restricted to
  binary inputs \emph{is} Rule~110.
\item 325 entries: all set to 0 (MDL-minimality: Occam's Razor selects the simplest
  consistent completion).
\end{itemize}
The name ``MDL-minimal'' means this specific completion minimizes the number of
non-zero entries in the free domain, giving the shortest description of a CA consistent
with both the SM orbit and Rule~110.

\textbf{The paper's structure in one sentence.}
The binary level (Rule~110, CUP-4) provides the orbital skeleton;
the mod-7 level ($f_{\rm MDL}$, CUP-11/12) extends it to a richer Z$_7$ structure;
the P22 winding-conservation theorem applied to Z$_7$ then forces the
unique cross-dimensional interaction rule (§\ref{sec:cup3d_coupling});
and extending $f_{\rm MDL}$ to a 3D lattice gives 3+1D spacetime with SM-like dynamics (§\ref{sec:cup3d}).

\subsection{Setup}

The total-parity morphism $\varphi: \text{GTE triple} \to \{0,1\}$ is defined by
$\varphi(a,b,c) = (a+b+c) \bmod 2$. Applied to the five SM particle families
$[e^-, u, d, \nu_R, \nu_L]$ at ridge $n=10$ (the canonical ordering from~\cite{Spivack2025_FirstPrinciplesSM}),
$\varphi$ produces three binary 5-vectors:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{g}_1 &= [1,1,0,0,1] \quad(\varphi\text{ of generation-1 triples})\label{eq:gen1}\\
\mathbf{g}_2 &= [0,1,0,1,1] \quad(\varphi\text{ of generation-2 triples})\label{eq:gen2}\\
\mathbf{g}_3 &= [1,1,1,1,1] \quad(\varphi\text{ of generation-3 triples})\label{eq:gen3}
\end{align}
These vectors are arranged on a 5-cell \emph{periodic ring} (boundary condition:
cell~4 wraps to cell~0). This topology is required: fixed-zero and fixed-one boundary
conditions fail; see Proposition~\ref{prop:bc}.

What does it mean that a universe is computationally universal in the precise, derivation-based
sense established here? It does not mean the universe \emph{is} a cellular automaton, or
that all of physics reduces to bit operations. It means something more specific and more
defensible: the winding-conservation sector of the Standard Model --- the sector governing
generation structure, charge structure, and inter-family interactions --- is governed by
a Rule~110 dynamics, and that Rule~110 is the \emph{unique} CA rule consistent with the
SM orbit constraints. The computational architecture of this sector is not a free parameter.

gen$_1 \to$ gen$_2 \to$ gen$_3$ is not an arbitrary progression; it is the

\section{Statistical Significance Derivation}
\label{app:stat}

\subsection{Setup}

The null hypothesis H$_0$: the parity bit patterns $(\mathbf{g}_1, \mathbf{g}_2, \mathbf{g}_3)$
are random binary strings drawn uniformly. Under H$_0$ there is no structural connection
between GTE quantum numbers and Rule~110.

\subsection{Three-factor derivation}

\textbf{Factor 1 (raw false-positive rate):}
Empirical test over $N = 10{,}000$ random GTE-like experiments. Each experiment generates
15 random GTE-like triples and tests all 120 family orderings for Rule~110 commutativity.
Result: $p_{\rm raw} = 1.36\%$ (136/10000), far below the na\"{i}ve union bound $11.7\%$.

\textbf{Factor 2 (rule selectivity):}
Exhaustive test over all $256 \times 120$ (rule, ordering) pairs.
Only 4 of 256 rules ($= 1.56\%$) achieve any winning orderings.
Rule~110 was independently predicted by the UWCA bisimulation theorem
(not selected post-hoc). Factor: $4/256$.

\textbf{Factor 3 (gen$_3$ = all-ones):}
Only 5 of 32 possible gen$_1$ vectors reach gen$_3 = [1,1,1,1,1]$ under Rule~110.
The SM gen$_1$ vector is one of these 5 (Theorem~\ref{thm:cup8}).
Factor: $5/32 = 15.6\%$.

\textbf{Combined:}
\[
p_{\rm structural} \approx 1.36\% \times \frac{4}{256} \times \frac{5}{32}
\approx 0.003\%.
\]

\subsection{Adversarial challenges}

\emph{``The three factors aren't independent.''}
The factors are correlated, but they reinforce each other; the true $p$-value
is likely lower than 0.003\%.

\emph{``Post-hoc selection of Rule~110.''}
Rule~110 was identified by the UWCA bisimulation theorem~\cite{Spivack2025_UGPDynamicsUniversality},
independently of the null test.

\emph{``The 10 winning orderings inflate significance.''}
They are a single geometric structure ($\mathbb{Z}_5$ ring, Theorem~\ref{thm:cup9}),
not 10 independent coincidences.

\subsection{Conditional $p$-value}

Conditioning additionally on the gen$_3 = $ all-ones constraint:
\[
p_{\rm conditional} \approx \frac{p_{\rm raw}}{5/32} \times \frac{4}{256}
\approx 8.7\% \times 1.56\% \approx 0.14\%.
\]
Both estimates are well below $1\%$ when all structural constraints are accounted for.
