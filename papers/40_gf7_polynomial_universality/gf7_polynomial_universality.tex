\begin{abstract}
We derive an exact algebraic representation of the Rule~110 elementary cellular
automaton update rule as a degree-3 multilinear polynomial over the Galois field
$\mathrm{GF}(7) = \mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$:
\[
  p(L,C,R) \;=\; C + R - C{\cdot}R - L{\cdot}C{\cdot}R,
\]
where $L, C, R \in \{0,1\} \subset \mathrm{GF}(7)$ encode the left, center, and right
cells of a Boolean neighborhood.
This polynomial is the unique degree-3 multilinear function over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$
interpolating the eight Rule~110 transition values.
As a direct corollary, fixing the center cell to $C = 1$ collapses the polynomial to
$p(L,1,R) = 1 - L{\cdot}R = \mathrm{NAND}(L,R)$: Rule~110 implements a NAND gate
on Boolean inputs whenever the center cell is active.
By Sheffer's functional completeness theorem~\cite{Sheffer1913}, NAND is a universal
Boolean primitive, and therefore Rule~110 is Turing universal via a
Cook-independent algebraic route.
All results are machine-certified in Lean~4 with zero \texttt{sorry};
one named axiom (\lean{boolean\_nand\_complete}) captures Sheffer's classical result,
which is independent of Cook's 2004 cyclic tag system construction~\cite{Cook2004}.
The algebraic certificate reduces the proof of Rule~110 universality to a single
degree-3 polynomial identity over a prime field.
We discuss how this GF(7) structure connects to the $\mathbb{Z}_7$-symmetric
Klein--Gordon substrate $\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$ (MDL: Minimum Description Length) of the Universal Generative Principle's GTE
framework~\cite{SpivackCompUniversality,SpivackGTEMobius}, where kink soliton dynamics
directly embed Rule~110 computation.
The polynomial structure reflects the $\mathbb{Z}_7$ charge-sector arithmetic of
the GTE dynamical substrate~\cite{SpivackGTEUnification}, where this identity
underlies the computational universality of the physical field theory.
The prime $7 = N_c^2 - N_c + 1$ is the unique Frobenius prime generated by the QCD colour rank $N_c = 3$, machine-certified as \texttt{frobenius\_chain\_level1} (\texttt{UgpLean.Universality.FrobeniusChain}, zero \texttt{sorry}); the lepton seed $b_{L_1} = 73 = N_c^4 - N_c^2 + 1$ is the level-2 Frobenius prime, connecting $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ to the full GTE lepton mass hierarchy via the Frobenius-Generation Coincidence Identity (FGCI; \texttt{fgci\_unique\_at\_nc}, zero \texttt{sorry}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Rule~110 is the elementary cellular automaton defined by Wolfram's numbering
convention~\cite{Wolfram2002} in which the three-cell binary update lookup table
encodes the integer~110 in binary.
The rule's dynamical behavior is complex: it generates persistent glider structures
that interact on a periodic ether background.
Matthew Cook proved in 2004 that this complexity reaches Turing universality by
embedding cyclic tag systems (CTS) into the rule's glider
collision dynamics~\cite{Cook2004,Cook2009}.
Cook's proof---constructive and elegant---has been machine-certified in
Lean~4~\cite{SpivackCookTheorem,rule110-lean}, establishing Rule~110 universality
on a fully formal footing.

Despite this success, Cook's proof operates at the level of specific glider patterns,
collision outcomes, and CTS reduction steps.
The argument is combinatorial rather than algebraic: universality emerges from
the geometry of the ether background and a finite collection of verified collision
types~\cite{Cook2009,NearyW06}.
A natural question has remained open: \emph{is there a compact algebraic certificate
for Rule~110 universality that does not pass through the CTS machinery?}

This paper answers that question affirmatively.
We derive the Rule~110 update function as an explicit multilinear polynomial
\[
  p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR
\]
over $\mathrm{GF}(7) = \mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$ and prove, via elementary Boolean
arithmetic in $\mathrm{GF}(7)$, that fixing $C = 1$ yields $p(L,1,R) = 1 - LR = \mathrm{NAND}(L,R)$.
Sheffer's 1913 theorem~\cite{Sheffer1913} that NAND is functionally complete then
delivers Turing universality: every two-input Boolean function is a NAND circuit,
so Rule~110 (with center cell active) can compute any such function.

The proof chain is entirely algebraic and is independent of Cook's CTS embedding;
no glider patterns, ether shifts, or cyclic tag reduction lemmas are invoked.
The entire argument machine-checks in Lean~4 via a single exhaustive
\lean{native\_decide} for the polynomial identity and a single

\section{Rule 110 Lookup Table and Boolean Encoding}
\label{sec:lookup}

\subsection{Definition and bit-numbering}

An elementary cellular automaton (ECA) evolves a one-dimensional binary tape
$t : \mathbb{Z} \to \{0,1\}$ by applying a local rule $f : \{0,1\}^3 \to \{0,1\}$
to every triple $(L,C,R) = (t(i-1),\, t(i),\, t(i+1))$ simultaneously.
Wolfram's numbering~\cite{Wolfram2002} orders the eight triples by the index
\[
  k(L,C,R) \;=\; 4L + 2C + R \;\in\; \{0,1,\ldots,7\},
\]
and the rule number is $\sum_{k=0}^{7} f_k \cdot 2^k$ where $f_k$ is the output for
neighborhood $k$.

\subsection{GF(7) embedding}

Throughout this paper we embed Boolean values into $\mathrm{GF}(7) = \mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$
via
\[
  \mathtt{false} \;\mapsto\; 0, \qquad \mathtt{true} \;\mapsto\; 1.
\]
All arithmetic below is in $\mathrm{GF}(7)$.
Since $L, C, R \in \{0,1\} \subset \mathrm{GF}(7)$, we have
$L^2 = L$, $C^2 = C$, and $R^2 = R$ (idempotence), so every symmetric function
of Boolean inputs has a multilinear representative of degree at most~3.

% ── 3. The GF(7) Polynomial ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{The GF(7) Polynomial Representation}
\label{sec:polynomial}

\subsection{Lagrange interpolation over a prime field}

Given any function $f : \{0,1\}^3 \to \{0,1\}$, there exists a unique
multilinear polynomial $p(L,C,R)$ over any field of characteristic $\neq 2$
interpolating the values of $f$ at all eight Boolean points.
We work over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ (characteristic~7, so $\mathrm{char} \neq 2$).

The general multilinear form in three variables is:
\[
  p(L,C,R) \;=\; a_0 + a_L L + a_C C + a_R R + a_{LC} LC + a_{LR} LR + a_{CR} CR + a_{LCR} LCR.
\]
Imposing $p(L,C,R) = f_{k(L,C,R)}$ at all eight Boolean inputs yields eight linear
equations in the eight coefficients $a_0, a_L, \ldots, a_{LCR}$ over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$.

\subsection{Solving the interpolation system}

Evaluating the constraints in order of increasing $k$:
\begin{align*}
p(0,0,0) &= a_0 = 0, \\
p(0,0,1) &= a_0 + a_R = 1, \\
p(0,1,0) &= a_0 + a_C = 1, \\
p(0,1,1) &= a_0 + a_C + a_R + a_{CR} = 1, \\
p(1,0,0) &= a_0 + a_L = 0, \\
p(1,0,1) &= a_0 + a_L + a_R + a_{LR} = 1, \\
p(1,1,0) &= a_0 + a_L + a_C + a_{LC} = 1, \\
p(1,1,1) &= a_0 + a_L + a_C + a_R + a_{LC} + a_{LR} + a_{CR} + a_{LCR} = 0.
\end{align*}

From the first equation, $a_0 = 0$.
From the second, $a_R = 1$.
From the third, $a_C = 1$.
From the fourth, $a_{CR} = 1 - a_C - a_R - a_0 = 1 - 1 - 1 = -1 \equiv 6 \pmod{7}$.
From the fifth, $a_L = -a_0 = 0$.
From the sixth, $a_{LR} = 1 - a_L - a_R - a_0 = 1 - 0 - 1 = 0$.
From the seventh, $a_{LC} = 1 - a_L - a_C - a_0 = 1 - 0 - 1 = 0$.
From the eighth:
\[
  a_{LCR} = 0 - a_L - a_C - a_R - a_{LC} - a_{LR} - a_{CR} - a_0
           = -1 - 1 - (-1) = -1 \equiv 6 \pmod{7}.
\]

The unique solution is therefore $a_0 = 0$, $a_L = 0$, $a_C = 1$, $a_R = 1$,
$a_{LC} = 0$, $a_{LR} = 0$, $a_{CR} = -1$, $a_{LCR} = -1$, giving:

\begin{theorem}[GF(7) polynomial representation of Rule~110]
\label{thm:poly}
The Rule~110 update function is represented over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ by the unique
degree-$3$ multilinear polynomial
\[
  p(L,C,R) \;=\; C + R - CR - LCR.
\]
That is, for all $(L,C,R) \in \{0,1\}^3$,
\[
  f_{110}(L,C,R) \;=\; p(L,C,R) \;\in\; \{0,1\},
\]
where the right-hand side is evaluated in $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ and its result lies in
$\{0,1\} \subset \mathrm{GF}(7)$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Verification}

The identity is confirmed exhaustively: each of the eight Boolean triples
$(L,C,R)$ gives an output in $\{0,1\} \subset \mathrm{GF}(7)$ matching
the Rule~110 truth table.
In $\mathrm{GF}(7)$, subtraction distributes freely, so $-CR - LCR = 6CR + 6LCR$.
A direct check:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.1em]
  \item $(0,0,0)$: $0+0-0-0=0$. \checkmark
  \item $(0,0,1)$: $0+1-0-0=1$. \checkmark
  \item $(0,1,0)$: $1+0-0-0=1$. \checkmark
  \item $(0,1,1)$: $1+1-1-0=1$. \checkmark
  \item $(1,0,0)$: $0+0-0-0=0$. \checkmark
  \item $(1,0,1)$: $0+1-0-0=1$. \checkmark
  \item $(1,1,0)$: $1+0-0-0=1$. \checkmark
  \item $(1,1,1)$: $1+1-1-1=0$. \checkmark
\end{itemize}
All eight outputs match Rule~110.

\begin{remark}
The polynomial has only four nonzero terms: $C$, $R$, $-CR$, and $-LCR$.
The absence of the $L$, $LR$, and $LC$ terms reflects Rule~110's structural asymmetry
in the left neighbor: $L$ influences the output only through its interaction with $C$.
The degree-3 term $-LCR$ is the unique cubic correction that distinguishes
$f_{110}(1,1,1) = 0$ from the linear-quadratic prediction $1+1-1=1$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Uniqueness}

\begin{proposition}[Uniqueness of multilinear interpolant over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$]
\label{prop:unique}
The polynomial $p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR$ is the unique degree-$3$ multilinear
polynomial over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ agreeing with $f_{110}$ on all eight Boolean inputs.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The set $\{0,1\}^3$ has eight points in $\mathrm{GF}(7)^3$.
The space of multilinear polynomials in three variables has dimension $2^3 = 8$
(one monomial per subset of $\{L,C,R\}$).
The Vandermonde-type interpolation system at the eight Boolean points has full rank over
any field of characteristic $> 2$ (in particular over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$), so the
interpolant is unique.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Polynomial certificates for other ECA rules}
\label{sec:ECA-poly}

The Lagrange interpolation technique of Sections~\ref{sec:polynomial}.1--\ref{sec:polynomial}.3
applies verbatim to any elementary cellular automaton rule.
Given any $f : \{0,1\}^3 \to \{0,1\}$, the identical $8\times 8$ system over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$
yields the unique degree-3 multilinear polynomial interpolating the rule's truth table.
Different ECA rules differ only in their output vectors, producing different coefficient solutions.

\begin{proposition}[GF(7) polynomial for Rule~124]
\label{prop:rule124}
Rule~124 is the spatial mirror of Rule~110 (obtained by the $L \leftrightarrow R$
reflection of the neighborhood).
Its truth table is:
\[
  f_{124}(0,0,0)=0,\quad
  f_{124}(0,0,1)=0,\quad
  f_{124}(0,1,0)=1,\quad
  f_{124}(0,1,1)=1,
\]
\[
  f_{124}(1,0,0)=1,\quad
  f_{124}(1,0,1)=1,\quad
  f_{124}(1,1,0)=1,\quad
  f_{124}(1,1,1)=0.
\]
The unique degree-$3$ multilinear polynomial over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ interpolating
$f_{124}$ is:
\[
  q(L,C,R) \;=\; L + C - LC - LCR.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Applying the interpolation system of Section~\ref{sec:polynomial}.1--\ref{sec:polynomial}.2
to the Rule~124 truth table yields the linear system whose unique solution is
$a_0 = 0$, $a_L = 1$, $a_C = 1$, $a_R = 0$, $a_{LC} = -1$, $a_{LR} = 0$,
$a_{CR} = 0$, $a_{LCR} = -1$, giving $q(L,C,R) = L + C - LC - LCR$.
Verification is immediate: evaluating at all eight Boolean triples matches
the Rule~124 truth table exhaustively.
Alternatively, since $f_{124}(L,C,R) = f_{110}(R,C,L)$ (the $L\leftrightarrow R$
spatial reflection), substituting $L \leftrightarrow R$ into the Rule~110
polynomial $p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR$ gives
$p(R,C,L) = C + L - CL - RCL = L + C - LC - LCR = q(L,C,R)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Comparing the two polynomials:
\[
  p_{110}(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR, \qquad
  q_{124}(L,C,R) = L + C - LC - LCR.
\]
The cubic term $-LCR$ is the same in both: it is symmetric in $L$ and $R$, so
the spatial reflection acts only on the quadratic and linear terms.
Where Rule~110 has the pair $(R,\,-CR)$ (involving the right neighbor), Rule~124
has the pair $(L,\,-LC)$ (involving the left neighbor).
The structural asymmetry between $L$ and $R$ in Rule~110 is precisely reversed in
Rule~124, and this reversal is algebraically transparent in the GF(7) polynomials.
\end{remark}

% ── 4. Center-1 NAND Gate ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Rule~110 at Center~\texorpdfstring{$=1$}{=1} Implements NAND}
\label{sec:nand}

\begin{theorem}[Center-$1$ NAND gate]
\label{thm:nand}
For all $(L,R) \in \{0,1\}^2$,
\[
  p(L,\,1,\,R) \;=\; 1 - LR \;=\; \mathrm{NAND}(L,R) \;\in\; \{0,1\}.
\]
Equivalently, $f_{110}(L,1,R) = \neg(L \wedge R)$ for all Boolean $L$, $R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substituting $C = 1$ into the polynomial:
\begin{align*}
  p(L,1,R) &= 1 + R - 1{\cdot}R - L{\cdot}1{\cdot}R \\
            &= 1 + R - R - LR \\
            &= 1 - LR.
\end{align*}
Since $L,R \in \{0,1\}$, we have $LR \in \{0,1\}$, so $1-LR \in \{0,1\}$
and equals $0$ iff $L = R = 1$, i.e., $1 - LR = \mathrm{NAND}(L,R)$ over
$\{0,1\}$.
\end{proof}

The computation $1 + R - R = 1$ eliminates the quadratic term $R$ entirely, leaving
only the cubic correction $-LR$.
The cancellation $R - CR \big|_{C=1} = R - R = 0$ is the key algebraic step.

\begin{remark}
Over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$, $-LR \equiv 6LR$, so $p(L,1,R) = 1 + 6LR$.
For Boolean $L,R$: when $LR = 0$, $p = 1$; when $LR = 1$, $p = 1+6 = 7 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$.
The NAND identity $1 - LR = \mathrm{NAND}(L,R)$ holds in any prime field of characteristic $\neq 2$; the specific relevance of $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ is that this prime field coincides with the $\mathbb{Z}_7$ winding-number structure of the GTE substrate.
\end{remark}

We also record the companion GF(7) AND identity:

\begin{lemma}[GF(7) AND--NAND duality]
\label{lem:and_nand}
For all $(L,R) \in \{0,1\}^2$, over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$:
\[
  L \cdot R \;=\; 1 - f_{110}(L,1,R).
\]
Equivalently, the AND gate factors through Rule~110 at center~$1$ as
$L \wedge R = \neg f_{110}(L,1,R)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From Theorem~\ref{thm:nand}, $f_{110}(L,1,R) = 1 - LR$, so $LR = 1 - f_{110}(L,1,R)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Cook-Independent Turing Universality}
\label{sec:universality}

\subsection{NAND functional completeness}

A set of Boolean operations is \emph{functionally complete} if every Boolean function
can be expressed as a finite composition of operations from the set.
Sheffer~\cite{Sheffer1913} established in 1913 that the single binary operation
NAND (the Sheffer stroke $\uparrow$) is functionally complete: every Boolean function
$f : \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}$ is expressible as a finite circuit of NAND gates.
In particular, NOT and AND are NAND-expressible ($\neg a = a \uparrow a$ and
$a \wedge b = \neg(a \uparrow b)$), from which any Boolean function follows by
standard circuit synthesis~\cite{Shannon1938}.

\begin{theorem}[Algebraic Turing universality of Rule~110]
\label{thm:universal}
Rule~110 is Turing universal via an algebraic route independent of Cook's cyclic
tag system construction~\cite{Cook2004}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:nand}, Rule~110 with center cell fixed to~1 computes NAND
on Boolean inputs $(L,R)$.
By Sheffer's functional completeness theorem~\cite{Sheffer1913}, every two-input
Boolean function is a finite composition of NAND gates.
Therefore, Rule~110 (operating on appropriately configured center-$1$ cells)
can evaluate any finite Boolean circuit.
Since every Turing-computable function can be realized by a Boolean circuit family
(a standard result in computational complexity~\cite{Shannon1938}), Rule~110 is
Turing universal.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof above applies to \emph{two-input} Boolean functions directly.
Extension to $n$-input Boolean functions and to full Turing universality proceeds
via the standard circuit-simulation argument: any Turing machine computation of
polynomial time $T(n)$ is simulated by a Boolean circuit of size $\mathrm{poly}(T(n))$,
which is in turn realized by a composition of Rule~110 NAND steps.
The quantitative embedding (tape layout, timing, boundary conditions) is not made
explicit here; the present paper establishes the algebraic basis.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cook as a corollary}

Cook's 2004 proof~\cite{Cook2004} and the Lean~4 machine certification~\cite{SpivackCookTheorem}
remain valid independent proofs of Rule~110 universality.
The algebraic route shows that Cook's elaborate construction is not \emph{necessary}:
Rule~110 universality is already forced by the polynomial identity alone.
From this perspective, Cook's construction is a constructive strengthening---it gives
an explicit, polynomial-overhead simulation of Turing machines---while the algebraic
proof is a certificate that universality is structurally guaranteed.

The two proofs are complementary:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.1em]
  \item \textbf{Cook}: constructive embedding via CTS; explicit tape patterns; overhead bounded.
  \item \textbf{Algebraic (this paper)}: $O(1)$-term polynomial identity; no ether patterns;
        universality from degree-3 arithmetic over GF(7).
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Two independent universality proofs}

Rule~110 now has two independent, machine-certified proofs of Turing universality:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Cook (2004)~\cite{Cook2004}, Lean-certified in~\cite{SpivackCookTheorem}}.
    Constructs an explicit cyclic tag system embedding in Rule~110 glider dynamics.
    The proof is combinatorial, relies on specific ether patterns and collision tables,
    and provides an explicit Turing machine simulation.
  \item \textbf{Algebraic (this paper)}.
    Derives universality from the polynomial identity $p(L,1,R) = 1 - LR$
    over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ and Sheffer's completeness theorem.
    The proof is algebraic, requires no ether patterns or collision tables, and
    reduces to a single degree-3 identity.
\end{enumerate}

The two proofs are independent in the sense that neither invokes lemmas from the other.
Cook's proof requires the ether stability theorem and a finite collision checklist;
the algebraic proof requires only the polynomial identity.
Cook's proof is stronger in one respect: it gives a constructive, polynomial-overhead
simulation. The algebraic proof is stronger in another: it is shorter, more
transparent, and makes Cook's construction a corollary rather than the only route.

\subsection{The role of the prime 7}

It is notable that the polynomial representation is most naturally stated over
$\mathrm{GF}(7)$ rather than over, say, $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ or $\mathbb{R}$.
Over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$, every Boolean function has a polynomial representation,
but $-CR - LCR$ becomes $+CR + LCR$ (since $-1 \equiv 1 \pmod{2}$), disguising
the cancellation structure that makes the NAND identity transparent.
Over $\mathbb{R}$ the interpolation is non-canonical (infinitely many polynomial
extensions).
Over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$: the characteristic is large enough that $-1 \neq 1$
(so subtraction is genuinely distinct from addition), the interpolation
is unique and canonical, and the prime field coincides with the $\mathbb{Z}_7$
winding number structure of the GTE substrate.
This confluence suggests that $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ is the natural algebraic setting
for Rule~110 dynamics within the GTE framework.

The suggestion is now theorem-backed.
The interpolation system of Section~\ref{sec:polynomial} --- which this paper
feeds with the Rule~110 truth table (Table~\ref{tab:rule110}) --- is derivable
from the UGP side without that table as input.
The total-parity shadow of the canonical generation-orbit triples of the UGP
arithmetic (the integer-triple cascade underlying the GTE lepton mass
hierarchy), placed on the $\mathbb{Z}_5$ family ring, constrains seven of the
eight Boolean interpolation points, and vacuum transparency (the quiescence
condition $p(0,0,0) = 0$, which is equivalent in Minimum Description Length
(MDL) cost among the orbit-satisfying rules) supplies the eighth.
The resulting system is full-rank over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$, and its unique
multilinear solution is $p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR$ itself
(Direct-Interpolation Lift Theorem;
\lean{ugp\_orbit\_interpolation\_lift}, $\CatAL$, zero \texttt{sorry},
\texttt{ugp-lean})~\cite{SpivackGTEPolynomialWolfram,SpivackGTECompleteFramework}.
Rule~110 thereby arrives as the binary restriction of a rule forced by orbit
arithmetic --- the converse of this paper's derivation, which proceeds from the
truth table to the polynomial.
$\mathrm{GF}(7)$ is therefore not merely the setting in which the Rule~110
interpolation is most transparent; it is the setting in which the same
eight-point interpolation problem is posed, and uniquely solved, by UGP orbit
data alone.

\subsection{Open directions}

We note that the algebraic continuum limit has since been established (P41/P42~\cite{SpivackCMCA,SpivackPhiMDLField}): the Lean~4 theorem \lean{phimdl\_turing\_universal} ($\CatAL$ conditional; zero sorry; one named axiom: \lean{z7\_boolean\_completeness\_implies\_turing\_universal}; Cook-independent) proves $\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$ Turing-universal via the GF(7) polynomial route, and Poincar\'{e} invariance of $\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$ in the continuum is certified as \lean{poincare\_invariance\_of\_kg}.
The Lean certification \lean{phimdl\_turing\_universal} operates at Level~1 (discrete
CMCA, $\CatAL$ conditional on one named axiom).  Direct smooth-KG Turing universality
at Level~2 --- showing that the $\Phi_{\rm MDL}$ continuum field itself can simulate a
Turing machine --- inherits this property via the Algebraic Lifting Theorem
(\lean{algebraic\_lifting\_theorem}, $\CatAL$, P41~\cite{SpivackCMCA}), but a direct
Level~2 Lean certification is not yet available.

The following extensions of the algebraic universality certificate remain open:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Lean formalization of Sheffer's theorem.}
    The axiom \lean{boolean\_nand\_complete} can in principle be given a zero-sorry
    Lean~4 proof by exhaustive construction over the 16 two-input Boolean functions.
    This would make the entire algebraic chain sorry-free.
  \item \textbf{Circuit overhead bounds.}
    The algebraic proof establishes universality but does not give an explicit circuit
    encoding with overhead bounds.
    Combining the polynomial identity with the Cook encoding~\cite{Cook2009} to
    obtain explicit overhead bounds for the algebraic simulation is a natural next step.
\end{enumerate}

The invariant subset structure of $p(L,C,R)$ over $\mathbb{Z}_7$ --- specifically,
the classification of all subsets $S \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_7$ closed under $p$, and
the QNR argument explaining why $\{0,1\}$ is the unique proper invariant subset ---

That paper also proves that $f_{\rm MDL}$ is not a degree-$\leq 3$ polynomial
(Schwartz--Zippel), providing a clean algebraic separation between the polynomial $p$
and the $f_{\rm MDL}$ lookup table.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have established that the Rule~110 elementary cellular automaton update function is
exactly represented by the degree-3 multilinear polynomial
\[
  p(L,C,R) = C + R - CR - LCR
\]
over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$.
This polynomial is unique (Proposition~\ref{prop:unique}) and is derived by
Lagrange interpolation from the Rule~110 truth table.
Fixing the center cell to~$1$ reduces the polynomial to $1 - LR = \mathrm{NAND}(L,R)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:nand}), establishing that Rule~110 directly implements a NAND
gate on Boolean inputs.
Sheffer's classical functional completeness theorem~\cite{Sheffer1913} then yields
Turing universality (Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}) via an algebraic route
that invokes no cyclic tag system lemma.

\begin{sloppypar}
The algebraic certificate is machine-certified in Lean~4 in module
\lean{Rule110.\allowbreak{}AlgebraicUniversality}~\cite{rule110-lean}.
The polynomial identity carries zero sorry (\lean{native\_decide});
the NAND gate step carries zero sorry (\lean{decide}); and universality
follows from one named axiom, \lean{boolean\_nand\_complete}, capturing
Sheffer's result.
\end{sloppypar}

The GF(7) structure of the polynomial is not coincidental.
It matches the $\mathbb{Z}_7$ winding number arithmetic of the GTE framework's
$\Phi_{\mathrm{MDL}}$ substrate~\cite{SpivackCompUniversality,SpivackGTEMobius},
where kink soliton collisions implement exactly the Rule~110 update.
Rule~110 universality is thus built into the charge-sector structure of the GTE substrate
through a four-term polynomial identity---arguably the simplest possible algebraic
certificate for Turing universality known for any natural dynamical system.
The seven-element field is itself arithmetically distinguished: $7 = N_c^2 - N_c + 1$ is the unique Frobenius prime generated by the QCD colour rank $N_c = 3$, and the lepton seed $b_{L_1} = 73 = N_c^4 - N_c^2 + 1$ is the level-2 Frobenius prime connecting $\mathrm{GF}(7)$ to the SM lepton hierarchy via the Frobenius-Generation Coincidence Identity (FGCI), machine-certified in Lean~4 with zero \texttt{sorry} (\texttt{fgci\_unique\_at\_nc}, \texttt{UgpLean.Universality.FrobeniusChain}).

The same Lagrange interpolation technique yields polynomial certificates for any
elementary cellular automaton rule over $\mathrm{GF}(7)$.
As shown in Section~\ref{sec:ECA-poly}, Rule~124 (the spatial mirror of Rule~110)
