\begin{abstract}
The Universal Generative Principle (UGP) addresses the Standard Model parameter spectrum,
classifying each sector by epistemic status~\cite{Spivack2026_SM_UGP},
from a uniquely selected integer seed at ridge level $n{=}10$ via a deterministic
arithmetic cascade.
We investigate whether UGP is itself a shadow of a deeper mathematical structure.

\textbf{Four main results.}
(1)~The \textbf{Asymptotic Sparsity Theorem} proves the joint arithmetic-admissibility and physical-viability constraint has exactly one solution across all ridge levels: $(n{=}10,\,b_1{=}73)$.  The arithmetic component is Lean-certified (unconditional); the physical-viability component uses the CODATA-derived instantiation factor (A/D).
(2)~The \textbf{Positive Root Theorem} identifies an exact arithmetic correspondence between the SM bare gauge-coupling numerators and the positive-root counts $|\Phi^+(G)|$ of the respective gauge groups; the \textbf{Chirality Theorem} shows the squareness/non-squareness of these numerators is the Lean-certified arithmetic signature of the vector-like/chiral distinction.

(4)~The \textbf{WZW route is falsified}: the Wess-Zumino-Witten construction cannot reproduce the UGP bare coupling rationals, closing a natural alternative-parent hypothesis.

Epistemic status: all main arithmetic theorems are A$_{\rm Lean}$ (Lean-certified, zero \texttt{sorry}); physics-bridge interpretations and CODATA-conditioned results are A/D.  A front-matter claim-status table lists every result's status explicitly.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Universal Generative Principle (UGP)~\cite{Spivack2026_SM_UGP} is a
deterministic number-theoretic framework that addresses the Standard Model parameter
spectrum, classifying each sector by epistemic status, from three axioms --- locality, symmetry, and compression (MDL) --- via a
uniquely selected integer seed at ridge level $n{=}10$.
Its Lean~4 formalization~\cite{SpivackUGPFormalization,ugp-lean} (see companion formalization paper~\cite{SpivackUGPFormalization}, zero
\texttt{sorry}, zero custom axioms beyond Mathlib) machine-checks the core derivation
chain, including the Residual Seed Uniqueness Classification (RSUC) theorem that
isolates the lepton seed $(1, 73, 823)$ as the unique MDL-minimal survivor of the
two-stage arithmetic sieve.

The framework has produced a series of certified structural results: the blind
$\alpha_s$ prediction at $+0.24\sigma$ of PDG 2024~\cite{Spivack2026_SM_UGP}, the neutrino
mass-squared ratio $\Delta m^2_{21}/\Delta m^2_{31} = 0.02936$ matching NuFIT 6.0~\cite{NuFIT60} to
$0.16\sigma$ ($0.52\%$)~\cite{SpivackNeutrino}, the Koide closed form predicting $m_\tau$ from
$(m_e, m_\mu)$ at 61~ppm~\cite{Spivack2026_Koide}, and the Two-Layer PSC Theorem
establishing the Standard Model as PSC-optimal among all $4$D gauge
theories~\cite{SpivackNEMS05}.

The question of why the Standard Model has its specific gauge group and
representation content has been approached from several mathematical directions
that are complementary to the present work: via noncommutative spectral geometry
and arithmetic motives~\cite{ConnesMarcolli2008,MarcolliManin2020}, via exceptional
Jordan algebras and octonions~\cite{BaezHuerta2010,Baez2021SM}, and via division
algebras~\cite{Furey2022}.  The present paper addresses a related selection question
through arithmetic uniqueness: the UGP seed is the unique solution of a
Diophantine sieve, machine-certified for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

A natural question arises: is UGP itself a shadow of a deeper mathematical structure?
The recurring patterns --- the same integers ($\Nc = 3$, $\ddelta = 7$,
$29 = 4\Nc^2 - \ddelta$) appearing in algebraically independent sectors, the golden
ratio $\PHI$ throughout the kernel, the cyclotomic angles $\pi/6$, $\pi/10$, $\pi/12$
--- suggest that UGP might be a coordinate representation of a more fundamental object.

This paper reports the results of a systematic investigation of this question.
We tested the hypothesis that UGP is the shadow of a specific WZW modular invariant,
found it falsified, and identified the correct characterization: UGP is the unique
rational point on the intersection of two independent constraint systems.

\section{The Joint Constraint as a Diophantine System}
\label{sec:diophantine}

\subsection{Formulation}

The joint constraint (Stage~1 $+$ Stage~2) is the system:
\begin{align}
  R_n &= 2^n - 16, \label{eq:Rn} \\
  \btwo \cdot \qtwo &= R_n, \quad \btwo, \qtwo > 15, \label{eq:divpair} \\
  \bone &= \btwo + R_n/\btwo + 7, \label{eq:b1} \\
  \cone &= \bone(\btwo - 13) + 20 \;\text{ prime}, \label{eq:primelock} \\
  \Calg / \bone &= \deltatar. \label{eq:viability}
\end{align}

From~\eqref{eq:viability}: $\bone = \bonereq := \Calg/\deltatar \approx 73.00017$.
Substituting into~\eqref{eq:b1} and clearing denominators:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\btwo^2 - (\bonereq - 7)\,\btwo + R_n = 0.}
  \label{eq:quadratic}
\end{equation}
This is a \emph{quadratic in $\btwo$ for each $n$}, with discriminant:
\[
  \Delta(n) = (\bonereq - 7)^2 - 4\,R_n
            = (66.00017)^2 - 4\,(2^n - 16).
\]

\subsection{Solution at $n=10$}

At $n{=}10$: $R_{10} = 1008$, $\Delta(10) = 4356.022 - 4032 = 324.022$,
$\sqrt{\Delta} \approx 18.00062$.
The two quadratic solutions are:
\[
  \btwo = \frac{66.00017 + 18.00062}{2} \approx 42.00040,
  \qquad
  \btwo = \frac{66.00017 - 18.00062}{2} \approx 23.99978.
\]
The actual UGP solution is $\btwo = 24$, $\qtwo = 42$ (and its mirror $\btwo = 42$,
$\qtwo = 24$).
The near-integer property --- solutions within $4{\times}10^{-4}$ and
$2{\times}10^{-4}$ of the actual integers --- is the geometric signature of the
uniqueness.
If $\deltatar$ were exactly $\Calg/73$, the solutions would be exactly $24$ and $42$.
The small gap ($\bonereq - 73 \approx 1.7{\times}10^{-4}$) traces to the
$2.39$~ppm offset between the bare UGP prediction and CODATA $\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}$
(see \texttt{uniqueness/canonical\_run/\allowbreak delta\_noncircular.json} for the
non-circular derivation chain).

\section{The Residual Classification Theorem: Proof and Computational Certificate}
\label{app:rcc}

\subsection{Statement and Scope}

\begin{definition}[PSC Layers]
Let $G$ be a compact simple Lie group proposed as a 4D gauge group.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Layer I (chirality):} $G$ passes if and only if it admits at least one complex representation $R$ (satisfying $R \not\cong R^*$), since chiral fermion content in 4D requires complex irreps.
  \item \textbf{Layer II (dissonance):} Given Layer~I passing, $G$ passes only if its PSC dissonance $D[G] \le D_{\rm SM}$, where $D_{\rm SM} = 1.009$ is the SM value established by the TE2.2 scan and the dissonance proxy lower bound is $D_{\rm lb}(G) = \dim(G)/12$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Residual Classification Conjecture (RCC)]
No compact simple (or SM-like rank-3 semi-simple) gauge group other than $\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$ satisfies both PSC layers in 4D with three generations.
\end{definition}

The RCC was established computationally for 12 gauge groups (34,560 universes, TE2.2 scan~\cite{SpivackNEMS05}).  This appendix establishes it over \emph{all} compact simple Lie groups through a combination of Lean-certified Lie-algebraic arguments for the four infinite classical families and an extended computational certificate for the finite exceptional sector.

\subsection{Lean-Certified Proof: Classical Infinite Families}
\label{app:rcc:lean}

The key Lie-theoretic tool is the longest Weyl group element $w_0$.  For a compact simple Lie group $G$, the dual representation $R_\lambda^*$ has highest weight $-w_0(\lambda)$.  A representation is self-dual (real or pseudoreal, never complex) if and only if $-w_0(\lambda) = \lambda$.  If $w_0 = -\mathrm{id}$ on the weight lattice, every representation is self-dual and no complex representations exist.

\begin{theorem}[RCC --- All Classical Families (A$_{\rm Lean}$)]
\label{thm:rcc_classical}
Let $G$ be any compact simple Lie algebra in an infinite classical family.  Then $G$ fails at least one PSC layer:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{$B_n = \mathrm{SO}(2n{+}1)$, all $n \ge 1$:}
    $W(B_n)$ is the group of signed permutations of $n$ letters; the total-sign-reversal element (negating all $n$ coordinates) is in $W(B_n)$, so $w_0 = -\mathrm{id}$.  Therefore every $B_n$ irrep is self-dual (no complex reps) $\Rightarrow$ Layer~I fail.

  \item \textbf{$C_n = \mathrm{Sp}(2n)$, all $n \ge 1$:}
    $W(C_n) = W(B_n)$ as signed permutation groups; same argument.  Layer~I fail.

  \item \textbf{$D_n = \mathrm{SO}(2n)$, $n$ even, all $n \ge 2$:}
    $W(D_n)$ consists of signed permutations with an \emph{even} number of sign changes.  Negating all $n$ coordinates requires $n$ sign changes, which is even when $n$ is even, so $-\mathrm{id} \in W(D_n)$ and again $w_0 = -\mathrm{id}$.  Layer~I fail.

  \item \textbf{$D_n = \mathrm{SO}(2n)$, $n$ odd, $n \ge 5$:}
    Here $-\mathrm{id} \notin W(D_n)$; the chiral half-spinors $S^\pm$ are genuinely complex (each is the conjugate of the other) and $\dim(S^\pm) = 2^{n-1}$.  For $n \ge 5$: $\dim(S^\pm) \ge 2^4 = 16$, so the dissonance proxy $D_{\rm lb} = 16/12 > 1 > D_{\rm SM}^{-1}$.  Layer~II fail.

  \item \textbf{$A_n = \mathrm{SU}(n{+}1)$, $n \ge 3$:}
    The fundamental representation is complex (Layer~I passes).  The adjoint dimension is $\dim(\mathrm{adj}) = (n+1)^2 - 1 \ge 4^2 - 1 = 15$ for $n \ge 3$, giving dissonance proxy $D_{\rm lb} = 15/12 > 1$.  Layer~II fail.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Lean certificate:} \texttt{rcc\_all\_classical\_families} in \texttt{PSC.RCCInfiniteFamilies} (\texttt{ugp-lean}~\cite{ugp-lean}, zero \texttt{sorry}).  The proof assembles five lemmas:
\begin{sloppypar}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \texttt{bn\_all\_irreps\_self\_dual} (cases 1--2): \texttt{funext} + \texttt{simp} on $-(-\lambda) = \lambda$ in $\mathbb{Z}^n$.
  \item \texttt{dn\_even\_all\_irreps\_self\_dual} (case 3): same, conditioned on \texttt{Even n}.
  \item \texttt{dn\_odd\_spinorDim\_exceeds\_threshold} (case~4): \texttt{2\^{}(n-1) >= 16} for $n \ge 5$ by \texttt{Nat.pow\_le\_pow\_right}.
  \item \texttt{an\_adjDim\_ge\_15} (case~5): \texttt{(n+1)\^{}2 - 1 >= 15} for $n \ge 3$ by \texttt{nlinarith}.
  \item \texttt{dissonanceLB\_exceeds\_one}: $d/12 > 1$ for $d \ge 13$ by \texttt{linarith}.
\end{itemize}
\end{sloppypar}
\end{theorem}
