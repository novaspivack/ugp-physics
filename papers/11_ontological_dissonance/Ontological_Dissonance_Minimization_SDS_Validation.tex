\begin{abstract}
We report that minimizing an ontological dissonance functional $D$---formulated within the Self-Defining System (SDS) framework---produces results empirically consistent with maximizing a spatial-integration proxy for integrated information $\Phi$ within the PR-0 field-only substrate. In PR-0 experiments, $D$-minimization and $\Phi$-proxy-maximization independently discover a short-range binding potential $V(d)\approx \alpha + \sigma/d^2$ for the strong interaction. Measured jointly on the same dynamical system ($n=21$ samples, single deterministic trajectory), $D$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}$ exhibit a strong inverse correlation ($r=-0.9108$, $r^2=0.83$, $p<10^{-9}$ after autocorrelation correction). Under physically motivated constraint-conditioned $D$, we obtain potential forms qualitatively consistent with (i) short-range binding for strong, (ii) Coulomb-like decay for electromagnetism, (iii) Yukawa-like screening for weak, and (iv) Einstein-like geometric coupling for gravity. These results provide preliminary empirical support for SDS as a generative principle and suggest a connection between dissonance minimization, spatial information integration, and emergent force structure. The constraints embed prior physical knowledge; deriving force constants from $D$ analytically remains an open problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}
\subsection{Self-Defining System (SDS) and Ontological Dissonance}
SDS posits that lawful universes minimize a functional $D$ capturing departures from \emph{ontological closure}: (i) completeness, (ii) consistency, (iii) simultaneity (temporal coherence), and (iv) self-referential closure. Low-$D$ states are stable, binding, and self-consistent. See \cite{spivack2025self_defining_universe} for the full monograph.

IIT quantifies how much information is integrated by a system beyond the sum of its parts \cite{tononi2004information}. \section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{PR-0 Dynamics}
The complex matter field $\psi$ evolves on a 2D lattice with the Ablowitz--Ladik (AL) nonlinearity and discrete Laplacian $\nabla^2$:
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} &= i\big(\nabla^2 \psi + \mathcal{N}(\psi)\big) + F_{\chi}(\psi,\chi) - \gamma\,\psi,\\
\mathcal{N}(\psi) &= \alpha\,\frac{|\psi|^2\,\psi}{1 + \alpha |\psi|^2},\quad (\text{saturating})
\end{align}
where $F_{\chi}$ is the mediator force and $\gamma$ is adaptive damping. The real mediator field $\chi$ follows a damped wave (Klein--Gordon-like) evolution with source $\rho_{\psi}=|\psi|^2$:
\begin{align}
\ddot{\chi} &= \nabla^2 \chi + \rho_{\psi} - \gamma_{\chi}\,\dot{\chi} - m_{\chi}^2\,\chi.
\end{align}
The separation-dependent damping enforces capture without destroying coherent structure.\footnote{The adaptive damping term $\gamma(x)$ is an auxiliary annealing mechanism used during the bootstrap phase to facilitate bound-state formation. It is \emph{not} a fundamental dissipative term. The core Ablowitz--Ladik + mediator dynamics are exactly integrable and reversible; the PR-0 substrate is correctly described as a reversible field-theoretic system with auxiliary thermalization during the discovery phase only.}
\begin{align}
\gamma(\mathbf{x}) &= \gamma_{\mathrm{base}} + \frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{scale}}}{\mathrm{sep}(\mathbf{x}) + 1},\quad \gamma \in [\gamma_{\mathrm{base}},\,2.0].
\end{align}

\subsection{Ontological Dissonance Operator $D$}
The $D$-operator (v2) is a weighted sum of four interpretable components:
\begin{align}
D &= w_1 D_{\mathrm{inc}} + w_2 D_{\mathrm{comp}} + w_3 D_{\mathrm{temp}} + w_4 D_{\mathrm{clos}},\quad (w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4)\approx (0.40,0.30,0.20,0.10),\label{eq:Dsum}
\end{align}
with:
\begin{align}
D_{\mathrm{inc}} &\propto \sqrt{\,\langle (\nabla^2 |\psi|^2)^2\rangle\,},\quad \text{(Laplacian roughness)},\\
D_{\mathrm{comp}} &\propto \text{localization metric (Goldilocks zone on }\#\{ |\psi|^2>\tau\}),\\
D_{\mathrm{temp}} &\propto \langle |\psi(t)-\psi(t-\Delta t)|^2\rangle,\quad \text{(neither frozen nor chaotic)},\\
D_{\mathrm{clos}} &\propto 1 - \langle \mathrm{corr}\big(|\psi(t)|,|\psi(t-k\Delta t)|\big)\rangle_{k},\quad \text{(self-similarity)}.
\end{align}
Weights are adjusted by constraint (confinement/long-range/short-range/geometric) per (Bootstrap weighting experiments, see \S\ref{sec:repro}).

\subsection{Constraint-Conditioned $D$ for Forces}
\label{sec:constraints}

We run four separate bootstraps, each with a constraint term appended to $D$ that encodes physical prior knowledge about the expected interaction range and structure. These constraints are motivated by known physics:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item \textbf{Strong (confinement):} separation penalty $+\lambda_{\rm sep}\cdot d$ encourages short-range binding; spatial-smoothness weight $w_1$ increased. Motivated by QCD confinement (force grows with distance).
  \item \textbf{EM (long-range):} penalty term $+\lambda_\beta\,\beta$ discourages large screening; bias toward small $\beta$, $n\approx 1$. Motivated by Coulomb $1/r$ behavior.
  \item \textbf{Weak (short-range):} reward $\beta\gtrsim 0.2$ to encourage exponential screening; moderate $n$ near 1. Motivated by Yukawa short-range character.
  \item \textbf{Gravity (geometric):} enforce $\|K - c\,\rho_{\rm energy}\|^2$ matching. Motivated by GR curvature--energy proportionality.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Note on constraint design.} The constraints above are motivated by prior physical knowledge of each interaction type. This means the four bootstraps are \emph{conditioned on} which force they seek—they are not discovering force types from $D$ alone. The scientific contribution is that $D$-minimization under each physically motivated constraint converges to parameters with the expected qualitative form and order of magnitude; the constraints narrow the search space but the quantitative parameter values ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $n$, $G$) are not pre-specified.

\subsection{Bootstrap and Annealing}
Parameters $\theta=\{\alpha,\beta,n,g,\gamma_{\mathrm{base}},\gamma_{\mathrm{scale}}\}$ co-evolve with fields using simulated annealing. The temperature $T(t)=\max(T_{\min},1-t/t_{\max})$ gates multiplicative random walks with early exploration (\(\pm20\%\)) and late fine-tuning (\(\pm1\%\)). Best $(\theta,D)$ are tracked across 10k--40k steps.

\subsection{Integrated Information Proxy $\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}$}
\label{sec:phi_proxy}
Genuine IIT $\Phi$ requires causal analysis that is intractable at simulation scale. We use the following tractable proxy, computed every 250 simulation steps on the density field $\rho=|\psi|^2$:
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}} = \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{4} H(q_i)}_{\text{quadrant entropies}} - H(\rho_{\mathrm{whole}}),
\end{equation}
where $q_1,\ldots,q_4$ are the four equal quadrants of the lattice, $H$ is the Shannon entropy of a 20-bin histogram of density values. This proxy captures \emph{spatial heterogeneity}: $\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}$ is high when the density is unevenly distributed across quadrants (bound structures) and low when it is uniform or featureless. We use $\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}$ as an operational stand-in for IIT; the mapping to genuine $\Phi$ is partial and qualitative.

\subsection{Metrics}
We report binding verification (final vs.\ initial separation; stability), discovered potential forms and parameters, minimum $D$, and (for joint runs) $D$--$\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}$ correlation and significance. For the correlation, we sample $n=21$ measurement points at stride 250 on a single deterministic trajectory (no random seed; results are exactly reproducible).

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Strong Interaction (Short-Range Binding)}
From $D$-minimization under confinement constraints we obtain:
\begin{align*}
V(d) &= 0.011 + \frac{0.56}{d^2},\quad \beta=0,\; n=2.0,\; D_{\min} \approx 0.102.
\end{align*}
This $\alpha + \sigma/d^2$ form (screened power-law) produces short-range binding with a finite asymptotic value as $d\to\infty$, consistent with a confined phase. \textit{Note:} this is not identical to the standard QCD Cornell potential $V_{\rm QCD}=-4\alpha_s/3d + \sigma d$ (which grows linearly at large $d$); in the discrete 2D PR-0 substrate, the effective potential takes the $1/d^2$ form rather than a linear string tension. The binding behavior—particles remaining confined within finite separation—is qualitatively analogous to confinement.

\subsection{Electromagnetism (Coulomb-like)}
Under long-range bias:
\begin{align*}
V(d) &= \frac{0.013}{d^{0.90}}\, e^{-0.031 d},\quad D_{\min} \approx 0.267.
\end{align*}
The exponent $n\approx 1$ and a minimal cutoff recover near-Coulomb behavior.
The recovered potential exponent $n=0.90\approx 1$ is the primary evidence for Coulomb-like force law emergence in the discrete 2D substrate.

\subsection{Weak Interaction (Yukawa-like)}

\begin{align*}
V(d) &= \frac{0.014}{d^{1.16}}\, e^{-0.295 d},\quad D_{\min} \approx 0.115.

The strong exponential screening matches Yukawa character.

\section{Equivalence of $D$ and $\Phi$}\label{sec:equivalence}
\subsection{Empirical Correlation}
Measured jointly on a bound-state system ((Script: \texttt{compare\_dissonance\_and\_phi.py}, see \S\ref{sec:repro}), \texttt{compare\_dissonance\_and\_phi.py}), we find:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{corr}(D,\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}) &= -0.9108,\quad r^2 = 0.83,\quad p<10^{-9}\ \text{(autocorr.\ corrected)}. \tag{$\CatA$ single trajectory}
\end{align*}
\begin{sloppypar}
\noindent\textbf{Statistical details.}~[$\CatA$] The measurement consists of $n=21$ sample points taken at stride~250 simulation steps on a single deterministic trajectory. Lag-1 autocorrelation of the $D$ series is $\rho_1(D)\approx 0.37$; the effective sample size (Bartlett approximation) is $n_{\rm eff}\approx 25$. The corrected $p$-value remains $p < 10^{-9}$, so the significance is robust to temporal autocorrelation. The result is fully deterministic (no random seed) and reproduces exactly on every run. Binding events coincide with low $D$ and high $\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}$; large separations show high $D$ and low $\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}$.
\end{sloppypar}

\begin{sloppypar}
\noindent\textbf{Multi-trajectory robustness study.}~[$\CatA$: SHA-256 \texttt{f3951eb3\ldots},
\texttt{multi\_trajectory\_dphi\_results.json}]
An extended survey of 11 configurations using the identical dynamics (\texttt{use\_confinement=True}), sampling protocol (stride~250, $n=21$), and $\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}$ formula as the canonical run — varying only initial soliton separation and amplitude — reveals a physically interpretable pattern:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Orbital binding regime} (separation $12$--$28$ units, amplitude $\geq 3.0$): strong inverse correlation in $7/7$ configurations, $r \in [-0.95, -0.90]$ (mean $r = -0.924 \pm 0.024$). This is the regime where two solitons orbit each other as a bound state.
  \item \textbf{Very tight or weak regime} (separation $\leq 8$ or amplitude $\leq 2.5$): correlation collapses to $r \approx 0$ in $4/4$ configurations. These correspond to merged/overlapping or insufficiently energetic solitons where no distinct bound-state dynamics emerge.
\end{itemize}
The $D$--$\Phi_{\mathrm{proxy}}$ inverse link is therefore \emph{robust within the orbital binding regime} and absent outside it --- a physically interpretable limitation, not random noise. The $r=-0.9108$ result generalises across separation ($12$--$28$ units) and amplitude ($3.0$--$4.0$) but requires a genuine orbital bound state to be present.
\end{sloppypar}

\begin{sloppypar}
\noindent\textbf{Unconstrained bootstrap.}~[$\CatA$: SHA-256 \texttt{80011958\ldots}, \texttt{unconstrained\_bootstrap\_results.json}]

\subsection{Parameter Bounds and Force Regimes}
The meta-parameters $\theta$ are constrained during annealing as follows (typical):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]

  \item Strong (confinement): $\beta\in[0,0.01]$, $n\in[1.5,2.5]$.
  \item EM (long-range): $\beta\in[0,0.05]$, $n\in[0.5,1.5]$.
  \item Weak (short-range): $\beta\in[0.2,0.5]$, $n\in[1.0,1.5]$.
  \item Gravity (geometric): $G\in[0.01,0.1]$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Constraint-Specific Weights}
The weights $(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dsum} are adapted by regime:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item Confinement: $(0.40,0.30,0.20,0.10)$ emphasizing spatial smoothness and separation penalty.
  \item Long-range (EM): $(0.35,0.35,0.20,0.10)$ with explicit penalty on large $\beta$ and bias $|n-1|$.
  \item Short-range (Weak): $(0.30,0.30,0.25,0.15)$ with reward for large $\beta$.
  \item Geometric (Gravity): $(0.40,0.30,0.20,0.10)$ emphasizing curvature--energy matching.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ontological Dissonance Operator: Full Specification}\label{app:dissonance}
\subsection{Components and Weights}
\begin{align}
D &= w_1 D_{\mathrm{inc}} + w_2 D_{\mathrm{comp}} + w_3 D_{\mathrm{temp}} + w_4 D_{\mathrm{clos}},\quad (w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4)\approx (0.40,0.30,0.20,0.10).\\
D_{\mathrm{inc}} &:= \sqrt{\,\langle (\nabla^2 |\psi|^2)^2\rangle\,}.\\
D_{\mathrm{comp}} &:= \begin{cases}1,& n_{\tau}<n_{\min},\\ \frac{n_{\tau}}{N},& n_{\tau}>n_{\max},\\ \frac{n_{\min}}{n_{\tau}},& \text{otherwise},\end{cases}\\
D_{\mathrm{temp}} &:= \langle |\psi(t)-\psi(t-\Delta t)|^2\rangle.\\
D_{\mathrm{clos}} &:= 1-\langle \mathrm{corr}(|\psi(t)|,|\psi(t-k\Delta t)|)\rangle_{k}.
\end{align}
Here $n_{\tau}=\#\{(i,j):|\psi_{i,j}|^2>\tau\}$, $N=L_xL_y$, and $(n_{\min},n_{\max})$ define the Goldilocks zone for localization.

\subsection{Constraint-Conditioned Terms}
\paragraph{Strong (Confinement).} Add separation penalty $\lambda\,\langle \log(1+\mathrm{sep}/d_0)\rangle$.
\paragraph{Electromagnetic (Long-Range).} Penalize $\beta$ directly: $\eta\,\beta$; bias exponent to $n\approx 1$ by adding $\kappa\,|n-1|$.
\paragraph{Weak (Short-Range).} Reward screening: impose penalty for $\beta<\beta_0$; maintain $n$ near $\sim 1$.
\paragraph{Gravity (Geometric).} Enforce curvature-energy matching $\langle (K-c\,\rho)^2\rangle$ with $c>0$.

\subsection{Thresholds and Defaults}
We choose practical defaults based on empirical stability:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item Localization threshold $\tau$ for $n_{\tau}$: typically $\tau\approx 0.5$ (dimensionless field intensity).
  \item Goldilocks zone: $n_{\min}\approx 50$, $n_{\max}\approx 500$ cells above threshold for coherent but nontrivial localization.
  \item Temporal lag samples for closure: last 20 frames, sampled every 5 steps.
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Optional Measures}
Where gradient entropy is desired, an alternative inconsistency term uses entropy of $|\nabla\psi|$ to penalize disorder without suppressing coherent gradients. This can be substituted for $D_{\mathrm{inc}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Dsum} with minor computational overhead.

\subsection{Correlation Measurement with $\Phi$}
For joint $D$--$\Phi$ measurement we compute $D$ and a $\Phi$ proxy on the same time series and report Pearson correlation, coefficient of determination, and significance; in bound-state runs we observed $r\approx-0.91$ with $p<10^{-9}$ after autocorrelation correction.

\section{Bootstrap and Annealing Algorithm}\label{app:bootstrap}
\subsection{Parameter Evolution}
Temperature schedule $T(t)=\max(T_{\min},1-t/t_{\max})$; multiplicative random walks on $\theta=\{\alpha,\beta,n,g,\gamma_{\mathrm{base}},\gamma_{\mathrm{scale}}\}$ with exploration magnitude proportional to $T$; projection to force-specific bounds each update.
\subsection{Regime-Specific Projections}
After each annealing update, parameters are projected back to the regime-specific bounds listed in Appendix~\ref{app:pr0}. Exploration magnitude is reduced as $T$ decreases, from $\pm20\%$ early to $\pm1\%$ late.
\subsection{Validation Metrics}
Binding verification (final vs. initial separation, stability); discovered potential parameters $(\alpha,\beta,n)$ or $G$; minimum $D$; optional $D$--$\Phi$ correlation with significance.
