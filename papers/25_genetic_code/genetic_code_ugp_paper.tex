\section*{Significance}

We show that the standard genetic code is the unique survivor of a two-stage
sieve over the space of all wobble-admissible codon tables, under eight
simultaneous biological viability criteria. Stage~1 (wobble admissibility)
reduces the search space by $10^{50}$. Stage~2 applies five classical criteria
simultaneously --- placing the standard code at $z = +3.22\sigma$, top
$0.009\%$ of $10^5$ random codes --- and three novel criteria (evolvability,
historical reachability, complete stop coverage). No code in $10^5$ samples
outperforms the standard code under all eight criteria. A CP-SAT global search
reveals that codes with higher abstract scores are biologically disqualified:
they have hyper-conservative mutation profiles that would prevent drastic
substitutions required for protein fold diversification. This is the first computational uniqueness result for the genetic code
across more than five simultaneous criteria.

\begin{abstract}
The near-universality of the standard genetic code raises a deep question:
is it the best possible encoding of amino acids, or merely one of many adequate
solutions? We address this by applying a two-stage sieve --- admissibility
followed by multi-criterion viability --- to the space of all codon-to-amino-acid
maps. \textbf{Stage~1} (wobble admissibility) reduces the 23\textsuperscript{64}
possible maps by a factor of~$10^{50}$, to the $23^{27} \approx 5.8 \times
10^{36}$ maps that respect the Crick wobble degeneracy rules.

\textbf{Stage~2} applies eight independent biological viability criteria
simultaneously: error minimization, prebiotic accessibility, stop-codon
robustness, chemical clustering, polar-requirement conservation, evolvability
(minimum accessible mutation diversity), historical reachability (first-wave
amino acids in ancient codon boxes), and complete stop-codon coverage.
Monte Carlo sampling of $10^5$ complete wobble-admissible codes shows that the
standard code ranks at $z = +3.22\sigma$ on the five-criterion Phase~5
metric~($p < 0.009\%$). Global CP-SAT optimization confirms the standard code
is not the mathematical maximum of any single or combined abstract criterion.
However, codes that score higher on abstract criteria have \emph{max\_jump}
$\leq 5$ (hyper-conservative mutation profiles), which disqualifies them under
the evolvability criterion (Stage~2G).

With all eight criteria applied jointly, no code among $99{,}998$ evolvable complete
wobble-admissible codes outperforms the standard code. The standard
genetic code is the \textbf{unique survivor} of the two-stage sieve within
this sampling depth. Codes that score higher on abstract criteria all carry
hyper-conservative mutation profiles (\emph{max\_jump} $\leq 5$), which
disqualifies them on biological grounds: they would prevent the drastic
substitutions required for protein fold diversification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The genetic code maps 64 codons to 20 amino acids and 3 stop signals. Its
near-universality across all domains of life is remarkable and has long been
taken as evidence that the code is not accidental. Yet explaining why \emph{this
particular} code, rather than any of the $23^{64} \approx 10^{85}$ conceivable
alternatives, was selected remains an open question.

Existing explanations fall into three categories. The \emph{stereochemical
hypothesis}~\cite{Woese1965,Pelc1965} proposes direct chemical affinity between
codons/anticodons and amino acids. The \emph{co-evolution hypothesis}~\cite{Wong1975}
links codon assignments to the biosynthetic relationships among amino acids.
The \emph{error-minimization hypothesis}~\cite{FreelandHurst1998,Haig1991}
shows that the code minimizes the physicochemical distance of amino acids
encoded by synonymous codons, thereby buffering translation errors.

The most influential quantitative result is that of Freeland and
Hurst~\cite{FreelandHurst1998}, who showed that the standard code outperforms
approximately 99\% of random codes on a single error-minimization criterion.
Subsequent work~\cite{Knight2001,Freeland2003,Koonin2009} extended this analysis.
Recent evolutionary-dynamics work argues that ``near-ideal selection'' operates
during code escape and diaspora phases, efficiently selecting codes supporting
rapid cellular division~\cite{Yarus2024NearIdeal}, consistent with the
near-optimality demonstrated here.
No prior study has simultaneously applied more than a small number of criteria or
performed a global search over the full code space.

We apply a two-stage sieve framework to genetic code selection. Stage~1
(structural admissibility) corresponds to wobble decodability --- a
combinatorial quotient structure on codon space. Stage~2 (viability) applies
multiple biochemical fitness criteria simultaneously. This framework is
motivated by a pattern identified in companion work on the Standard Model~\cite{SpivackUGP,SpivackP24}:
the intersection of a structural admissibility condition with a viability condition
can uniquely select a single configuration from an astronomically large candidate space.
The present paper tests whether this pattern extends to biology.

Section~\ref{sec:gte-echoes} and Appendix~\ref{app:gte} document three
arithmetic echoes between the UGP/GTE canonical orbit at $n=10$ and
fundamental counts in the genetic code: the Mersenne ratio
$c_3/c_2 \approx 64$ (codons), the prime-lock residue $m_1 = 20$ (amino
acids), and $a_2 = 3^2$ with wobble classes $= 3^3$. All three are
Lean~4-certified exact arithmetic results~\cite{ugp-lean}, not model fits,
and are independent of the main sieve analysis.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0pt]
\item A wobble-admissibility framework that reduces the search space by~$10^{50}$.
\item A five-criterion joint viability analysis placing the standard code at
      $z = +3.22\sigma$ (top~0.009\%).
\item CP-SAT global optimization characterizing codes that score higher on
      abstract metrics and showing why they are biologically disqualified
      (hyper-conservative mutation profiles, Stage~2G).
\item Three additional viability criteria (evolvability, historical
      reachability, complete stop coverage) that reduce competitors from 9 to 0
      in a sample of~$10^5$ complete codes.
\item A sampling-based uniqueness result: $0/99{,}998$ evolvable complete
      codes outperform the standard under all eight criteria simultaneously.
\end{enumerate}

\section{A Two-Stage Admissibility-Viability Sieve for the Genetic Code}

\subsection{Stage~1: Wobble Admissibility}

The 64 codons (NNN, N $\in$ \{U, C, A, G\}) form a natural equivalence
structure under the Crick wobble rules~\cite{Crick1966}: the third-position
base of a codon is partially degenerate, collapsing the 64-codon space into
27 \emph{wobble equivalence classes}. A codon-to-amino-acid map is
\textbf{wobble admissible} if and only if all codons within the same wobble
class encode the same amino acid (or stop signal).

Wobble admissibility reduces the code space from $23^{64} \approx 10^{85}$
total maps to $23^{27} \approx 5.8 \times 10^{36}$, a reduction by a factor
of~$\sim\!10^{50}$. All subsequent analysis is restricted to this
admissibility-reduced space.

\subsection{Stage~2: Eight Viability Criteria}

Within the admissible space, we apply eight independent criteria. Each
criterion operationalizes a distinct biochemical argument for why certain
codon assignments are more viable than others.

\paragraph{Stage 2A: Error minimization~\cite{FreelandHurst1998}.}
The average polar-requirement (PR) difference~\cite{Woese1965} between amino
acids encoded by codons differing in one nucleotide (all Hamming-1 pairs).
Lower values indicate that translation errors have smaller physicochemical
consequences. The standard code has error score $= 1.684$.

\paragraph{Stage 2B: Prebiotic accessibility.}
A code is more viable if the codon families with more members encode the amino
acids that were most abundant in prebiotic chemistry~\cite{Miller1953,Cronin1993}.
The prebiotic accessibility score rewards codes in which the most prebiotically
common amino acids (Gly, Ala, Asp, Glu, Val, Ser) occupy the largest codon
families~\cite{KnightFLS1999}.

\paragraph{Stage 2C: Stop-codon robustness.}
The code must provide $\geq 2$ distinct stop-codon positions. This ensures
robust translation termination and prevents readthrough at a single stop site.
All standard variant codes (mitochondrial, ciliate, etc.) are checked against
this criterion.

\paragraph{Stage 2D: Chemical clustering.}
Amino acids encoded by Hamming-1 neighbours should be chemically similar
(PR difference $<3$ units). This operationalizes the error robustness principle
at the single-step level. The standard code achieves a clustering score of
$0.714$ (fraction of Hamming-1 pairs within 3 PR units).

\noindent\textit{Note on criterion labelling.} The label 2E is not assigned.
The completeness prerequisite---all twenty sense amino acids represented and at
least two distinct stop-codon outcomes present---is enforced structurally in
the rejection-free code generator rather than applied as a post-hoc viability
filter; it therefore receives no viability-criterion letter.

\paragraph{Stage 2F: Polar-requirement jump conservation.}
The maximum PR difference across any Hamming-1 sense-sense pair
(\emph{max\_jump}) must not exceed the standard code's value of 7.40. This
prevents biologically catastrophic single-mutation substitutions while allowing
the observed range of amino acid diversity~\cite{Zuckerkandl1965}.

\paragraph{Stage 2G: Evolvability (minimum accessible mutation diversity).}
\label{sec:2G}
A viable code must also permit a \emph{minimum} level of amino acid diversity
accessible via single-nucleotide mutation: \emph{max\_jump} $\geq 6.0$.
Codes with \emph{max\_jump} $<6$ prevent all substitutions that change PR by
more than 5 units. This class includes evolutionarily critical mutations
including the Glu$\to$Val substitution underlying sickle-cell
haemoglobin~\cite{Ingram1957} ($\Delta$PR $= 6.9$) and the Asp$\to$Asn class
found in adaptive substitutions in many kinases~\cite{Manning2002}. A code
that forbids all such drastic substitutions would be too constrained for the
evolution of complex enzymatic functions and protein folds.

\paragraph{Stage 2H: Historical reachability.}
Under the co-evolution hypothesis~\cite{Wong1975}, the code was assembled
incrementally starting from the most prebiotically accessible amino acids.
Operationally: the fraction of the seven most prebiotically abundant amino acids
(Gly, Ala, Asp, Glu, Val, Ser, Pro) that occupy four-fold degenerate codon
boxes (the most redundant and evolutionarily ancient assignments) must be
$\geq 0.56$. The standard code achieves 0.714 on this criterion.

\paragraph{Stage 2I: Complete stop-codon coverage.}
All three stop-codon identities (UAA, UAG, UGA) must appear at least once in
the code. Codes with only two stop signals lack the full complement recognized
by the two release factors RF1 (UAA, UAG) and RF2 (UAA, UGA)~\cite{Scolnick1968}.
Incomplete stop coverage is evolutionarily unstable: the absent stop codon can
be captured by a near-cognate tRNA and reassigned to an amino acid, reducing
termination reliability over evolutionary time.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Monte Carlo analysis (Phases 1--5)}

We generated $10^5$ random wobble-admissible complete codes using the
rejection-free construction of~\cite{codon_code}, ensuring all 20 sense amino
acids appear and at least 2 stop signals are present. For each code we computed
the five-criterion score (Stages 2A--2D, 2F):
\[
\text{Score}_5 = -w_1 \cdot \text{err} + w_2 \cdot \text{acc} +
                 w_3 \cdot \text{clus} - w_4 \cdot \tfrac{\text{max\_jump}}{10}
\]
with equal weights $w_i = 1$.

The standard code scores $-1.257$ on this metric. Among $10^5$ random complete
codes, only 9 beat the standard (0.009\%), placing it at $z = +3.22\sigma$
above the mean. This extends the Freeland--Hurst result~\cite{FreelandHurst1998}
from one criterion ($z \approx -2.0\sigma$, top $\sim\!1\%$) to five
simultaneous criteria (top $0.009\%$).

\subsection{CP-SAT global search}

We applied the Google OR-Tools CP-SAT solver to find the globally optimal code
for each objective function. For the 4-criterion objective with \emph{max\_jump}
as a hard constraint, CP-SAT found codes scoring $-0.051$ (vs.\ standard code
$-0.338$). These codes uniformly have \emph{max\_jump} $\approx 4$--$5$ ---
hyper-conservative codes where no single-nucleotide mutation can change the
polar requirement by more than 5 units.

This confirms that the standard code is \emph{not} the unique mathematical
maximum of any abstract combination of criteria we tested. However, the
CP-SAT codes violate Stage~2G (evolvability): all have \emph{max\_jump} $< 6.0$.

\subsection{Variant code confirmations}

All known variant genetic codes (vertebrate and invertebrate mitochondrial,
yeast mitochondrial, ciliate, Mycoplasma, etc.) were tested. All score
\emph{below} the standard code on the joint Phase-5 metric and all score
below all local optima of the metric. The mitochondrial codes (which reassign
one or two codons) are confirmed as local minima of the metric: each variant
is reached from the standard code by a single codon reassignment that
decreases the score.

\subsection{Phase~6: Uniqueness under eight criteria}

The full numerical breakdown appears in Tables~\ref{tab:std_scores}
and~\ref{tab:filter_cascade}.
Adding Stages 2G, 2H, and 2I sequentially:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{+Stage 2G (evolvability, \emph{max\_jump} $\geq 6.0$):}
  99,998/100,000 random complete codes pass (Stage 2G is nearly vacuous for
  random codes since hyper-conservative codes are rare in random sampling).
  The 9 competitors from Phase~5 all pass Stage~2G. Competitors: 9.

\item \textbf{+Stage 2H (historical reachability $\geq 0.56$):}
  Reduces competitors from 9 to 1. The remaining code has historical
  reachability 0.571 (just above the 0.56 threshold) but is missing a
  stop codon.

\item \textbf{+Stage 2I (all 3 stop codons):}
  The remaining competitor uses only Stop1 and Stop3, missing Stop2~(UAG).
  Stage 2I eliminates it. \textbf{Competitors remaining: 0.}
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Computational uniqueness]
Among $10^5$ randomly generated wobble-admissible complete genetic codes,
no code outperforms the standard genetic code on all eight criteria
simultaneously (Stages~2A--2I). The standard code is the unique survivor
at this sampling depth.
\end{theorem}

The probability of observing 0 competitors by chance (if the standard code
were average) is less than $9 \times 10^{-5}$, conservatively. The
probability that the uniqueness result is an artifact of insufficient sampling
decreases as $p^{-5}$ per order-of-magnitude increase in sample size, where
$p = 0.009\%$ is the Phase-5 fraction.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Two types of ``better'' codes}

Our analysis reveals a qualitative distinction between two types of codes
that beat the standard on abstract criteria:

\textbf{Type I (hyper-conservative):} Codes with \emph{max\_jump} $\leq 5$
found by CP-SAT. These minimize abstract error and clustering scores but
prevent the full range of evolutionarily important single-nucleotide substitutions.
They represent codes that would be optimal for organisms evolving in
hyper-stable environments (e.g., extreme-radiation environments where any
mutation is lethal, so only the most conservative code survives). They are
appropriate for \emph{stasis}, not for the evolution of complex life.

\textbf{Type II (incomplete stop coverage):} The one code with 2 stops that
passes Stages 2G and 2H. This code is evolutionarily unstable: the missing
stop codon (UAG) can be captured by a suppressor tRNA and reassigned to an
amino acid, eventually leaving the organism with only one functional stop signal.

Both types reveal scientifically interesting alternatives --- the existence of
``alien code space'' populated by codes optimal for environments radically
different from early Earth. Synthetic biology could test whether organisms with
Type-I codes show greater robustness to mutation at the cost of reduced
evolvability.

\subsection{Arithmetic echoes from the GTE canonical orbit}
\label{sec:gte-echoes}

The Universal Generative Principle (UGP) addresses the Standard Model parameter spectrum, classifying each sector by epistemic status~\cite{Spivack2026_SM_UGP}, via a number-theoretic framework~\cite{SpivackUGP,SpivackUGPFormalization}.
At the canonical ridge level $n=10$, the Generative Triple Evolution (GTE)
produces an orbit of integer triples $(a,b,c)$ starting from
$G_1 = (1,\,73,\,823)$ via deterministic update rules (see Appendix~\ref{app:gte}).
Two arithmetic quantities in this orbit approximate fundamental counts in the
universal genetic code:

\paragraph{Echo 1 — Mersenne ratio $\approx$ 64 codons.}
The consecutive Mersenne-like c-values $c_2 = 2^{10}-1 = 1023$ and
$c_3 = 2^{16}-1 = 65535$ satisfy:
\[
c_3 / c_2 = 65535/1023 = 64 + 63/1023 \approx 64.06 \approx 64 = 4^3.
\]
The exact integer division $65535 = 64 \times 1023 + 63$ is
Lean~4-certified as \texttt{mersenne\_ratio\_approx} in module
\texttt{UgpLean.GTE.GeneralTheorems}~\cite{ugp-lean}.
The echo is noted in~\cite{SpivackUGP} as ``codon-count proximity.''

\paragraph{Echo 2 — Shared residue $= 20$ amino acids.}
Under the prime-lock condition at $n \geq 10$, the shared residue
$m_1 \equiv c_1 \pmod{b_1} = 823 \bmod 73 = 20$ is uniquely forced
by the interior divisor condition: any $b_1 > m_1$ gives $m_1 = 20$ exactly.
This is Lean-certified as \texttt{g1\_derived} in module
\texttt{UgpLean.GTE.UpdateMap}~\cite{ugp-lean}:
\texttt{gteRemainder 823 73 = 20}.
The shared residue $m_1 = 20$ equals the number of standard amino acids.

\paragraph{Echo 3 — $a_2 = 3^2$ and wobble classes $= 3^3$.}
The first derived triple value $a_2 = m_1 - (n+1) = 20 - 11 = 9 = 3^2$,
Lean-certified as \texttt{odd\_step\_a2}~\cite{ugp-lean}. The number of
wobble equivalence classes is $27 = 3^3$, with $3 = \sqrt{a_2}$ and also
$3$ = the codon length (triplet). The chain $a_2 = 3^2 \to 27 = 3^3$
is a structural pattern, not a derivation.

\medskip
\noindent
These are \emph{arithmetic echoes}, not derivations. The UGP does not predict
the genetic code from first principles. The $m_1=20$ echo merits special
notice: $m_1$ is the specific residue that makes $c_1 = b_1 q_1 + 20 = 823$
prime (the prime-lock condition). Among the five residues satisfying all
prime-lock constraints at $n=10$ (namely $\{18,20,24,26,36\}$), $m_1=20$
is uniquely selected by the RSUC-admissible orbit solution
$(b_2,q_2)=(42,24)$. Separately, the Shannon entropy of the polar-requirement
distribution of the 20 standard amino acids gives $\exp(H_{\rm PR}) \approx 19.0$,
indicating that the actual code near-maximally utilizes its chemical diversity
budget---a self-consistency condition consistent with $A=20$. Three biological
counts ($20$, $64$, $27$) from the same GTE orbit as the fine-structure-constant
echo $137 \approx 1/\alpha_{\rm EM}$~\cite{SpivackUGP,ugp-lean} constitute
a pattern; whether it reflects a common arithmetic self-consistency principle
operating at both the particle-physics and molecular-biology scales is an open
question.

\subsection{Connection to the admissibility-viability convergence pattern}

The structural pattern observed here mirrors a result established for the
Standard Model of particle physics~\cite{SpivackP24}. In that setting, the SM
parameters are the unique arithmetic intersection of a number-theoretic admissibility
sieve (GTE triples satisfying discrete constraints) and a physical viability
condition (coupling constants, masses, mixing angles matching observation). The
present result establishes the same pattern in biology:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Stage 1 (admissibility):} Wobble equivalence reduces $10^{85} \to
  10^{36}$, a $10^{50}$-fold reduction. The admissibility condition here is
  combinatorial (decodability under wobble rules) rather than number-theoretic,
  but structurally analogous: a large discrete space is collapsed by a consistency constraint.
\item \textbf{Stage 2 (viability):} Eight biological criteria applied jointly
  reduce the feasible set to (approximately) one: the standard code.
\end{enumerate}

The convergence of two independent domains --- particle physics and molecular
biology --- onto this admissibility-viability sieve structure is suggestive of a
general principle: \emph{wherever a large discrete space is filtered by the
intersection of a structural consistency condition and a multi-criterion viability
condition, the surviving set is small or unique}. This generalisation is explored
in the UGP Physics programme~\cite{SpivackUGP,SpivackResearchProgrammes} but the present paper's results are
independent of any specific UGP mechanisms and rest only on the biological criteria
described here.

\subsection{Limitations}

The uniqueness result is computational (sampling-based) rather than analytical.
A formal proof of uniqueness would require either: (a) an exhaustive enumeration
of all $5.8 \times 10^{36}$ admissible codes (computationally infeasible), or
(b) an algebraic argument that no complete wobble-admissible code passes all
eight criteria while scoring better than the standard. Path (b) would require
the Stage 2 criteria to be expressible as polynomial inequalities, allowing
application of the Tarski-Seidenberg theorem --- an open problem.

The biological justifications for Stages 2G, 2H, 2I are well-motivated but
not derived from first principles of prebiotic chemistry. However, a
supporting Gen/Drain analysis~\cite{codon_code} with published rate
constants~\cite{Rode1999,Orgel1992} finds that the minimum enhancement
factor for a self-sustaining prebiotic network equals exactly
$\mathrm{IPT} \approx 1.1309$: the transition from non-viable to viable
chemistry occurs at this specific threshold, consistent with the P15
Information Profit Principle~\cite{SpivackIPT}.
Rigorous MDL-based versions of the viability criteria from autocatalytic
network theory (with experimentally determined rate constants) would
complete the formal uniqueness proof and are an open problem.

\section{Conclusion}

We have shown that the standard genetic code is the unique survivor of a
two-stage sieve applied to the full space of wobble-admissible codon tables,
under eight simultaneous viability criteria. The five classical criteria
(error minimization, prebiotic accessibility, stop robustness, chemical
clustering, polar-requirement conservation) place the standard code at
$z = +3.22\sigma$ (top 0.009\%). Three additional criteria (evolvability,
historical reachability, complete stop coverage) eliminate all remaining
competitors in a sample of $10^5$ complete codes.

The CP-SAT global search reveals that codes mathematically outperforming the
standard code all have hyper-conservative mutation profiles (\emph{max\_jump}
$\leq 5$), making them incompatible with the evolution of complex enzymatic
diversity. This separates \emph{evolved optimality} (the standard code's
position as the locally-reachable, evolvability-preserving maximum) from
\emph{abstract optimality} (the mathematical maximum of simplified criteria).

This result extends to biology a pattern established for the Standard
Model~\cite{SpivackP24}: when a large discrete space is filtered by the
intersection of a structural admissibility condition and multi-criterion
viability, the survivor set is generically small or unique. Whether this
reflects a general mathematical principle or a domain-specific coincidence
is an open question.

The General Selection paper~\cite{SpivackGeneralSelection} generalises the
two-stage sieve framework used here to eight domains spanning biology, physics,
and economics, and situates the genetic-code result within a broader comparative

\section{Robustness and Validation}

\paragraph{Null test.}
We verified that the z-score ordering matches across five independent random seeds
($z = +3.18\sigma$ to $+3.26\sigma$), confirming the result is not seed-dependent.
Phase~6 null test: all 9 competitors in Phase~5 were manually examined; their
\emph{max\_jump} values were in the range $[6.5, 8.2]$ (all pass Stage~2G), and
each lacked at least one of: historical reachability $\geq 0.56$ or complete stop
coverage. No competitor passed all three novel criteria.

\paragraph{Variant code consistency check.}
All 17 known variant genetic codes (vertebrate mitochondrial, invertebrate
mitochondrial, yeast mitochondrial, mold/protozoan, ciliate, Mycoplasma,
Spiroplasma, flatworm, etc.) were tested. Every variant scores \emph{below}
the standard code on the joint Phase-5 metric. Each variant corresponds to a
reassignment of one or two codons; in every case this reassignment is a
downhill move on the viability metric, consistent with the variant codes being
secondary local minima.

\paragraph{CP-SAT global bound.}
OR-Tools CP-SAT~\cite{Perron2023} was run on the 4-criterion and 5-criterion
objectives with a 120-second time limit. In both cases, the solver found codes
scoring better than the standard code only when \emph{max\_jump} $\leq 5$, and
the solver status was FEASIBLE (not OPTIMAL). The true global optimum may differ;
these are lower bounds on the maximum achievable score among all complete codes.
Regardless, all reported CP-SAT codes have \emph{max\_jump} $< 6.0$ and are
therefore eliminated by Stage~2G.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to Stage 2G threshold.}
We scanned $\text{max\_jump}_{\min} \in [5.5, 7.0]$. For any threshold $\leq 6.8$,
the standard code passes; CP-SAT competitor codes (max\_jump $\approx 4$--$5$)
are eliminated for any threshold $\geq 5.1$. The result is robust to the precise
choice of threshold in this range.

\section{The UGP/GTE Framework: A Primer for Biologists}
\label{app:gte}

This appendix gives minimal background for readers unfamiliar with the
Universal Generative Principle (UGP) and Generative Triple Evolution (GTE),
referenced in Section~\ref{sec:gte-echoes}. The main results of this paper
are independent of UGP; the echoes in \S\ref{sec:gte-echoes} are
observations, not derivations. Readers focused on the biological results may
skip this appendix.

\paragraph{UGP in one sentence.}
The Universal Generative Principle~\cite{SpivackUGP} is a deterministic,
number-theoretic framework proposing that the Standard Model parameter
spectrum (fermion masses, gauge coupling constants, mixing angles) can be
derived from three axioms: (A1)~\emph{Locality}, (A2)~\emph{Symmetry}, and
(A3)~\emph{Compression/MDL} (rules that minimize total description
length)~\cite{SpivackUGP}. It is complementary to QFT: it fixes the numerical
constants that a QFT Lagrangian takes as input; QFT supplies the scattering
dynamics. The structural core is machine-checked in Lean~4 (see companion formalization paper~\cite{SpivackUGPFormalization},
zero \texttt{sorry})~\cite{ugp-lean,SpivackUGPFormalization}.

\paragraph{What is GTE?}
Generative Triple Evolution is the dynamical mechanism realizing the axioms.
GTE operates on integer vectors $(a,b,c;\,g)\in\mathbb{Z}^3\times\{1,2,3\}$:
$a$ encodes parity/phase; $b$ is the ladder index (particle's complexity
position and information content $N_{\rm eff}=|b|$); $c$ is the branch
capacity; $g\in\{1,2,3\}$ is the Standard Model generation~\cite{SpivackUGP}.
Two deterministic operators (odd step $g{:}1\to2$; even step $g{:}2\to3$)
evolve triples with no adjustable parameters.

\paragraph{The prime-lock ridge at $n=10$.}
Initial conditions are not free but are uniquely selected by:
(i)~the ridge $R_n = 2^n-16$; at $n{=}10$, $R_{10}=1008$;
(ii)~mirror duality: a divisor pair $(b_2,q_2)$ and its swap must
both be admissible; and
(iii)~the prime-lock: $c_1 = b_1 q_1 + 20$ must be prime, where the
residue $m_1 = 20$ is uniquely forced by the interior-divisor condition
($b_1>20$ at $n\geq10$) --- it is not a free parameter.
A systematic search over interior divisors of $R_{10}=1008$ finds the
unique solution $(b_2,q_2)=(42,24)$, fixing $b_1=73$ and the canonical
first triple $G_1=(1,73,823;\,1)$ (RSUC:\ Residual Seed Uniqueness
Condition, Lean-certified~\cite{ugp-lean,SpivackP24}).

The orbit at $n{=}10$:
\begin{align*}
G_1=(1,73,823;\,1) &\xrightarrow{\text{odd}} G_2=(9,42,1023;\,2) \\
                   &\xrightarrow{\text{even}} G_3=(5,275,65535;\,3)\to\cdots
\end{align*}
This orbit also gives the fine-structure constant echo
$2b_1-a_2=2\times73-9=137\approx1/\alpha_{\rm EM}$.

\paragraph{Biological echoes (derived quantities).}
From $G_1=(1,73,823)$, Lean-certified in \texttt{UgpLean.GTE}~\cite{ugp-lean}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0pt]
\item $q_1=\lfloor823/73\rfloor=11$; $m_1=823\bmod73=\mathbf{20}$
  (\texttt{g1\_derived}; \textbf{equals 20 amino acids})
\item Odd step: $a_2=m_1-(n+1)=9$, $b_2=73-31=42$,
  $c_2=2^{10}-1=1023$ (\texttt{odd\_step\_produces\_g2})
\item Even step: $c_3=2^{16}-1=65535$, $c_3/c_2\approx\mathbf{64}$
  (\texttt{mersenne\_ratio\_approx}; \textbf{equals 64 codons})
\item $a_2=9=3^2$; wobble classes $=27=3^3=3\times a_2$
\end{itemize}
